What works

The scripture itself is the proof — a reader lands, hears a verse in Sanskrit, and understands what the product is in ten seconds. Signals real depth to search users who arrived for a specific verse and would bounce on marketing copy.

What risks

Loses the emotional entry for someone whose reason to read the Gita is anxiety, not scholarship. Cold-start users who don't know 2.47 from 6.35 may not know why they should care about this particular verse.

Pick this if

SEO traffic dominates and drop-off is highest on the emotional-copy fold. Ship this if Mixpanel shows "Today's verse" is the most-clicked section already — this variant just promotes it.

B

Path picker — the choice IS the hero

Optimizes for first-click depth: every visitor lands on a verse that matches their disposition (Karma / Jnana / Bhakti), not a generic 1.1. Tradeoff demotes the SEO H1 and asks a cold visitor to self-identify before reading.

What works

Turns the hero into the highest-leverage decision on the site. Personalization on cold traffic without a login. The three-paths framing is authentically Gita, not a growth-hack quiz overlaid on scripture.

What risks

SEO cost: the H1 "Which path is yours?" doesn't rank for "bhagavad gita". Need to keep the schema-rich content lower or bury a hidden H2. Cold users may pick wrong and land on a verse that feels random.

Pick this if

The metric you care about is not landings but "verses per session" and return visits. Path Quiz already exists on the current page mid-scroll — this variant tests whether promoting it to the hero lifts engagement.

C

Ask the Gita — search-led hero

Optimizes for modern seekers who arrive with a question ("why can't I let go?") rather than a chapter number. Positions Wisdom as an oracle you consult, not a book you read. Tradeoff sets an expectation that requires the search backend to be genuinely good; a bad match burns trust harder than a bad landing page.

What works

Turns the whole product story into one input field. Native to how people search now (ChatGPT-shaped). Positions Wisdom as the Gita's answering surface — an angle no other scripture site owns.

What risks

The backend has to actually match well. First bad answer breaks the promise. Also invites theology arguments ("that's not what 6.35 means") — needs a human-curated seed of the top ~50 questions to bootstrap trust.

Pick this if

Scripture-AI is the direction anyway (the current page already has ScriptureAI waitlist CTAs). Ship this as a way to validate demand before building the full product — even the input alone captures intent + waitlist emails.

D

Editorial minimalist — hushed and confident

Optimizes for taste, gravitas, shareability. The kind of page a Kindle-highlighter screenshots and sends to a friend. Tradeoff gives up nearly all conversion machinery — no emoji, no proof bar, no path quiz above the fold — trusting the copy and the type to do the work.

What works

Uniquely restrained in a category (spiritual sites) that is usually loud, gradient-heavy and overproduced. The confidence itself is a positioning statement: "we didn't need to convince you." Highly linkable, highly screenshottable.

What risks

Leaves the anxious first-time reader without a lifeline — they may not click "Begin at 1.1" cold. Loses the built-in cross-links (path quiz, themes, iconic verses) that the current page uses to catch users who don't know what they want.

Pick this if

The bet is on trust and word-of-mouth over per-visit conversion. Also the right hero if you want the site to grow through essays and screenshots on X rather than through search.

How to pick a direction

Pick on the tradeoff, not the visual. Any of these will look good in the brand.

If you want →To reward SEO traffic that arrived to READ, promote depth over pitch → pick A (Verse-first)
If you want →The first click to feel personal, and to grow "verses per session" → pick B (Path picker)
If you want →To validate the Scripture-AI thesis on cold traffic with the input as the funnel → pick C (Ask the Gita)
If you want →A page that reads as taste and trust, growing through essays and screenshots → pick D (Editorial)
