## The Ashtavakra Gita and other Gitas

The Ashtavakra Gita is a Sanskrit dialogue between the sage Ashtavakra and King Janaka of Mithila, 20 chapters and 298 verses, a standalone Advaita text that is not part of the Mahabharata. Wisdom publishes every verse with Devanagari, IAST and its own plain English; 40 key verses also have word meanings, a short commentary and a link to the Bhagavad Gita verse that carries the same idea. A separate guide lists about twenty other texts called Gita, with each one's source, speakers and an honest note where a text is a later anthology or a modern publication.

- Ashtavakra Gita guide: https://wisdomquotes.in/ashtavakra-gita. What it is, the 20 chapters, where to start, dating range and FAQ.
- Chapter pages: https://wisdomquotes.in/ashtavakra-gita/chapter/1 (to /chapter/20). Every verse of the chapter in Sanskrit and English.
- Verse pages: https://wisdomquotes.in/ashtavakra-gita/chapter/1/verse/11. One verse with Sanskrit, IAST, English, word meanings and commentary (1.1 to 1.20, 2.1, 9.6, 15.4, 15.7, 15.11, 17.3, 18.1, 18.35, 18.59, 18.100 and others).
- Famous lines: https://wisdomquotes.in/ashtavakra-gita/quotes. Twenty lines as shareable cards, starting with 1.11, "as you hold yourself, so you become".
- Compared with the Bhagavad Gita: https://wisdomquotes.in/ashtavakra-gita-vs-bhagavad-gita. Setting, speakers, length, path and who should start where, with the verses the two texts share.
- Seven mornings: https://wisdomquotes.in/ashtavakra-gita/seven-mornings. A seven-day plan, one verse and one small practice each morning.
- Other Gitas: https://wisdomquotes.in/gitas. Avadhuta, Uddhava, Anu, Vyadha, Hamsa, Guru, Ribhu, Devi, Ganesha, Shiva, Ishvara, Rama, Pandava and more, with sources and speakers.
