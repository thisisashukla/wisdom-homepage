The idea in one line

A ritual you can feel in the hand, ending in one line of the Manas and the scene it came from.

Every rival page is the same: a 15×15 table, a click, and a chaupai with a one-line verdict. Ours keeps every step of the tradition: remembering Ram, asking once, eyes closed, the shalaka (needle), counting every ninth square. Each step becomes something you do with your finger, and the answer comes with the story behind the line, so it carries meaning on top of the yes or no.

1

Sankalp. Hold a question. Typing is optional and stays on the phone. Three diyas at the top are today's three questions, the traditional limit.

2

Dhyan. Press and hold. The diya rises and श्री राम जय राम जय जय राम lights word by word. Let go early and the flame dips.

3

Shalaka. The grid sits under a cloth written with Ram naam. Rest your finger anywhere on it and hold: that is the digital "eyes closed".

4

The count. The cloth lifts. A gold thread hops every ninth square, wrapping around, and each syllable drops into its slot until the chaupai assembles itself. This is exactly how Tulsidas's grid works, made visible.

5

Uttar. The chaupai, the traditional sign, the meaning, the scene it comes from, and what it asks of you today. Then a share card, a 7-day reading plan from this line, and the mala of nine.

The prototype is fully working: the grid is the traditional layout, and the answer you get comes from the cell you choose by the ninth-square rule. Hindi is the default; EN shows the English twin. Sound is a soft synthesized bell per hop. Diyas and the mala are saved in this browser only.

The gap we take

No ritual pacing, so it feels like a lottery click. No scene: who said the line, to whom, in which moment. Negative answers land as bare verdicts. No reason to come back and nothing worth sharing. Nothing leads to reading the Manas itself.

We keep the tradition exactly as it is and give it presence, context and a next step.

The spin

Five design moves

Each move turns one element of the tradition into something the person does. Engagement comes from the ritual itself, so there are no streaks or points.

१

The cloth of Ram naam

The grid sits under a red cloth woven with "राम" written over and over, like a Ram naam lekhan sheet. You rest your finger on the cloth and hold for about a second while a gold ring closes. The cloth then lifts like a curtain at an aarti.

Keeps it sacredChoosing blind is the traditional instruction (eyes closed, finger on the page). The cloth makes it impossible to "pick a nice letter".
Makes it engagingThe hold gives weight to the choice, and the lift is a reveal. "Lift the cloth" is there for anyone who wants to choose with open eyes.
२

The count made visible

From your cell, a gold thread hops every ninth square, wrapping around the grid, about 170 ms per hop. Each syllable drops into one of 25 slots in the line's own order, so the chaupai assembles out of order, starting wherever your finger landed. A soft bell sounds per hop and the phone gives a tiny vibration on Android.

Keeps it sacredIt shows exactly how the prashnavali works: Tulsidas's 225 syllables, counted by nines. Nothing is random or hidden, and devotees can check it.
Makes it engagingFour seconds of watching your answer spell itself out is the moment people screen-record and share.
३

Three diyas a day

The tradition says at most three questions a day and never the same question twice. Three diyas at the top are today's questions; each one goes out when used. The day resets at 4 AM (Brahma muhurta). A repeated question gets "यह प्रश्न आज पूछा जा चुका है" and the earlier chaupai.

Keeps it sacredThe rule comes from the tradition and is shown as such. It stops people re-rolling until they get a "yes".
Makes it engagingA limit makes each question count, and the diyas coming back at dawn give people a reason to return.
४

The scene behind your line

Every answer has two tabs. "आज आपके लिए" says what the line asks of you today. "प्रसंग" tells the story: Lankini sending Hanuman into Lanka, Shiva letting Sati go, Mandodari over Ravana. The traditional sign stays visible as a chip: shubh, sanshay, or "अभी नहीं".

Keeps it sacredWe never hide or soften the traditional reading. We add what the episode teaches when you read its whole arc.
Makes it engagingA "no" turns into guidance ("somewhere in this there is pretense, set it right first"), which is what makes people trust the tool and come back.
५

The mala of nine

Nine beads under the answer, one per chaupai. The beads you've received stay lit, and each opens its own page. Over weeks, a person learns nine chaupais of the Manas by heart without trying. Saved on the device; a mala synced to the Wisdom account comes later.

Keeps it sacredThe mala is a devotional object, so collecting feels like japa. There are no points or badges.
Makes it engagingAn empty bead invites the next visit, and the full mala is a natural moment for "read the Manas with us: 7 days".

Motion and sound

  • Dhyan hold: 3.5 s. The flame scales from 0.55 to 1 and the halo grows; the seven words light in step. Letting go early dips the flame back.
  • Shalaka hold: 0.9 s ring fill at the finger, then the cloth lifts in 0.9 s.
  • Count: 25 hops at 170 ms (about 4.3 s), a bell at 1,320 Hz per hop, a two-note bell at the end. Vibration of 6 ms per hop where supported.
  • Reduced motion: no flicker or lift; all slots fill in one step; sound stays off by default.
  • Production audio: replace the synth with a recorded manjira tick and a temple bell, plus an optional tanpura drone. Recite the chaupai in Manas-path style (recorded, or the Wisdom TTS voice).
Rules and edge cases

Respectful by construction

Product rules

  • Three per day, reset at 4 AM local. Shown as diyas; the fourth attempt opens "आज के तीन प्रश्न पूरे हुए".
  • No repeat questions. Typed questions are normalised and hashed on the device. A repeat shows the earlier answer. Untyped questions are trusted.
  • Private. The question never leaves the phone. It goes to /start only if the person taps "7-day plan" and agrees, using the same consent sheet as the app.
  • No ads, no astrology upsell, no deity images. This is a big part of how we stand apart from every rival.
  • The traditional sign is never hidden. Negative signs use calm colours (muted sindoor, never alarm red) and always come with "what it asks of you".

Edge cases

  • Health, legal, money questions: if typed text matches a small keyword list (doctor, surgery, case, loan…), show one line under the answer: "शलाका मन को दिशा देती है। स्वास्थ्य, क़ानून या पैसे के निर्णय में विशेषज्ञ की सलाह भी लें।"
  • Lifted finger mid-hold: the ring resets and nothing is chosen.
  • Tap by keyboard or screen reader: the cloth exposes a "choose a square" button. When lifted, cells are focusable buttons with aria-labels, and Enter chooses.
  • No storage (private mode): the tool still works, diyas reset on reload, and the mala doesn't persist.
  • Low-end Android: no canvas, no images, CSS only. The grid is server-rendered HTML; JS under 25 KB gzipped.
Growth loops

From one question to a reader

Share card

A 4:5 and 9:16 card with the chaupai, the scene in one line and "श्रीराम शलाका · wisdomquotes.in". The question is never printed. WhatsApp first, then Save image. Generated server-side with @vercel/og so it works on any phone.

Nine line pages

Each chaupai gets its own page (/hi/ram-shalaka/prabisi-nagar-kije-sab-kaja), with the meaning, the scene, the other 3 lines of the full chaupai unit and audio. These capture "<line> meaning" searches and are where the mala beads lead.

Into Wisdom

"इस चौपाई से 7 दिन का पाठ" opens /start seeded with this line's theme (and the question, if consented). Android: the WhatsApp channel "रोज़ एक चौपाई". iPhone: "इस चौपाई को लॉक स्क्रीन पर रखें" with an App Store deep link. A full mala prompts a 7-day Manas plan.

Be realistic: this audience is Hindi, Android-heavy and older North Indian. Expect it to grow reach and the WhatsApp and email list. App revenue from here will be small.

Build spec

Next.js, one data file, one client component

Structure

  • content/ram-shalaka.json: the 225-cell grid, the nine line records, and the copy for hi and en.
  • app/hi/ram-shalaka/page.tsx: a server component that renders the grid table, the nine answers, how-to and FAQ.
  • components/shalaka/Ritual.tsx: a client component with the state machine sankalp → dhyan → shalaka → ganana → uttar → done.
  • lib/shalaka.ts: sequence(p) returns 25 cell indices; class k = p mod 9, start position m = (p − k) / 9, positions m..24 then 0..m−1.
  • app/api/og/shalaka/[k]/route.tsx: the share card image.
  • Storage key wq_shalaka_v1: {day, used, asked:[{hash,k}], mala:[k]}, inside try/catch.

Events (Mixpanel, same style as /start)

  • Shalaka Opened {lang, device, referrer}
  • Sankalp Set {typed: bool}
  • Diya Lit {held_ms}, Dhyan Abandoned
  • Shalaka Placed {cell, veil: bool}
  • Chaupai Revealed {k, sign, nth_today}
  • Prasang Opened, Listen Played
  • Shalaka Shared {channel}, Plan From Chaupai {k}, WhatsApp Joined
  • Daily Limit Reached, Repeat Question Blocked
