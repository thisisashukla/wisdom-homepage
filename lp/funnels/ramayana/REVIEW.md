## 2. References: what to confirm against the Gita Press edition

- **Valmiki** references are given to the sarga only ("Aranya Kanda, sarga 45"). Sarga numbers were checked against the Gita Press Hindi sarga titles in `vr_sargas.csv`. Shloka numbers are not shown because the corpus groups Dutt's paragraphs (often 2 to 4 shlokas per unit), so `vr.3.45.25` is a Wisdom line id, not a shloka number. Each line shows its line id.
- **Manas** doha numbers are computed by `gita_press_groups()` in `scripts/funnels/ramayana/corpus.py`: a new Gita Press group starts at the first chaupai or chhand after a doha or sortha; consecutive closers share a number as (क), (ख). Checks: Aranya total = 46 and Shabari = 34 (match), Kishkindha total = 30 (match), Ayodhya Kevat = 100/101 (bhaktibharat pad 100 + memory), Uttar par hit = 41 (RESEARCH.md). Bal, Sundar and Lanka drift by 1 to 4 (totals 358/59/117 vs 361/60/121), and Ayodhya drifts after 101 (323 vs 326). **For those, the pages show the kand name only.** Computed guesses are in the table below (Bal looks like guess + 1: Ahalya doha 210, bow 261).
- To add a number later: confirm it, then extend `gp_verified()` or add an override, and rebuild.

## 4. Claims in the editorial copy to verify

- Comparison page: "Most scholars place its composition in the last centuries BCE"; "about 24,000 shlokas by the traditional count"; "many scholars read the Uttara Kanda as a later layer"; samvat 1631 = 1574 CE (rcm.1.32.chaupai.2, "संबत सोरह सै एकतीसा"); Gita Press doha totals per kand (IITK Ramcharitmanas site); the four teller frames of the Manas.
- Lakshman Rekha arc: "The drawn line, and Sita stepping over it to give alms, come from later retellings and stage versions." No specific later source is named on purpose.
- Shabari: "The tasted berries come from later devotional retellings." No source named.
- Kaikeyi arc: Kaikeyi's remorse at Chitrakoot (rcm.2.262.chaupai.1, "गरइ गलानि कुटिल कैकेई"); Valmiki 2.7 Kaikeyi rewards Manthara.
- Sita into the fire: "He closes the loop in Lanka Kand, where the fire gives the real Sita back" (rcm.6.109 chhand per RESEARCH.md).
- Diwali: "Neither text describes rows of clay diyas lit for the return." Valmiki 6.127 (flowers, puffed rice, flags); Manas Uttar 9 (aarti) and Uttar 27 (gem lamps, describing Ayodhya in Ram's reign, not the night of return).
- Ahalya arc: Indra comes in Gautama's guise (vr.1.48.20). I did not quote the verse where Valmiki says Ahalya knew; the arc says only that Gautama cursed both.
- Speakers are set by hand. The corpus `speaker` field is unreliable (vr.3.45.18 and vr.3.45.24 are labelled Ravana; rcm.3.31.chaupai.2 is labelled Sita).
