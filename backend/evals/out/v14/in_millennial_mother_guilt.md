## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 6.34
LINE: The mind is restless, stormy, stubborn and strong. Holding it feels like holding the wind.
TRANSLATION: For the mind is restless, Krishna, turbulent, powerful and stubborn. I think it is as hard to hold as the wind.
MEANING: Arjuna, standing on the field of battle, tells Krishna that the mind is restless, turbulent and stubborn. He says it is as hard to hold as the wind. Krishna hears this and agrees. He tells Arjuna that the mind is difficult to master, but through practice and letting go, it can be trained. The mind is not your enemy. It is a force that needs steady handling.
FOR YOU: Your daughter asked why you are not at her school events. The guilt that followed you in the car is the mind being stormy. It is not a verdict on your mothering. It is just a thought that needs practice to hold steady.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Bhartrihari · Vairagya Shatakam
LINE: Why exhaust yourself daily winning others' approval? Settle inside, and your own clarity grants every wish.
TRANSLATION: O heart, why do you enter into weariness, day after day trying to please the minds of others in so many ways, to win their favour? When you are at peace within, your own clear resolve rises by itself like a cluster of wish-granting gems: what wish of yours would it not fulfil?
MEANING: Bhartrihari speaks to his own heart at the start of his Advice to the Mind. He asks why it wears itself out every day, trying in so many ways to win the favor of others. He says that when you are at peace inside, your own clear resolve rises by itself, like a cluster of wish-granting gems. Then he asks what wish of yours that inner clarity would not fulfill.
FOR YOU: You do not have to earn your daughter's love by being at every school event. Your own clear presence, even in a single shared moment, is enough to grant her what she truly needs from you.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: Let them praise or blame, let money come or go: the steady don't step off the right path.
TRANSLATION: Let the worldly-wise blame or praise; let fortune come or go as she pleases; let death come today or ages hence: the steadfast do not move a step from the path of what is right.
MEANING: Bhartrihari speaks to the reader from a place of quiet resolve. He tells them to let the world offer its blame or praise, to let money arrive or disappear, and to let death come today or far in the future. The steadfast person does not move a step from the path of what is right. He is saying that setbacks rarely last for those who keep going on the right path. The steady do not step off the right path.
FOR YOU: The praise and blame you carry from the office to the car do not move you off your path. Your duty is to act with care, and your worth is not measured by your daughter's question or the other mothers' presence. You can hold this steady place while it hurts.

## Day 4 [steady] Mahabharata · Udyoga Parva · Vidura Niti
LINE: Don't swell with praise or burn at a slight; stay deep and still like a river pool.
TRANSLATION: One who is not elated when honoured, nor pained when slighted, and who stays unruffled like a deep pool of the Ganga, is called wise.
FOR YOU: The question your daughter asks in the car is a current, but you are the deep pool beneath it. Your worth as a mother is not the verdict of one moment. Hold your care steady, and let the noise pass over you.

## Day 5 [act] Aitareya Upanishad 2.1.3
LINE: Caring for your child, you care for yourself. Through them you are born a second time.
TRANSLATION: When he cares for the child from birth onward, he is caring for himself, for the continuation of these worlds; that is how they continue. That is his second birth.
MEANING: The Seer is speaking to the reader about the second birth, the raising of a child. He says that when a person cares for the child from birth onward, they are caring for themselves, because through the child the worlds continue. That is how they continue. That is the second birth.
FOR YOU: When you are at work, you are not away from your daughter. You are caring for the world she will enter. The guilt in your car is not proof of failure. It is the weight of a second birth. Let it carry you, not crush you.

## Day 6 [act] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: Cut craving, forgive, drop arrogance, speak truth, win over even enemies, be kind to those suffering.
TRANSLATION: Cut off craving, practise forbearance, give up arrogance, take no delight in wrong, speak the truth, follow the path of the good, serve the wise, honour those worthy of honour, win over even your enemies, show courtesy, guard your good name, be kind to those in pain: this is how the good behave.
MEANING: Bhartrihari speaks to the reader from a place of reflection. He tells them that true success is not measured by how high you stand, but by how low you bow when you reach the top. Real ornaments are not gold or titles, but the acts of listening, giving, and helping. He says the good behave by cutting craving, forgiving, dropping arrogance, and speaking truth. They serve the wise, honour the worthy, and win over even enemies. Yet he warns that winning over an enemy is not the same as tolerating harm.
FOR YOU: After a long day, sit with your daughter and speak one honest word about how much you care. Then, for one small task, give it your full attention without checking your phone. Let your presence be the gift, not a grand gesture.

## Day 7 [story] Kausalya and the child who ran from dinner
LINE: Kausalya was shown the whole universe in her newborn, and all she wanted was for him to cry like a baby.
FOR YOU: Your daughter did not ask if you were the cosmic form. She asked if you would come to the school event. Your daughter's love was not a prize you had to win by being everywhere at once. You can be whole, and still be her mother.
STORY: At his birth Ram shows Kausalya his cosmic form, and she asks him instead to be simply a baby. Later scenes are pure childhood: the boy runs off mid-meal, curd on his face, dusty from play, while his father laughs and his mother chases him. When she once glimpses the whole universe in him again, she asks only to be spared that and keep her child.
LESSON: The grandest thing about a child may be the ordinary hours of childhood itself.
