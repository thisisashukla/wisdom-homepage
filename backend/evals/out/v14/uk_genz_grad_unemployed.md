## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 18.47
LINE: Better your own path, imperfectly walked, than someone else's done perfectly.
TRANSLATION: Better is one's own duty, though imperfect, than the duty of another well performed. Doing the work born of one's own nature, one incurs no wrong.
MEANING: Krishna tells Arjuna that it is better to do your own duty, even with flaws, than to do another's duty perfectly. He means that when you act from your own nature, you cannot do wrong. This is not about staying in a role you were born into. It is about finding work that fits you, and doing it as an offering, knowing that every path has its cracks.
FOR YOU: You are measuring your own path against the polished screens of your friends. Let your work be your own, even if it is making flat whites today. Do it as an offering, and know that a path walked imperfectly is still your path.

LINE: Envy is an itch. Burning at another's happiness is a wasting disease of the mind.
TRANSLATION: Possessiveness is ringworm and envy is the itch; the swings of elation and dejection are throat ailments. The burning you feel at another's happiness is consumption, and malice and a crooked mind are leprosy.
MEANING: Kakbhushundi tells Garuda that the mind has its own sicknesses. Possessiveness is ringworm and envy is the itch. The swings between elation and dejection are throat ailments. The burning you feel at another's happiness is consumption, and malice and a crooked mind are leprosy. He is naming the illness that lives inside us, so we can know it for what it is instead of letting it rule us.
FOR YOU: You are making coffee and seeing your friends at firms you respect. That comparison is the itch Kakbhushundi names. The applications are not the disease. The burning at their success is. Notice the itch without scratching it today.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhagavad Gita 4.22
LINE: Content with what comes, beyond envy, steady in success and failure, you stay free even while working.
TRANSLATION: Content with whatever comes unasked, beyond the pairs of opposites, free from envy, the same in success and failure, even when acting he is not bound.
MEANING: Krishna is telling Arjuna how the wise move through the world. They are not driven by wanting more or envying someone else's corner office. They accept what lands in their lap, they stay steady when they win or lose, and they keep working. This steadiness is not laziness. It is a deep freedom. They are not tied to the outcome because their peace is not borrowed from anyone else's success.
FOR YOU: You are making flat whites while your friends are in glass towers. That is your path right now. It is not perfect. It is not behind. It is simply the one you are walking, and that is enough to carry you through today.

LINE: The ocean is not stirred by a small fish's flick. Petty things needn't shake you.

LINE: Do tomorrow's work today, and the afternoon's in the morning. Time doesn't wait for you to be ready.
TRANSLATION: Do tomorrow's work today, and the afternoon's work in the morning. Death does not wait to see whether your work is done or not.
MEANING: A young man named Medhavin speaks to his father. He tells him not to delay what matters. He says that days and nights fall like arrows.
