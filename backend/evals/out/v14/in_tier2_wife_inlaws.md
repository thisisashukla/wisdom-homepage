## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 18.47
LINE: Better your own path, imperfectly walked, than someone else's done perfectly.
TRANSLATION: Better is one's own duty, though imperfect, than the duty of another well performed. Doing the work born of one's own nature, one incurs no wrong.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna on a battlefield, explaining that true perfection lies in doing the work that comes naturally to you, even if it is flawed, rather than perfectly performing a role that is not yours. He tells Arjuna that whatever one is born to do, however imperfectly, is better than another's duty done perfectly. The work born of your own nature carries no fault, because the focus is on the offering, not on flawless results.
FOR YOU: You built this practice from nothing. That is your own work. The mother-in-law's words try to place you in a role that is not yours. You do not need to perform it perfectly or let it define you. Your own path, however imperfectly walked, is the only one that fits you.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Ashtavakra Gita 1.11
LINE: Believe you are free and you are. Believe you are bound and you are. As you think, so you become.
TRANSLATION: One who thinks 'I am free' is free, and one who thinks 'I am bound' stays bound. The saying here is true: as you think, so you become.
MEANING: King Janaka, who rules Mithila, asks the young sage Ashtavakra how a person finds freedom. Ashtavakra answers that you are not your body or your roles, but the awareness that watches them. He says you are more than your roles and worries. Freedom is available right now. The verse tells us that what you believe about yourself becomes your reality. If you believe you are bound by your family or your work, you will feel bound. If you believe you are free, you begin to live as free.
FOR YOU: Your in-laws are trying to shrink your picture of yourself, but the reality is already there. When you think of your practice and your worth, you are not just imagining it. You are seeing what is already true.

## Day 5 [act] Bhagavad Gita 17.15
LINE: Speak words that don't wound, that are true, kind and helpful. That is discipline of speech.
TRANSLATION: Speech that causes no distress, that is truthful, pleasant and beneficial, and the regular study of sacred texts: this is called austerity of speech.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna, laying out what real effort looks like. He says that true austerity of speech is not silence or empty words. It is speaking in a way that causes no distress, that is truthful, pleasant and beneficial. This is not about being soft or hiding the truth. It is about saying what is real without cruelty, offering words that do not wound the one who hears them. It is the discipline of using your voice to build up rather than tear down.
FOR YOU: When you tell your partner the truth about your practice and your worth, speak it without cruelty. Let your words be true, kind and helpful. Let them land as a steady fact, not an attack.

TRANSLATION: Always doing the work that comes, seeing friend and foe alike, free of wanting and not-wanting, such a one neither grieves nor craves. Saying what is agreeable to all, speaking gently when asked, knowing the intent of others' hearts, such a one does not sink in the world.
MEANING: Ribhu is teaching Nidagha how a free person lives among others without losing themselves. Ribhu adds that this person speaks what is agreeable to all, speaks gently when asked, and knows the intent of others' hearts, so they do not sink in the world. Ribhu is showing that you can live fully in the world while staying unhurried inside.
