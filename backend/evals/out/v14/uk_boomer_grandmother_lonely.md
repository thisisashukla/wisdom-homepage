## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 10.9
LINE: Their minds on me, they talk of me with each other, and find joy and contentment in it.
TRANSLATION: With minds fixed on me and lives given to me, enlightening one another and always speaking of me, they are content and delighted.
MEANING: Krishna tells Arjuna that those who fix their minds on the divine and give their lives to it find a particular kind of joy. They enlighten one another and speak of this presence with each other. In doing so, they become content and delighted. It is a picture of people finding light not in isolation, but in shared attention, where their talk turns into a source of deep contentment.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Mahabharata · Shanti Parva · Shanti: Consolation of Yudhishthira
LINE: In grief, ask: where did I come from, where am I going, who am I? Let that steady you.
TRANSLATION: 'Where was I before? Where will I go? Who am I, really, and why am I here? Why should I grieve, and for what?' Ask these, and steady the mind.
MEANING: The sage Ashma answers King Janaka, who has lost his family and wealth. Ashma says that pain comes from what we did not want and what we lost, that meetings end, and that days do not return. He tells Janaka to steady his mind by asking three questions: where did I come from, where am I going, and who am I, really? By turning inward to these questions, you find a footing that the loss of people and things cannot shake.
FOR YOU: Your Sunday mornings with the Ramayan are a quiet space where the same questions can steady you. You do not need to find a new story. You could sit with the self that reads it and ask where you came from and who you are, without the noise of the house around you.
