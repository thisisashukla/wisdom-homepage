LINE: The mind is restless, stormy, stubborn and strong. Holding it feels like holding the wind.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Yoga Vasishtha
LINE: 'Done this, doing this, must do that.' Caught in endless lists, the mind never gets a moment's rest.
TRANSLATION: Carried along on endless great waves of tasks, the mind never gets a moment's rest; its depths dance with worry. 'This I've done, this I'm doing, this I'll do': so the bird of the mind, tangled in a net of reckonings, faints.
MEANING: Years ago, two childhood friends, Bhasa and Vilasa, grew up together in a forest hermitage. They later drifted apart and spent decades in hard austerities. When they meet again as old men, Bhasa asks if Vilasa has found peace. Vilasa admits he has not, because the mind keeps spinning through endless tasks and worries. This verse shows that a mind caught in the loop of past actions and future duties, never pausing to ask what it itself needs, becomes its own enemy.
FOR YOU: The mind has become its own enemy by being fed every demand and never asked what it needs. Today, the lists of work and family are not the enemy, but the fact that your mind is never allowed to simply be. Let the mind faint for a moment.

## Day 3 [steady] Ashtavakra Gita 18.58
LINE: A restless mind is agitated even doing nothing; a skilled one stays calm doing everything.
TRANSLATION: The fool is restless even when doing nothing; the skilful one stays calm even when doing everything that needs doing.
MEANING: Ashtavakra speaks to Janaka, the king who has already given up his throne to seek truth. The king sits in the quiet of a forest, having left behind every duty that once defined him. Ashtavakra tells him that a restless mind is agitated even when doing nothing. The fool is restless even when doing nothing. The skilful one stays calm even when doing everything that needs doing. The point is that calm is not found by chasing it or changing places. It is how you hold ordinary life.
FOR YOU: The lists of what you could do never end. The mind runs even in the silence. Let the body do its work, but do not let the mind become its own enemy. It is enough to be doing. Let the doing be enough.

## Day 5 [act] Bhagavad Gita 6.26
LINE: Wherever the restless mind wanders, gently bring it back. Again and again.
TRANSLATION: Wherever the restless, unsteady mind wanders, from there one should draw it back and bring it under the control of the self.
MEANING: Krishna is showing Arjuna how to sit with the mind when it will not stay still. He says that the mind will wander. It will run after its own noise. The instruction is not to fight it or to give up. It is to gently guide it back. You do this over and over. Each time you notice it has left, you bring it back to the self. That is the work. It is a quiet, patient return, not a single victory.
FOR YOU: Before you answer anyone today, let your hand rest on your desk and ask your mind one quiet question. What do you need right now. Listen for the answer, and let that be your first step.

## Day 6 [act] Bhartrihari · Vairagya Shatakam
LINE: Why wander, mind? Rest. Not replaying the past, not scripting the future, I take what comes.
TRANSLATION: Why do you wander about in vain, mind? Rest somewhere. Whatever is to happen happens just as it will, and not otherwise. Not recalling the past, not planning the future, I enjoy the pleasures that come to me unlooked-for.
MEANING: Bhartrihari speaks to his own mind. He asks why it exhausts itself pleasing others, replaying the past and scripting the future. The mind is told to rest somewhere. Whatever is to happen happens just as it will, and not otherwise. He will enjoy the pleasures that come unlooked-for. The idea is to stop seeking approval and rehearsing outcomes. The calm you want is already inside. The poet asks what all our gains amount to. This is not an argument against planning. It is for the worry loop once you have done your part.
FOR YOU: You are answering to everyone else. Try this today. Sit for two minutes and ask your mind what it needs before you pick up the phone. Do not plan or replay. Just let the answer come.
