## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 6.34
LINE: The mind is restless, stormy, stubborn and strong. Holding it feels like holding the wind.
TRANSLATION: For the mind is restless, Krishna, turbulent, powerful and stubborn. I think it is as hard to hold as the wind.
MEANING: Arjuna is looking at their own mind and telling Krishna that it is impossible to control. The mind is restless, stormy, stubborn and strong. It is as hard to hold as the wind. Krishna hears this and does not argue. He simply says that the mind is hard to hold, but practice and letting go can train it.

## Day 3 [steady] Katha Upanishad 1.3.5-6
LINE: Without understanding, the senses bolt like wild horses; with a steady mind, they run like trained ones.
TRANSLATION: For one who lacks understanding, whose mind is never held steady, the senses run out of control like a driver's wild horses. For one who has understanding, whose mind is always steady, the senses obey like good horses.
MEANING: Yama speaks to Nachiketa about the chariot of the self. He says that without understanding, the senses bolt like wild horses. With a steady mind, they run like trained ones. He is explaining that the mind is the reins. If you do not hold the reins, the horses will run where they want. If you hold them, they go where you intend.
FOR YOU: The phone is a wild horse. It moves your mind wherever the screen lights up. You can hold the reins. Pick one task and let your eyes rest on it until it is done. Let the rest wait.

## Day 4 [steady] Bhagavad Gita 2.15
LINE: The one who stays the same in pain and pleasure, undisturbed by either, is ready for real freedom.
TRANSLATION: The steady person whom these do not disturb, who stays the same in pain and pleasure, becomes fit for immortality.
MEANING: Krishna tells Arjuna that the person who can stand in the middle of pain and pleasure without being thrown off balance is the one who is ready to be free. This is not about being cold or not feeling anything. It is about not letting a single feeling take over the whole person. When joy comes, it is known as joy. When pain comes, it is known as pain. Neither one is allowed to become the whole story.
FOR YOU: When the restlessness comes, let it be there as restlessness. Do not let it become the whole of who you are. You are the one who notices the restlessness, and that is a different thing entirely.

## Day 5 [act] Bhagavad Gita 6.26
LINE: Wherever the restless mind wanders, gently bring it back. Again and again.
TRANSLATION: Wherever the restless, unsteady mind wanders, from there one should draw it back and bring it under the control of the self.
MEANING: Krishna is showing Arjuna how to sit with the mind when it wants to run. The mind is not a wild animal that must be beaten into place. It is a restless thing that will look at a hundred different directions in one minute. The teaching is not to fight it. It is to notice where it has gone and to turn it back, gently, again and again. This is the whole practice. The mind does not need to be fixed in one day.
FOR YOU: Before you pick up the phone, sit for one quiet minute. When the mind wanders to the screen or the noise, notice it. Then bring it back to the feeling of your own breathing. Do this one more time. That is the practice for today.

## Day 6 [act] Ram Gita 46 (Adhyatma Ramayana, Uttara Kanda 5)
LINE: Sit somewhere quiet, let the senses rest, and give your full attention to one thing.
TRANSLATION: Sitting in a quiet place, senses at rest, self-mastered, pure within, one should contemplate the One with undivided attention, resting in the Self.
