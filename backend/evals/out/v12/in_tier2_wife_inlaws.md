## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 3.35
LINE: Better your own path done imperfectly than someone else's done perfectly. Borrowed lives are dangerous.
TRANSLATION: Better is one's own duty, even done imperfectly, than the duty of another well done. Better is death in one's own duty; the duty of another is full of fear.
MEANING: Krishna is telling Arjuna that it is better to follow your own path, even if you stumble on it, than to take on someone else's life and do it perfectly. The idea is that a borrowed life brings constant fear because it is not yours to own. Your own path, with all its flaws, is the only one that can truly be yours. The anger you feel right now comes from watching someone else try to write your ending while you stand silent.
FOR YOU: What you are cannot be cut smaller by what they see; your worth was never theirs to grant, and standing in your own truth is itself the duty.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Mahabharata · Vana Parva · Vana: Draupadi and Yudhishthira
LINE: Never look down on yourself. Nothing good grows from self-contempt.
TRANSLATION: A person should never look down on himself. The one who despises himself never finds real prosperity.
MEANING: Draupadi tells Yudhishthira that waiting on fate is no plan: people must act. She warns him that a person should never look down on himself. The one who despises himself never finds real prosperity. She argues that effort still matters, that fate rewards no one who waits.
FOR YOU: You built this practice from nothing. The picture your in-laws hold of you does not erase that. Let your work remain your own path, done imperfectly but truly. Let that be your answer.

## Day 3 [steady] Mahabharata · Udyoga Parva · Vidula's counsel
LINE: Don't think less of yourself or settle for little. Set your mind on something good, and stop fearing.
TRANSLATION: Do not hold yourself in contempt, and do not keep it going on too little. Set your mind on what is truly good; don't be afraid. Pull yourself together.
MEANING: Queen Vidula finds her son Sanjaya lying defeated after losing his kingdom. She does not coddle him. She tells him that self-contempt is a poison that stops all good from growing. She says to set his mind on something truly good and to stop fearing. The point is that past failure does not define you, and those who never try never succeed.
FOR YOU: The self cannot be cut, burned or made less; what they see is a role, and you were never only a role.

## Day 4 [steady] Katharudra Upanishad 8
LINE: Don't wear out your body chasing pleasure or fleeing pain. Praised, don't gloat; blamed, don't curse others.
TRANSLATION: Let him not torment the body too much for the sake of pleasure or pain. Praised, let him not gloat; blamed, let him not curse others.
MEANING: A teacher is laying down the ground rules for a renunciant. They are not telling them to seek out pleasure or run from pain. They are saying do not exhaust yourself chasing what feels good or avoiding what feels bad. If someone praises you, do not swell with pride. If someone blames you, do not curse them in return. The lesson is to steady your mind and keep your energy for what is real, not to waste it on the swings of approval and blame.
FOR YOU: When others see only a role, their picture is their smallness and not your measure; the harm deepens only when their picture begins to replace your own.

## Day 6 [act] Mahabharata · Shanti Parva · Bhishma, Rajadharma
LINE: People walk over the soft and fear the harsh. Be firm when it's needed, gentle when it's needed.
TRANSLATION: People look down even on the gentle, and they shrink from the harsh. Do not be only harsh, do not be only soft: be firm when firmness is needed and gentle when gentleness is.
MEANING: Brihaspati tells Indra that people will always look down on the gentle and shrink from the harsh. He advises Indra not to be only harsh or only soft, but to be firm when firmness is needed and gentle when gentleness is needed. The point is that authority is not a single tone. It is the ability to shift between two modes, to know which one the moment requires without losing oneself in either extreme.
FOR YOU: Speak the true thing gently and firmly, hold your ground on what is yours, and do your own duty without shrinking to fit the picture they hold.

## Day 7 [story] Ahalya: the long wait ends
LINE: For years a stone lay in an abandoned hermitage, and only a sage knew it was a woman waiting.
FOR YOU: Ahalya spent years as a stone, unseen and frozen, until one person looked at her and brought her back. The life you are building cannot stay frozen because someone looks away. Let them see what is there.
STORY: On the way to Janakpur, Vishvamitra shows Ram a stone in an empty hermitage: Gautam's wife Ahalya, bearing a curse in a body of stone, longing for the dust of his feet. At his touch she rises, stands before him with folded hands, unable to speak for love, tears streaming, and asks only that her mind may always drink at his feet.
LESSON: However long you have been frozen, being seen can bring you back to life.
