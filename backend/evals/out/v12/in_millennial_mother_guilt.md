## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 2.67
LINE: When the mind chases a wandering sense, it is swept off like a boat in the wind.
TRANSLATION: When the mind runs after the roving senses, it carries away one's understanding, as the wind carries a boat away on the water.
MEANING: Krishna answers Arjuna's question about the steady mind. He says that when the mind runs after the roving senses, it carries away one's understanding, as the wind carries a boat away on the water. The point is that steadiness is built by guarding attention, because anger and ruin start with what you dwell on.
FOR YOU: You feel half a mother and half a project manager. The guilt is heavy. But the mind that chases the roving senses is swept off like a boat in the wind. Your attention is the boat. When you are with your daughter, guard it. Let the work thoughts stay on the shore.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Yoga Vasishtha
LINE: No one hurts us like we hurt ourselves. The mind becomes the mind's own enemy.
TRANSLATION: Our own senses are the enemies; truth itself has turned untrue; the self strikes the self, and the mind is the mind's own enemy.
MEANING: Rama, after he has seen how wealth hardens people and how time eats everything, asks what the point of it all is. He tells his teachers, Vishvamitra and Vasishtha, that no one wounds us like we wound ourselves. The senses become our own enemies. Truth itself seems untrue. The self strikes the self, and the mind becomes its own enemy.
FOR YOU: The guilt you feel is not a verdict on your work or your care. It is the mind striking itself, caught between two places that both matter. The split you feel is the mind becoming its own enemy, not you failing at either role.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhagavad Gita 6.7
LINE: Self-mastered and calm, you stay centred in heat and cold, pleasure and pain, praise and insult.
TRANSLATION: For one who has conquered himself and is at peace, the higher self stays steady in cold and heat, pleasure and pain, honour and dishonour.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna as they stand on the field of war, telling them that the one who has mastered their own mind remains steady no matter what comes. Cold and heat, pleasure and pain, praise and insult pass over them like weather. The one who is at peace with themselves does not let the outer world shake their centre, because they know they are their own best friend or their own worst enemy. The choice is in how you meet each moment.
FOR YOU: In the car with your daughter, you were cold and hot with shame and love. You were praise and insult to yourself in the same breath. Today, let that steadiness be enough. You are not less for being pulled, and you do not have to prove you are fully one thing to be whole.

## Day 4 [steady] Hamsa Gita 21 (Mahabharata, Shanti 12.305.21 in our data)
LINE: Like the moon slipping out of clouds, the steady one waits for the right time and succeeds.
TRANSLATION: Freed from wrongs like the moon from dark clouds, spotless and steady, waiting for the right time, the wise one succeeds through patience.
MEANING: Bhishma tells Yudhishthira about the golden swan Hamsa, who taught the gods that self-control and truth are the best way to live. The swan said that like the moon slipping out of clouds, the steady one waits for the right time and succeeds. This is not about giving up. It is about staying calm and doing your duty in the moment, without being thrown off by heat or cold, praise or insult.
FOR YOU: The guilt you feel at work and at home is not a sign you are failing. It is the feeling of two honest duties pulling at you. Let the guilt be there, but do not let it decide that you are half of everything.

## Day 5 [act] Aitareya Upanishad 2.1.3
LINE: Caring for your child, you care for yourself. Through them you are born a second time.
TRANSLATION: When he cares for the child from birth onward, he is caring for himself, for the continuation of these worlds; that is how they continue. That is his second birth.
MEANING: A seer speaks to a reader about the second birth. The child is born, and now the parent must raise them. The seer says that when a parent cares for the child from the very beginning, they are caring for themselves. The child is the continuation of the parent's life and the worlds. To raise a child is to be born again, carrying your own life forward through them.
FOR YOU: Your daughter's question about school is a second birth calling out. For one hour today, let her be your only world. Put your work aside and let her see the whole of you. That is how you care for yourself, by caring for her.

## Day 6 [act] Mahabharata · Udyoga Parva · Udyoga: Krishna's peace embassy
LINE: When children go astray, a parent's job is to set them back on the path, not look away.
TRANSLATION: We have treated you as a father; treat us the same way. When children go astray, it is the father's duty to set them back on the path.
MEANING: Krishna stands before Dhritarashtra's court in Hastinapura. He has come as a messenger of peace, asking the king to hold his sons back from war. He reminds the king that a parent's duty is to guide a child back when they stray, not to look away. The point is that you can make the best possible case, speak with full care, and still be refused. The burden of speaking does not rest on the outcome. It rests on the fact that you spoke.
FOR YOU: You are in your car, or at your desk, or at your daughter's school gate. Pick the one place you are right now. For the next hour, let the other role go. Be only the mother, or only the project manager, and let the rest wait.

## Day 7 [story] Kausalya and the child who ran from dinner
LINE: Kausalya was shown the whole universe in her newborn, and all she wanted was for him to cry like a baby.
FOR YOU: Kausalya saw the whole cosmos in her son, yet she begged him to stay small. You do not have to be the perfect mother or the flawless manager. In the moment you are with your daughter, let yourself be small with her. That ordinary presence is its own kind of greatness.
STORY: At his birth Ram shows Kausalya his cosmic form, and she asks him instead to be simply a baby. Later scenes are pure childhood: the boy runs off mid-meal, curd on his face, dusty from play, while his father laughs and his mother chases him. LESSON: The grandest thing about a child may be the ordinary hours of childhood itself.
