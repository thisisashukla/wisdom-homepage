## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 2.7
LINE: I'm confused about what's right. Tell me plainly what's best; I'm ready to learn.
TRANSLATION: My nature is stricken by weakness and my mind is confused about what is right. I ask you: tell me for certain what is best for me. I am your student; I have come to you, teach me.
MEANING: Arjuna stands on the field, his body shaking, his mind torn between two armies of his own kin. He tells Krishna he is paralyzed by grief and cannot see the way forward. He admits he does not know what is right, that his nature is overcome by weakness, and he asks Krishna to teach him plainly. This is the beginning of clarity. It starts not with a command, but with the courage to say I do not know, and to ask for help.
FOR YOU: You are running the business, yet the man who built it still stands in the room and speaks over you. You feel the weight of that silence in your chest. Today, let yourself say plainly to the truth of it. You do not need to fix the wrong right now.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Bhagavad Gita 18.16
LINE: If you think you alone made it happen, or alone made it fail, you're not seeing the whole picture.
TRANSLATION: That being so, whoever, through an untrained understanding, sees himself alone as the doer does not truly see.
MEANING: Krishna speaks to Arjuna on the battlefield, just after explaining that every action has five causes. He warns that the person who ignores these and believes they alone are the doer is mistaken. The verse says that if you think you alone made it happen, or alone made it fail, you are not seeing the whole picture. It asks you to own your part, but not the whole outcome. This is not a way to dodge responsibility. Your part is still real.
FOR YOU: Your father walks in and changes a decision in front of the staff. That is his action, not yours. You are one cause among five in the room, not the whole picture. Let the outcome belong to the whole, not only to your shoulders.

## Day 6 [act] Bhagavad Gita 17.15
LINE: Speak words that don't wound, that are true, kind and helpful. That is discipline of speech.
TRANSLATION: Speech that causes no distress, that is truthful, pleasant and beneficial, and the regular study of sacred texts: this is called austerity of speech.
MEANING: Krishna is laying out the qualities of a life that holds together. He tells Arjuna that speech should be a discipline. It should not wound. It should be true. It should be kind. It should be useful. It should also drop the performance, the show. This is the austerity of speech. What you say should build rather than break, and it should carry no hidden cost.
FOR YOU: When your father walks in and changes a decision, your first instinct is to swallow the insult and protect the room. Today, speak one sentence that is true, kind, and useful. State the fact without the wound.
