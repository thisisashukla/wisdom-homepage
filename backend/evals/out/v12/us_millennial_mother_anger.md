## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 6.34
LINE: The mind is restless, stormy, stubborn and strong. Holding it feels like holding the wind.
TRANSLATION: For the mind is restless, Krishna, turbulent, powerful and stubborn. I think it is as hard to hold as the wind.
MEANING: Arjuna, on the battlefield, tells Krishna that the mind is restless, stormy, powerful and stubborn. He feels it is as hard to hold as the wind. Krishna does not tell him to simply calm down. He agrees the mind is difficult, but says practice and letting go can train it. The verse proposes that holding the mind is a skill learned through steady effort, not a single act of force.
FOR YOU: The snap at home and the calm at work come from the same mind. The fear of becoming your mother is the mind judging itself after it finds its only release. You do not need to become a different person.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Bhagavad Gita 2.62
LINE: Dwell on something and you get attached; attachment becomes wanting, and blocked wanting turns into anger.
TRANSLATION: When a person dwells on objects, attachment to them is born. From attachment comes desire, and from desire, anger.
MEANING: Krishna tells Arjuna that a person's mind is shaped by what it dwells upon. When you fix your attention on objects and outcomes, attachment forms. If that attachment is blocked, it hardens into anger. The verse traces this exact path from focus to frustration, showing that the mind is not the problem, but the direction of its gaze is. The steady person is the one who guards this attention, knowing that where the mind rests, the heart follows.
FOR YOU: You manage people calmly at work. That steadiness is real. The anger at home is not a verdict on your character. It is the result of a mind that has had no other release all day, and then turns on itself in the evening.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhagavad Gita 5.23
LINE: Whoever can ride out the surge of desire and anger, here and now, is steady and happy.
TRANSLATION: One who is able, here in this life, before leaving the body, to withstand the surge born of desire and anger, is disciplined; he is a happy person.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna on the battlefield, describing the inner happiness of someone who sees clearly. He says that a person who can withstand the surge born of desire and anger, right here in this life, is truly disciplined and happy. This joy does not come from pleasures that start and end, but from finding a steady place within. The wise person sees that sense pleasures end in sorrow, and they are able to ride out these surges without being carried away.
FOR YOU: The same mind that snaps at your child is the one that can choose a different response. The texts say the self who gave in to anger is the same self who can decide to stand firm next time, right here in your own life.
