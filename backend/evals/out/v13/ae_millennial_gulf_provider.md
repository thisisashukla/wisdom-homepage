## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 6.34
LINE: The mind is restless, stormy, stubborn and strong. Holding it feels like holding the wind.
TRANSLATION: For the mind is restless, Krishna, turbulent, powerful and stubborn. I think it is as hard to hold as the wind.
MEANING: Arjuna speaks to Krishna on the battlefield, admitting that the mind is turbulent and stubborn. He says holding it feels like trying to catch the wind. Krishna agrees with him. He says the mind is hard to hold, but practice and letting go can train it.
FOR YOU: The mind that will not settle is not yours to fix alone today. It is only showing you how many directions are pulling at once. Let it be stormy. You do not need to hold the wind.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Yoga Vasishtha
LINE: 'Done this, doing this, must do that.' Caught in endless lists, the mind never gets a moment's rest.
TRANSLATION: Carried along on endless great waves of tasks, the mind never gets a moment's rest; its depths dance with worry. 'This I've done, this I'm doing, this I'll do': so the bird of the mind, tangled in a net of reckonings, faints.
MEANING: Two old friends, Bhasa and Vilasa, meet again after years of living alone in the forest. They talk about the life they have led. Vilasa admits they never found any real peace. He says the mind is always caught in a loop of past, present, and future tasks. It is carried on waves of doing and worrying, and it faints under the weight of endless lists. Years of hard effort without inner understanding leave a person exactly where they started, still exhausted.
FOR YOU: The exhaustion you feel is not because you are not enough. It is the conflict of duties, not the lack of you, that is draining you. Your mind is caught in the loop of what you have done, what you are doing now, and what you could do next.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: Let them praise or blame, let money come or go: the steady don't step off the right path.
TRANSLATION: Let the worldly-wise blame or praise; let fortune come or go as she pleases; let death come today or ages hence: the steadfast do not move a step from the path of what is right.
MEANING: Bhartrihari speaks to the reader from a place of deep weariness, knowing that praise and blame are just noise. He says that whether fortune smiles or turns away, and whether death comes today or far in the future, the steadfast do not move a step from the path of what is right. The verse proposes that you do not need to chase after the shifting opinions of others or the changing tides of luck to know where to stand.
FOR YOU: You are holding the weight of what your family expects while your mind refuses to settle. This verse says your own sense of what is right is the only ground you could stand on. Let their voices and their needs be the weather, but you keep your feet on the path that is yours alone.

## Day 4 [steady] Bhagavad Gita 5.20
LINE: Don't get carried away by good news or crushed by bad. Keep your understanding steady.
TRANSLATION: One should not rejoice on getting what is pleasant nor be troubled on getting what is unpleasant. Steady in understanding and undeluded, the knower of Brahman abides in Brahman.
MEANING: Krishna speaks to Arjuna on the inner happiness of one who sees clearly. The wise do not leap with joy when pleasant things arrive, nor do they collapse when unpleasant things come. They hold their understanding steady and undeluded, knowing that sense pleasures begin and end in sorrow. They abide in the deeper joy within, able to withstand desire and anger.
FOR YOU: That money sent from Dubai and the expectations at home are not yours to carry. What is truly yours is the steady work and the quiet hour that belongs only to you. Let the rest come and go, and keep your mind on what is right in front of you.

## Day 5 [act] Bhagavad Gita 2.47
LINE: Your work is yours to do; the results are not yours to hold. Don't let that make you stop.
TRANSLATION: Your right is to the work alone, never to its fruits. Do not let the fruit be your motive, and do not cling to doing nothing.
MEANING: Krishna speaks to Arjuna on the battlefield, telling him to focus entirely on the work itself and release his grip on the results. The idea is to act with full dedication while letting go of ownership over the outcome. It does not mean abandoning plans or ceasing to aim. It means working wholeheartedly and keeping your mind steady, whether the outcome is good or bad.
FOR YOU: Try setting aside one hour this evening that belongs only to you, without checking messages or thinking about money. Let go of the urge to solve everything for your family during that time. Just be present with the work you can control right now, and leave the rest for tomorrow.
