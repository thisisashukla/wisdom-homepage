## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 18.47
LINE: Better your own path, imperfectly walked, than someone else's done perfectly.
TRANSLATION: Better is one's own duty, though imperfect, than the duty of another well performed. Doing the work born of one's own nature, one incurs no wrong.
MEANING: Krishna is telling Arjuna that the work born from your own nature is the only work that truly fits you. He says that even if your own path is walked with flaws, it is better than performing someone else's duty perfectly. The work that comes from who you are carries no wrong, no matter how it looks to the world.
FOR YOU: You are carrying a business built by your own hands. Your father is overriding that work in public, and you are swallowing the insult because of the bond between you. This verse says your own nature is your true duty. Your work is not wrong just because someone else is watching it poorly.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Bhagavad Gita 18.16
LINE: If you think you alone made it happen, or alone made it fail, you're not seeing the whole picture.
TRANSLATION: That being so, whoever, through an untrained understanding, sees himself alone as the doer does not truly see.
MEANING: Krishna speaks to Arjuna after listing five causes behind every action. He says that the person who believes they alone made something happen or alone caused it to fail does not see the whole picture. The truth is that you are only one cause among five. Your part is real, but it is not the entire story. You are one piece of the puzzle, not the whole design.
FOR YOU: You built this business, but you are not the only cause of its life. See the five causes behind the daily friction. Name your part without claiming the whole outcome. Let the truth of your part hold your ground.

## Day 5 [act] Maha Upanishad 6.64-65
LINE: Do the work in front of you, speak gently, read people's hearts, and you won't sink in the world.
TRANSLATION: Always doing the work that comes, seeing friend and foe alike, free of wanting and not-wanting, such a one neither grieves nor craves. Saying what is agreeable to all, speaking gently when asked, knowing the intent of others' hearts, such a one does not sink in the world.
MEANING: Ribhu tells Nidagha that a person who is truly free does not get pulled under by the world's currents. They simply take up the work that is in front of them right now. They treat a friend and a foe with the same steady gaze. They let go of wanting things and pushing things away. They speak what is fair and kind, and they read the hearts of others with care. Because they do not cling to outcomes and they move with a calm inside, they do not drown in the world.
FOR YOU: You are carrying the work of your business and the weight of your parents. Let the work in front of you be enough. Speak gently to your father, but let your own care for the business be clear. Do not let his words pull you under.

## Day 6 [act] Valmiki Ramayana · Kishkindha Kanda
LINE: Not too long, not vague, not rushed, not strained, in an even voice: that is how to speak.
TRANSLATION: His words were not long-winded, not ambiguous, not rushed, not strained; they came from the chest and throat in an even, middle tone.
MEANING: Ram watches Hanuman speak to them and sees how the words land. Hanuman did not rush, did not use big words to sound important. He spoke in a steady, middle tone, as if he had all the time in the world. Ram notices that this calm, even speech is what builds trust. When someone speaks clearly and without strain, fear steps back and facts can take their place. That is what Ram notices about Hanuman, and it changes everything.
FOR YOU: When you speak to your father, let your voice come from the middle of your chest. Do not rush to prove your point. Speak the facts of the business in an even tone. Let the truth of what you built stand on its own without you straining to fill the silence.
