## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 18.47
LINE: Better your own path, imperfectly walked, than someone else's done perfectly.
TRANSLATION: Better is one's own duty, though imperfect, than the duty of another well performed. Doing the work born of one's own nature, one incurs no wrong.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna on the battlefield, where Arjuna is frozen by grief and confusion. Arjuna is asking whether it is better to fight or to step back. Krishna answers that doing the work that fits your own nature is the truest offering. Even if your own work is flawed, even if you stumble, it is better than taking on someone else's role perfectly. The work you are born to do, with all its imperfections, carries no wrong.
FOR YOU: Your parents want you to take their test. But your own path is the night shift you are already walking. Let that work, with all its flaws, be your offering today, and let it be enough.

TRANSLATION: O heart, why do you enter into weariness, day after day trying to please the minds of others in so many ways, to win their favour? When you are at peace within, your own clear resolve rises by itself like a cluster of wish-granting gems: what wish of yours would it not fulfil?
MEANING: Bhartrihari speaks to his own heart at the threshold of the Advice to the Mind. He asks why the mind exhausts itself, day after day, rehearsing ways to please others and win their favour. He says that when you settle inside, your own clear resolve rises naturally, like a cluster of wish-granting gems. What wish of yours would it not fulfil? He is asking to stop the exhausting performance of winning approval, because the calm you are looking for is already there, waiting inside.

LINE: Praise and blame weigh the same. Quiet, content with what comes, rooted in steadiness, not status.
