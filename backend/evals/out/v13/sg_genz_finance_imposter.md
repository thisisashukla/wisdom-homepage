## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 6.34
LINE: The mind is restless, stormy, stubborn and strong. Holding it feels like holding the wind.
TRANSLATION: For the mind is restless, Krishna, turbulent, powerful and stubborn. I think it is as hard to hold as the wind.
MEANING: Arjuna tells Krishna that the mind is restless, turbulent and stubborn, and as hard to control as the wind. Krishna agrees, but he also says that through practice and letting go, the mind can be trained. The verse does not promise that the wind will stop blowing. It says that you can learn to steer through it.
FOR YOU: You feel sick before you present. That is the wind. The replaying of who your father was and who you are is the storm. You do not need to stop the thoughts today. You only need to notice that they are blowing through, and that you can still stand in the room while they pass.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: When I knew a little, I thought I knew everything. Learning from the wise, my fever of pride broke.
TRANSLATION: When I knew a little, I was blind with pride like an elephant in rut, and my mind swelled: 'I know everything.' When, little by little, I learned from the wise, I realised 'I am a fool', and my pride left me like a fever.
MEANING: The poet Bhartrihari looks back at his own youth and remembers how a little knowledge made him arrogant and blind. He thought he knew everything, like an elephant in rut. But slowly, by learning from wiser people, he saw his own foolishness. His pride left him like a fever breaking. The point is simple. You cannot force every mind to change, and the first mind you could check is your own.
FOR YOU: The over-preparing will not land until you let the wise in. Let the next review be a place to check your own mind, not just to prove it.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: A ball knocked down bounces back up. For good people, setbacks rarely last.
TRANSLATION: A ball struck down by the hand flies right back up. As a rule, the misfortunes of those who live rightly do not last.
MEANING: Bhartrihari speaks from a long view, not a single moment. They have seen the mind swell with a little knowledge and then crash against what it does not know. They tell the reader that a ball struck down by a hand flies right back up. The point is that setbacks rarely last for those who keep going on the right path. The text does not promise that pain is always short, or that long suffering means a person did something wrong.
FOR YOU: The promotion is something that happened to you, not the whole truth of who you are. Let that sentence settle. You do not have to be the person who knows everything to be the person who is good enough.

TRANSLATION: Raise yourself by your own self; do not let yourself sink. For the self alone is the friend of the self, and the self alone is the enemy of the self.

## Day 6 [act] Mahabharata · Vana Parva · Vyadha Gita
LINE: Turn to doing good after a wrong, and you come out like the moon from behind a cloud.
TRANSLATION: If a person who has done wrong turns to doing good, he is freed from all his wrongs, like the moon coming out from behind a great cloud.
MEANING: In Mithila, the meat-seller Dharmavyadha tells the proud ascetic Kaushika that goodness is not a distant idea. It is simply this: after a wrong, turn to doing good.
