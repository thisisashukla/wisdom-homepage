## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 6.34
LINE: The mind is restless, stormy, stubborn and strong. Holding it feels like holding the wind.
TRANSLATION: For the mind is restless, Krishna, turbulent, powerful and stubborn. I think it is as hard to hold as the wind.
MEANING: Arjuna tells Krishna that the mind is restless and stubborn, as hard to hold as the wind. Krishna agrees, but says that through practice and letting go, it can be trained. The idea is that the mind resists force, yet it can be steadied by returning to it again and again, not by gripping it tighter.
FOR YOU: The terror at 2am and the rating you fear is your mind running ahead of you. You do not have to hold the wind. You can return to your breath, to the dark room, to the fact that the phone is still in your hand, and let the storm pass through.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Ashtavakra Gita 11.5
LINE: Suffering is born of worry, nothing else. Free of worry, you are happy and at peace.
TRANSLATION: One who knows for certain that suffering here is born of worry and nothing else is free of it, happy, at peace, and unattached everywhere.
MEANING: Ashtavakra speaks to Janaka, who sits surrounded by wealth and power yet cannot sleep. He tells him that the only thing that ever creates pain is the mind's habit of worrying. When a person sees clearly that worry itself is the root of all trouble, the grip of fear loosens. The real problems are still there, but the extra agony of imagining them over and over falls away. The mind can then rest in the present, even when the future is uncertain.
FOR YOU: The rating on your work is a real thing. But the terror you feel at two in the morning is mostly the extra weight of worry. You can let the rating be what it is, without letting it become the story that keeps you awake.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhagavad Gita 2.47
LINE: Your work is yours to do; the results are not yours to hold. Don't let that make you stop.
TRANSLATION: Your right is to the work alone, never to its fruits. Do not let the fruit be your motive, and do not cling to doing nothing.
MEANING: Krishna tells Arjuna that your only right is to the work itself, not to its fruits. He speaks to Arjuna on the battlefield, where the stakes are life and death, and shows that the moment for action is now, not after the outcome is known. The verse asks you to act fully, but to stop letting the result own you. It is a steadying thought, because it names a boundary between what you can do and what you cannot, and it lets you rest in the doing.
FOR YOU: Your work is yours to do. The results are not yours to hold. You can put your full attention into the task, but the rating is not yours to hold. Let it be what it is, and let your attention rest on the doing, not the outcome.

## Day 4 [steady] Maha Upanishad 6.68
LINE: Be busy on the outside and unhurried inside. Act fully, but don't let it agitate you.
TRANSLATION: Outwardly busy, inwardly free of agitation; a doer on the outside, a non-doer within: move through the world with a clear mind.
MEANING: Ribhu tells Nidagha how a person who is free inside lives in the world. They let go of what is lost, take up what comes, do the work at hand, speak gently, and treat the whole world as family. They are busy on the outside but unhurried inside. They act fully in the world without letting it agitate them. The truth is, you can do your work with a clear mind even when the ground beneath you feels shaky.
FOR YOU: Your work and your visa are real things, and you are doing them. But the terror that a bad rating will erase your life in days is not a fact. It is a story your mind is telling you over and over. You can do the work at hand while the fear is there.

## Day 5 [act] Bhagavad Gita 12.6
LINE: Lay your work in me and keep your mind on me; I soon lift you out of the drowning sea.
TRANSLATION: But those who lay all their actions in me, intent on me, and worship me meditating with single-minded yoga — for them, whose minds are set on me, I soon become the one who lifts them out of the ocean of death and rebirth, Partha.
MEANING: Krishna is telling Arjuna what it means to be truly devoted. He says that those who place every action in him and keep their mind fixed on him are lifted out of the cycle of life and death. The verse proposes that when you give your work to a presence beyond yourself, you do not have to carry the weight of it alone.
FOR YOU: You do not have to let the rating own your nights. When the phone lights up at two in the morning, try telling yourself that the work is laid down for now. Let the evening belong to the room and the quiet, not to the screen.

## Day 6 [act] Ashtavakra Gita 18.20
LINE: Don't cling to doing or to not doing. Do what's in front of you and stay at ease.
TRANSLATION: The steady person has no stubborn attachment to doing or to not doing. Doing whatever comes to be done, he stays at ease.
MEANING: Ashtavakra speaks to Janaka about the calm of the wise. The wise person does not cling to the idea of doing things or not doing things. They simply do what is in front of them and remain at ease. The restless chase peace and never find it, but the calm person holds ordinary life without struggle. This is the end of the long chapter on peace. The calm is not found by chasing it or changing places, but by how you hold your daily work.
FOR YOU: Try one hour where you do nothing for work, and one evening where the phone stays away. Let the silence hold you instead of filling it.
