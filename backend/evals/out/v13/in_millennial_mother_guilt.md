## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 6.34
LINE: The mind is restless, stormy, stubborn and strong. Holding it feels like holding the wind.
TRANSLATION: For the mind is restless, Krishna, turbulent, powerful and stubborn. I think it is as hard to hold as the wind.
MEANING: Arjuna tells Krishna that his mind is restless, stormy, stubborn and strong. He says holding it is like trying to hold the wind. Krishna agrees that the mind is hard to hold. He says it can be trained through practice and by letting go of attachment to results.
FOR YOU: What you feel right now is the pull between the mother you are and the professional you are. That guilt is not a verdict. It is the mind caught between two duties, and it is not a failure.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Ramcharitmanas · Ayodhya Kand
LINE: Duty and self-interest pull against each other. Hatred is blind, and love can't reason.
TRANSLATION: The Vedas, scriptures and Puranas say, and the world knows, that the duty of service is hard. Duty to one's master and self-interest pull against each other; hatred is blind, and love has no judgement.
MEANING: Bharat is standing before Janaka in the court at Ayodhya. Janaka asks him to judge a dispute between his own brothers. Bharat says he cannot do it. He says duty to the king and duty to his own family pull against each other. Hatred is blind, and love has no judgment. When you are too close to a choice, you could say so and speak briefly and honestly. This is what it means to be torn between two roles.
FOR YOU: You are trying to be the perfect mother and the perfect professional at the same time. The guilt comes from expecting one duty to be everything. Let yourself say out loud that these two roles pull against each other. You do not have to choose one.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: Let them praise or blame, let money come or go: the steady don't step off the right path.
TRANSLATION: Let the worldly-wise blame or praise; let fortune come or go as she pleases; let death come today or ages hence: the steadfast do not move a step from the path of what is right.
MEANING: Bhartrihari is writing to anyone who reads these verses. He is talking about the kind of person who stays on their course no matter what happens. People will praise them or they will blame them. Money will flow in or it will dry up. Death might come today or it might wait for years. Through all of this, the steady person does not step off the path of what is right. They do not move a step from the right path.
FOR YOU: You are holding two duties right now and neither one feels light. When your daughter asked about the school events, that hurt because you want to be there. But your work is also real and it needs you. Let the praise or blame of the day come and go.

## Day 4 [steady] Maha Upanishad 6.68
LINE: Be busy on the outside and unhurried inside. Act fully, but don't let it agitate you.
TRANSLATION: Outwardly busy, inwardly free of agitation; a doer on the outside, a non-doer within: move through the world with a clear mind.
MEANING: Ribhu, a teacher of stillness, is speaking to Nidagha, a householder who still thinks freedom means leaving the world. Ribhu tells him that a free person does not stop working or talking to people. They simply do not let the noise inside. They let praise or blame come and go. They let money appear or vanish. They do the task in front of them with a clear mind, but they do not let the result pull them away. This is the steady path. The mind stays free even while the hands are busy.
FOR YOU: You are back at work, and your daughter's question still sits in your chest. You do not have to choose between being a mother and a professional today. You can do both and let the guilt come and go.

## Day 5 [act] Bhagavad Gita 2.47
LINE: Your work is yours to do; the results are not yours to hold. Don't let that make you stop.
TRANSLATION: Your right is to the work alone, never to its fruits. Do not let the fruit be your motive, and do not cling to doing nothing.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna on the battlefield, showing him how to act without being chained to what happens next. He tells him to do the work fully and with his whole attention, but to let go of clinging to the results. The work itself is what belongs to him. The outcome is not. This does not mean you stop caring or stop planning. It means you do not let the result own your mind. You give your action to the moment and keep your center steady, whether success comes or failure.
FOR YOU: Try one honest conversation with your daughter today. Then, when you are with her, let the work of being with her be the only thing. Do not let the result of her mood or her words own your mind. Act fully in the moment, and let the rest go.

## Day 6 [act] Mahabharata · Udyoga Parva · Vidula's counsel
LINE: Don't think less of yourself or settle for little. Set your mind on something good, and stop fearing.
TRANSLATION: Do not hold yourself in contempt, and do not keep it going on too little. Set your mind on what is truly good; don't be afraid. Pull yourself together.
MEANING: Queen Vidula finds her son Sanjaya lying defeated after he has lost his kingdom. She tells him not to hold himself in contempt, not to settle for so little, and not to be afraid. She says he must set his mind on what is truly good and pull himself together. She is not offering comfort, but a charge to rise above his despair.
FOR YOU: The cry in the car was your daughter asking for your full presence. Today, let her see it. Put your phone away during a single meal or walk, and let her feel that you are truly there with her.
