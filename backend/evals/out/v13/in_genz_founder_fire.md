## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 18.48
LINE: Don't abandon work that suits you just because it has flaws. Every undertaking has some, like fire has smoke.
TRANSLATION: One should not give up the work suited to one's nature, Kaunteya, even if it has faults; for all undertakings are clouded by faults, as fire is by smoke.
MEANING: Krishna is telling Arjuna that a person should not abandon the work that suits their nature just because it has flaws. He says that every undertaking is clouded by faults, just as fire is covered by smoke. The path is not perfect. It will have smoke. But the work itself is the offering. The point is to do the work that fits you, flaws and all, as an offering.
FOR YOU: You left your job last week and the work is yours now. It will have smoke. The hard days will come and the fire will feel less bright. Do the work anyway. Let the flaws be part of the offering.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Yoga Vasishtha
LINE: Effort brings results, sooner or later, depending on time and place. That timing is what we call fate.
TRANSLATION: Results come from effort, sooner or later, depending on place and time. That arrival of the fruit, early or late, is what people call fate.
MEANING: Vasishtha speaks to Rama after Rama has given up on his own life. Vasishtha tells him that what people call fate is nothing but the fruit of past effort, arriving early or late depending on place and time. The timing is not a mystery. It is simply the result of what was done before. Today's effort shapes tomorrow. Do not grieve over what was never in your hands.
FOR YOU: You quit last week. The money is not there yet. This is not a flaw. This is the smoke from the fire you started. The result is coming. Your effort is what is shaping tomorrow. Do not let the wait look like failure.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: Let them praise or blame, let money come or go: the steady don't step off the right path.
TRANSLATION: Let the worldly-wise blame or praise; let fortune come or go as she pleases; let death come today or ages hence: the steadfast do not move a step from the path of what is right.
MEANING: Bhartrihari is telling the reader that the wise do not let outside noise change their course. Let people praise or blame. Let money come or go, or let death arrive today or in a thousand years. The steadfast person does not move a step from the right path. The text says setbacks rarely last for those who keep going on the right path. It uses the image of a struck ball bouncing back and a cut tree growing again.
FOR YOU: When your bank balance is empty and the work feels raw, you can feel the noise of doubt. Let the praise and blame of others pass through you. Hold on to the right path, and the steady bounce back will carry you through the hard mornings.
