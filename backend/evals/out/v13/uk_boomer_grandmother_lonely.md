## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 15.15
LINE: I am seated in every heart. Memory, knowledge and their fading come from me.
TRANSLATION: I am seated in the hearts of all. From me come memory, knowledge and their loss. I alone am what is to be known by all the Vedas; I am the author of Vedanta and the knower of the Vedas.
MEANING: Krishna tells Arjuna that he sits inside every heart, and from that place memory and knowledge arise, and also their loss. He is the source of all the Vedas and the one they point to. The world runs on this presence, and the mind draws its knowing from it. The loneliness you feel when your children look away is the ache of this inner light being dimmed by distraction.
FOR YOU: When your children sit in the room but look at their screens, they are not truly there. The company you find in the morning, before they arrive, is the one that does not look away. It is already seated in your heart, waiting.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Mahabharata · Shanti Parva · Shanti: Consolation of Yudhishthira
LINE: In grief, ask: where did I come from, where am I going, who am I? Let that steady you.
TRANSLATION: 'Where was I before? Where will I go? Who am I, really, and why am I here? Why should I grieve, and for what?' Ask these, and steady the mind.
MEANING: Ashma speaks to King Janaka, who has lost his family and wealth, about how to carry such grief. He tells him that pain comes from what we did not want and what we have lost, and that meetings always end. Ashma says that days do not come back. He asks Janaka to steady his mind by asking where he came from, where he is going, and who he is. This is not to dismiss the grief, but to hold it steady until it passes.
FOR YOU: The Ramayan is already doing this for you in the morning. It asks you where you come from and who you are. Let that be the steady company that Sunday cannot disturb.

## Day 3 [steady] Ramcharitmanas · Ayodhya Kand
LINE: Gain and loss, meeting and parting come like day and night. The steady hold both alike.
TRANSLATION: Birth and death, joy and sorrow, loss and gain, meeting and parting with those we love: these come under the sway of time and action, as surely as night and day. The foolish rejoice in good times and wail in bad; the steady hold both alike in the mind. Hold on to your courage, think with discernment, and let go of worry, you who care for everyone.
MEANING: Sumantra, the minister who drove Rama into the forest, returns to Dasharath, who is drowning in grief. He tells the old king that gain and loss, meeting and parting are as regular as night and day. The foolish mind swings between joy and sorrow, but the steady mind holds both the same. He asks the king to keep his courage, think clearly, and let go of worry.
FOR YOU: The Sunday you wait for is like the king's morning. Your children come, but the phones between you are a parting. You already hold the steady mind. Let the Ramayan be your courage, not a worry. Let it steady you while it hurts.
