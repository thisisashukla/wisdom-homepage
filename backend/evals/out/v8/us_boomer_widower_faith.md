## Day 2 [diagnose] Mahabharata · Shanti Parva · Shanti: Consolation of Yudhishthira
LINE: In grief, ask: where did I come from, where am I going, who am I? Let that steady you.
TRANSLATION: 'Where was I before? Where will I go? Who am I, really, and why am I here? Why should I grieve, and for what?' Ask these, and steady the mind.
MEANING: The sage Ashma speaks to King Janaka after he has lost family and wealth. Ashma says pain comes from what we did not want or what we lost, and that meetings end and days do not return. He tells the king to steady the mind by asking where he came from, where he is going, and who he is, so the grief can settle into something clear.
FOR YOU: Love does not end when a life does; grieve fully, and let the bond keep living in how you live.

LINE: Even if you believe life simply begins and ends, there's still no need to be crushed by grief.

Krishna tells him that even if the body is only a beginning and an end, there is no reason to collapse under the weight of it. The point is that what is real in us outlasts every loss and every passing pain.

## Day 4 [steady] Katha Upanishad 1.2.22
LINE: Knowing what stays steady inside all that changes, the wise do not grieve.
TRANSLATION: Knowing the self as bodiless within bodies, steady among the unsteady, great and all-pervading, the wise one does not grieve.
MEANING: Yama, the god of death, speaks to Nachiketa about the self that lives inside the body but is not the body itself. Even as the body changes and dies, this self remains. It is not born, and it does not die. It is steady and all-pervading. The wise person knows this and does not grieve, for what is real within them cannot be lost.
FOR YOU: The evenings you face now are heavy with a love that has nowhere to go. This verse tells you that the core of who you are is not touched by parting. What feels lost is not you, and you cannot be lost.
