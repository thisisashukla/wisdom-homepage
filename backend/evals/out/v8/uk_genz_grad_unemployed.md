## Day 2 [diagnose] Ramcharitmanas · Uttar Kand
LINE: Envy is an itch. Burning at another's happiness is a wasting disease of the mind.
TRANSLATION: Possessiveness is ringworm and envy is the itch; the swings of elation and dejection are throat ailments. The burning you feel at another's happiness is consumption, and malice and a crooked mind are leprosy.
MEANING: Kakbhushundi, a sage who has lived through many cycles of time, is telling Garuda, the mighty bird, about the sicknesses of the human mind. He says that possessiveness is like a skin disease, and envy is a constant itch. When you burn at the sight of another person's happiness, it is like a wasting sickness that consumes you from the inside. He lists these feelings as the real diseases: confusion, anger, greed, envy, and ego. These are the things that truly make a person sick, not the circumstances of their life.
FOR YOU: The shame you feel right now is doing the work that your applications left undone. You are not failing at your job. You are suffering from the itch of comparison. Notice that the envy is the real work, not the flat whites or the quiet room.

## Day 4 [steady] Bhagavad Gita 18.47
LINE: Better your own path, imperfectly walked, than someone else's done perfectly.
TRANSLATION: Better is one's own duty, though imperfect, than the duty of another well performed. Doing the work born of one's own nature, one incurs no wrong.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna on the battlefield, telling him that it is better to do your own work imperfectly than to do another person's work perfectly. Every person has their own nature and their own path. If you do what is yours, even if you stumble along the way, you are not doing anything wrong. You simply stay true to yourself.
FOR YOU: Your mother calls it exploring, but it does not feel like that right now. You are making flat whites and sending out applications. That is your own path. It might be imperfect and it might be slow, but it is the only one you have. Hold onto that.

## Day 6 [act] Mahabharata · Shanti Parva · Bhishma, Rajadharma
LINE: Make peace with other people's success, even when you lack it. The wise can enjoy good fortune that isn't theirs.
TRANSLATION: Bear the good fortune of others, even though it is not with you. Wise people know how to enjoy good fortune even when it belongs to someone else; good fortune drains away from the one who resents.
MEANING: The sage Kalakavrikshiya speaks to the fallen prince Kshemadarshi, who has lost his kingdom and sits in grief. The sage says that sorrow cannot bring back what is gone. He tells the prince to want what is within reach. He asks him to make peace with other people's success, even when it is not his. The wise know how to enjoy good fortune that belongs to someone else, because resentment drains away a person's own life.
FOR YOU: You have sent your applications and you are still waiting. Your friends at Goldman and Deloitte have moved ahead, and your mother calls it exploring, but the freeze is real. This verse asks you to bear their good fortune without letting it drain you.
