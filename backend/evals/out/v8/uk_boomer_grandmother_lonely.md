## Day 1 [hook] Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 1.4.3
LINE: Alone, he found no joy and longed for another. So, said Yajnavalkya, each of us is like a half.
TRANSLATION: He found no delight; that is why one who is alone finds no delight. He longed for a second. He became as large as a man and woman embracing, and split himself in two; from that came husband and wife. So, said Yajnavalkya, each of us is like a half. That is why this space is filled by a wife.
MEANING: The seer Yajnavalkya speaks to the reader about the very first being. Alone in the beginning, this being felt no joy. It longed for a companion and split itself in two, becoming a husband and a wife. Each of us, says Yajnavalkya, is like a half. This is why even this space is filled by a wife.
FOR YOU: Your mornings with the Ramayana are full, but the hunger you feel is not for another story. It is for the one who reads. You are looking for the companion, not the book.

MEANING: Bhartrihari speaks to the reader from the section on wealth. He says that when a man is starving, a handful of grain is everything to him. Later, when he is full, he counts the whole earth as straw. He means that nothing is fixed, and a person's condition makes things look great or small. ## Day 3 [steady] Sursagar vinay pada
LINE: The Lord is the companion of exactly those who have no one.
TRANSLATION: The Lord is the companion of exactly those who have no one: kind to the lowly, supremely compassionate, Hari who takes many forms for the good of his people.
MEANING: Surdas speaks to the reader from a place of absolute certainty about whose side God is on. He says the Lord is the companion of exactly those who have no one. This is not a general promise of help. It is a specific statement about the condition of being alone. When a person has no one else, the Lord becomes that one. The text does not say the Lord replaces people. It says the Lord is the companion of the one who is alone. The reading does not end the loneliness. It meets it.
FOR YOU: The Ramayana is there, but the one who reads it is looking elsewhere. Let the book be your companion today, not your escape. Sit with it knowing that the search for company is a search for yourself.
