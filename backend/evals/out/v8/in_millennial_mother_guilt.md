## Day 1 [hook] Ramcharitmanas · Ayodhya Kand
LINE: Hold on, child. The times turned against us. Don't carry this loss as your shame.
TRANSLATION: My child, bless you, hold on to courage even now. Seeing that the times are bad, set aside your grief. Knowing that the course of time and karma cannot be turned, don't take this loss as your shame.
MEANING: Kausalya finds Bharat at the edge of collapse. He has just returned to Ayodhya to learn his father is dead and his brother exiled, and he is certain the fault is his. He expects blame, but his mother draws him close and tells him to hold on to courage. She says the times turned against them, and because the course of time cannot be turned, he must set aside his grief and not carry this loss as his shame.
FOR YOU: You are in the car, crying, and your daughter's question is still ringing. The shame you feel is not yours to keep. It is the weight of a world that believes a mother must be one thing or the other, and it is okay to feel the ache of being pulled apart.

TRANSLATION: The Vedas, scriptures and Puranas say, and the world knows, that the duty of service is hard. Duty to one's master and self-interest pull against each other; hatred is blind, and love has no judgement.
MEANING: Bharat speaks to his grandfather Janaka in the court of Ayodhya. Janaka asks him to judge a difficult dispute, but Bharat steps back. ## Day 3 [steady] Maha Upanishad 6.68
LINE: Be busy on the outside and unhurried inside. Act fully, but don't let it agitate you.
TRANSLATION: Outwardly busy, inwardly free of agitation; a doer on the outside, a non-doer within: move through the world with a clear mind.
MEANING: Ribhu teaches his student Nidagha about inner stillness. He says a free person is busy on the outside but unhurried inside. They act fully in the world yet remain calm within. They let go of what is lost, take up what comes, and move through life with a clear mind.
FOR YOU: You can be busy on the outside and unhurried inside. You can do the work at hand without letting it agitate you. Your mind can stay clear even when you are pulled in two directions.

## Day 4 [steady] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: Let them praise or blame, let money come or go: the steady don't step off the right path.
TRANSLATION: Let the worldly-wise blame or praise; let fortune come or go as she pleases; let death come today or ages hence: the steadfast do not move a step from the path of what is right.
MEANING: Bhartrihari speaks to the reader from his section on steadfastness. He knows the reader will face people who praise and blame, who will offer wealth or take it away, and who will not care about the reader's inner state. He says the steady person does not step off the right path. This means setbacks rarely last for those who keep going on the right path. The steady mind holds its ground when everything else is in motion.
FOR YOU: You can hold both your work and your children without letting the noise of either pull you off your path. You do not need to be fully present in both at the same time to be enough for both.
