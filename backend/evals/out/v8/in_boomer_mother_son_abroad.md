## Day 1 [hook] Bhartrihari · Vairagya Shatakam
LINE: In old age, family stops listening and even a son turns cold. Alas for the aged.
TRANSLATION: The body shrinks, the gait falters, the teeth fall out, sight fades, deafness grows, the mouth drools; relatives pay no heed to his words, his wife no longer looks after him; alas, the misery of an old man: even his son turns hostile.
MEANING: Bhartrihari speaks from the edge of old age, looking back at a body that is failing and a world that is turning away. The teeth are gone, the eyes are dim, and the people who once knew you are no longer listening. Even a son can turn cold. The poet does not say this to frighten you, but to name the loneliness that comes when the body fails and the family has moved on.
FOR YOU: You have spent a lifetime teaching children, and now the house is quiet. Your son is far away, and you know the waiting will not bring him back. This verse sees that exact stillness.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Valmiki Ramayana · Ayodhya Kanda
LINE: Grief wears away your steadiness and your judgement. There is no enemy like it.
TRANSLATION: Grief destroys steadiness, grief destroys learning, grief destroys everything. There is no enemy like grief.
MEANING: After lashing out at her husband over their son's exile, Kausalya apologises. She explains that grief wore away her steadiness and her judgement. She says there is no enemy like grief. She means that sorrow can make us wound those we love, and that apologising quickly heals more than being right. Her words turn gently toward the one who has been waiting.
FOR YOU: The waiting is not the wound. It is the love with nowhere to land. You see that your heart has no place to put what you feel. That emptiness is what hurts, not the years passing.

## Day 3 [steady] Valmiki Ramayana · Sundara Kanda
LINE: 'Out of sight, out of mind' is true only of the ungrateful. Real love survives distance.
TRANSLATION: Love lasts while someone is in sight; out of sight, affection fades. But that is the way of the ungrateful. Ram will not let it fade.
MEANING: Alone in captivity, Sita fears Ram has forgotten her. She reminds herself that out of sight is out of mind only for the ungrateful. Real love survives distance. She holds that Ram will not let her affection fade. She tells herself to hold on to this truth.
FOR YOU: You hold that what is real in you cannot be lost. Your love is not smaller because one heart is far. You keep your steadiness, even when the waiting hurts.

## Day 4 [steady] Mahabharata · Anushasana Parva · Anushasana
LINE: There is no teacher like a mother.
TRANSLATION: A teacher outweighs ten instructors, a father ten teachers, and a mother ten fathers, even the whole earth. There is no teacher equal to a mother.
MEANING: Yudhishthira asks Bhishma how an eldest brother should treat his younger brothers, and Bhishma answers from his bed of arrows. He tells him that a mother outweighs ten teachers, a father outweighs ten mothers, and even the whole earth is less than a mother. He says a mother is the truest teacher because she overlooks small faults, corrects gently, shares fairly, and does not let outsiders divide the family. The idea is that love that has nowhere to go can still rest in the one who gives it freely.
FOR YOU: You taught children for years, and you know that love outlasts distance. Your son is far away, but your care for him has not become smaller. Let your heart rest in that truth today, in the quiet of your home.

## Day 5 [act] Ramcharitmanas · Ayodhya Kand
LINE: I won't hold you back with false affection. Go, son. Just don't forget your mother.
TRANSLATION: Thinking this over, I will not insist out of false affection. I bless you, son; only keep the bond of a mother, and don't forget me.
MEANING: Kausalya knows that holding Ram back would be false affection. She tells him to go, and she blesses him. She asks only that he not forget her. This is love letting go, and it is the hardest kind of love. It gives the waiting a shape, and it names the bond for what it is.
FOR YOU: try writing your son a letter. You do not need to send it. Just give your waiting a form on the page, and name the love for what it is: a bond that distance cannot break.

## Day 6 [act] Yoga Vasishtha, Vairagya 11.37
LINE: Say freely what is on your heart, blameless one. These sorrows need not keep wounding you.
TRANSLATION: Say quickly whatever you wish, blameless one, and you will have it, by which these sorrows will never pierce you again.
MEANING: Vishvamitra sees the withdrawn Rama and speaks to him gently. He tells him to say freely whatever is on his heart. By speaking it out, Rama can have it, and these sorrows will no longer pierce him. The idea is that grief kept inside wounds, while naming it aloud can set it down.
FOR YOU: The waiting will not stop. But your heart needs a place to put the love that has no one to land on. Find one quiet moment today to speak the love you feel, without expecting an answer. Let the words rest there.
