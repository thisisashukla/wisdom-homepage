## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 6.34
LINE: The mind is restless, stormy, stubborn and strong. Holding it feels like holding the wind.
TRANSLATION: For the mind is restless, Krishna, turbulent, powerful and stubborn. I think it is as hard to hold as the wind.
MEANING: Arjuna tells Krishna that his mind is restless, turbulent and stubborn, as hard to hold as the wind. Krishna does not argue. He agrees, and says that practice and letting go are what train it. The mind is hard to hold, but it can be steadied.
FOR YOU: You are preparing for the next meeting, and your mind feels like a storm. It is not a flaw. It is a mind that has not yet learned to rest. You do not need to stop the wind. You only need to learn how to stand in it.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Mahabharata · Vana Parva · Vana: Draupadi and Yudhishthira
LINE: Never look down on yourself. Nothing good grows from self-contempt.
TRANSLATION: A person should never look down on himself. The one who despises himself never finds real prosperity.
MEANING: Draupadi tells Yudhishthira that a person should never look down on themselves. The one who despises themselves never finds real prosperity. She says that effort still matters, and that fate rewards no one who waits. Do right for its own sake, then work as if it all depends on you.
FOR YOU: You are preparing for your meetings the way you do because you despise the idea of being found out. That self-contempt is what makes you sick. It is not the work that is hurting you. It is the belief that you do not deserve the place you have reached.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: A ball knocked down bounces back up. For good people, setbacks rarely last.
TRANSLATION: A ball struck down by the hand flies right back up. As a rule, the misfortunes of those who live rightly do not last.
MEANING: Bhartrihari says that a ball knocked down by the hand flies right back up. As a rule, the misfortunes of those who live rightly do not last. He is speaking to the reader, in the section on steadfastness, after describing how the gods kept churning the ocean past jewels and poison until they found nectar. The steady do not leave the right path whatever people say. A struck ball bounces back and a cut tree grows again.
FOR YOU: The promotion is a ball knocked down. It is not you. It is a thing that happened, and it will fly back up. Hold that fact against the voice that says you are an imposter, and let your body feel the difference.

## Day 4 [steady] Bhagavad Gita 2.57
LINE: Good news doesn't inflate them, bad news doesn't embitter them. That's steady wisdom.
TRANSLATION: One who is without attachment anywhere, who neither rejoices on meeting good nor hates on meeting bad, has wisdom that is firmly set.
MEANING: Krishna answers Arjuna's question about what a person of steady mind looks like. He describes someone without attachment, who neither rejoices on meeting good nor hates on meeting bad, and whose wisdom is firmly set. This steadiness is built by guarding attention, because anger starts with what you dwell on.
FOR YOU: The promotion is good news, but it does not have to inflate you. The criticism you take to heart is bad news, but it does not have to embitter you. You can hold your part in this work without letting either the praise or the fear take over your attention.

## Day 5 [act] Mahabharata · Udyoga Parva · Vidula's counsel
LINE: Don't think less of yourself or settle for little. Set your mind on something good, and stop fearing.
TRANSLATION: Do not hold yourself in contempt, and do not keep it going on too little. Set your mind on what is truly good; don't be afraid. Pull yourself together.
MEANING: Queen Vidula finds her son Sanjaya lying defeated after losing his kingdom. She tells him to stop thinking less of himself and to set his mind on what is truly good. She says he should not be afraid. The idea is that past failure does not define a person. Results are uncertain, but those who never try never succeed. Vidula wakes him up so he can fight for his kingdom again.
FOR YOU: Your father drove a taxi, and you have been taught to work hard for every step up. That is a good lesson. But right now, the preparation is making you sick. Stop thinking less of yourself. You earned the promotion. Set your mind on the work itself. Stop fearing the meeting.

## Day 6 [act] Bhagavad Gita 4.34
LINE: Learn from those who have seen the truth: approach with humility, ask real questions, and serve.
TRANSLATION: Learn it by bowing down, by questioning, and by service. Those who are wise and have seen the truth will teach you that knowledge.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna on the battlefield. Arjuna is frozen by doubt and guilt. Krishna tells him to cut through this confusion by learning from those who have seen the truth. He says to approach them with humility, ask real questions, and serve. This knowledge is powerful enough to carry even the worst wrongdoer across. The idea is simple: trust the process of learning, ask honestly, and stop trying to solve everything alone.
FOR YOU: You are preparing for your next meeting by yourself. Let someone who has been through this sit with you. Ask them what they actually did. You do not have to carry the whole weight of this alone.
