## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 18.26
LINE: Free of clinging and boasting, full of resolve and enthusiasm, unchanged by success or failure: the best kind of doer.
TRANSLATION: The doer who is free from attachment, does not speak of himself, is full of steadiness and enthusiasm, and unmoved by success or failure, is called sattvic.
MEANING: Krishna is sorting out the different ways people act and feel. He says the best kind of person is the one who acts with full resolve and energy but does not cling to the outcome or boast about it. This person is steady and feels the same whether they succeed or fail.
FOR YOU: You have just signed your first paying customer, and the high of that is real. This verse asks you to let that win stay in your pocket. Keep the same steady energy tomorrow morning when the inbox is empty and the work is just hard.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: Let them praise or blame, let money come or go: the steady don't step off the right path.
TRANSLATION: Let the worldly-wise blame or praise; let fortune come or go as she pleases; let death come today or ages hence: the steadfast do not move a step from the path of what is right.
MEANING: Bhartrihari speaks to the reader from a place of steady resolve. The world will praise you and it will blame you. Money will appear and it will vanish. Death might come today or not for years. The steadfast person does not move a step from the path of what is right, no matter what happens.
FOR YOU: Tomorrow the praise for your first customer might feel distant, and the quiet work ahead will test your resolve. Hold the steady path, not because the result is guaranteed, but because the effort itself is yours to own.

## Day 4 [steady] Mahabharata · Vana Parva · Vana: Draupadi and Yudhishthira
LINE: Don't despair because effort hasn't paid off yet. Every attempt can go either way.
TRANSLATION: 'I keep working and still don't succeed' is no reason to despair. Every effort can end two ways, in success or not; only by not acting is the outcome certain.
MEANING: Draupadi speaks to Yudhishthira in the forest, where waiting on fate feels like giving up. She argues that effort must continue. The outcome of any attempt can go two ways, toward success or not. The only certain failure is to stop acting. The steadfast do their part, then let the result go.
FOR YOU: When the quiet months stretch and the work feels less exciting, you will face the choice of waiting or pushing. Do the work as if it all depends on you, then release the outcome to time.

## Day 5 [act] Mahabharata · Udyoga Parva · Vidura Niti
LINE: Decide, then begin. Don't stall halfway, don't waste time, and keep yourself steady.
TRANSLATION: One who begins only after deciding, does not stop halfway through the work, does not waste time and keeps himself in hand, is called wise.
MEANING: The sleepless King Dhritarashtra calls his half-brother Vidura for counsel on the eve of the peace talks. Vidura tells him that a person who decides calmly, then begins without stopping halfway, wastes no time, and keeps themselves steady, is called wise. The idea is to decide, then act steadily and fully, without letting your mind scatter or your hands slow down halfway through.
FOR YOU: You have decided to build this, and the first customer signed. Now let that decision carry you through the next fourteen months. Set a single small task for today, and do not stop halfway. Let your work be the steady thing that holds you, not the mood of the day.

## Day 6 [act] Bhagavad Gita 2.47
LINE: Your work is yours to do; the results are not yours to hold. Don't let that make you stop.
TRANSLATION: Your right is to the work alone, never to its fruits. Do not let the fruit be your motive, and do not cling to doing nothing.
MEANING: Krishna tells Arjuna that his right is to the work alone, never to its fruits. He should not let the outcome be his motive, and he must not cling to doing nothing out of fear. The teaching is to act fully and give up owning the result, staying even whether things succeed or fail.
FOR YOU: The work is yours to do, and the result is not yours to hold. Today, focus only on the one task in front of you, the next step of the build. Let the outcome go, and keep your hands steady on the work itself.

## Day 7 [story] Hanuman's leap
LINE: Halfway across the ocean, a golden mountain rose out of the water and offered him a place to rest.
FOR YOU: The year ahead will test your steadiness when the daily work feels ordinary. Hanuman crossed the ocean without sinking by keeping his focus on the far shore, not on the water beneath him. When the excitement of the first customer fades into the quiet of building, let that same focus carry you.
