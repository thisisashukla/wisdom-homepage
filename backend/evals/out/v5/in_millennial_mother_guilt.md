## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 18.47
LINE: Better your own path, imperfectly walked, than someone else's done perfectly.
TRANSLATION: Better is one's own duty, though imperfect, than the duty of another well performed. Doing the work born of one's own nature, one incurs no wrong.
MEANING: Krishna is telling Arjuna that a person should stay in their own work, even if it is messy or flawed. He says that doing your own duty, however imperfectly, is better than taking on someone else's path and doing it perfectly. It is about finding your own honest fit with the world, not about following a script written by others. When you do the work that is truly yours, you do not carry the weight of someone else's expectations.
FOR YOU: You are trying to be a perfect mother and a perfect manager at the same time. The guilt comes from holding a standard that belongs to no one but you. Your work is your own, and your care for your daughter is your own.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Bhartrihari · Vairagya Shatakam
LINE: Why exhaust yourself daily winning others' approval? Settle inside, and your own clarity grants every wish.
TRANSLATION: O heart, why do you enter into weariness, day after day trying to please the minds of others in so many ways, to win their favour? When you are at peace within, your own clear resolve rises by itself like a cluster of wish-granting gems: what wish of yours would it not fulfil?
MEANING: Bhartrihari speaks to his own heart, asking why it exhausts itself daily trying to please others and win their favour. He says that when you settle inside and find your own peace, your mind becomes clear. That inner clarity naturally grants every wish, because it is no longer scattered by the need for outside approval.
FOR YOU: Yesterday you named the split between your two roles. Today you can see that the real weight is not the work itself. It is the waiting for your daughter to tell you that you did enough. Your own clarity is already there, waiting for you to notice it.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: Let them praise or blame, let money come or go: the steady don't step off the right path.
TRANSLATION: Let the worldly-wise blame or praise; let fortune come or go as she pleases; let death come today or ages hence: the steadfast do not move a step from the path of what is right.
MEANING: Bhartrihari is telling the reader that the steady person does not step off the right path. He says let them praise or blame, let money come or go. The steadfast do not move a step from what is right. The idea is that setbacks are temporary. The steady person keeps going on the right path no matter what people say.
FOR YOU: Your daughter's question and your own guilt are just noise. Your presence is not measured by your attendance. You can be a good mother and a good manager. The right path is your own. Hold to it, and the noise will not move you a step.

## Day 4 [steady] Bhagavad Gita 12.19
LINE: Praise and blame weigh the same. Quiet, content with what comes, rooted in steadiness, not status.
TRANSLATION: to whom blame and praise are equal, who is silent, content with whatever comes, without a fixed home, steady in mind and full of devotion, that person is dear to me.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna on the battlefield, describing the one who is dear to God. He says that for this person, praise and blame weigh exactly the same. They remain quiet and content with whatever comes. They do not cling to a fixed home or status, but stay steady in mind and full of devotion.
FOR YOU: Your daughter's question lands like blame, but your worth as a mother is not a project to be approved. Hold your presence as enough, just as it is, without needing her praise to balance the scales.

## Day 5 [act] Mahabharata · Anushasana Parva · Anushasana
LINE: Start the day by greeting your mother and father. Small daily respect adds up to a good life.
TRANSLATION: Don't sleep past sunrise. On rising, first greet your mother and father, and your teacher or another elder; that is how a long life is won.
MEANING: Bhishma is answering Yudhishthira. He has been asked how a person lives well for a long time. Bhishma says it is not one great act. It is small daily conduct. On rising, first greet your mother and father. Greet a teacher or an elder. That is how a long life is won. It is a life built from daily respect and kind words. A good morning is the foundation of a good day.
FOR YOU: When you wake tomorrow, before you reach for your phone, look at your daughter. Let her see your eyes fully. Give her that first moment. Let the rest of the day wait.

## Day 6 [act] Avadhuta Gita 8.7
LINE: Let go of old cravings, speak only what heals, and live in the present.
TRANSLATION: One who has given up old cravings, whose speech is wholesome, and who lives in the present: that is the meaning of 'va'.
MEANING: Dattatreya speaks to the seeker, explaining the syllable 'va' in the word Avadhuta. He says it means one who has given up old cravings. Their speech is wholesome, and they live entirely in the present moment.
FOR YOU: Let go of the craving for her approval and speak only what heals. Give her one full moment of your attention today and let the rest of the day go.
