## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 1.29–30
LINE: Shaking limbs, a dry mouth, a racing body: the Gita opens with a panic attack, not a hero.
TRANSLATION: My limbs give way, my mouth is parched, my body trembles and my hair stands on end.
MEANING: Arjuna, on the edge of battle, sees his own kin arrayed against him and collapses. His limbs shake, his mouth dries, his body trembles, his hair stands on end. He drops his bow and sinks into the chariot. This is how the Gita begins, not with a hero, but with a man breaking down under the weight of what he sees. The teaching starts here, in the breakdown, with the truth that even the strongest can freeze when the ground gives way.
FOR YOU: When your body wakes as if the office still owns your time, let it tremble. The verdict from that ten-minute call is not the measure of the twenty-two years you gave. Your body is still living the shock, and it has not caught up to the truth that you no longer belong to that place.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Maitri Upanishad 6.34
LINE: The mind alone binds or frees us: hooked on things, it binds; free of them, it liberates.
TRANSLATION: The mind alone is the cause of bondage and of liberation for human beings: attached to objects, it binds; free of objects, it liberates.
MEANING: Shakayanya, a sage, tells King Brihadratha that the mind alone is the cause of our bondage and our liberation. When the mind is attached to things, it binds us. When it is free of them, it liberates us. The mind is the world we live in, and we become what it dwells on. The verse proposes that the grip you feel is not just the job loss itself, but the mind's attachment to the identity that job gave you. It is the mind that keeps you bound to the fear of what might happen next.
FOR YOU: The mind keeps replaying the call, the ten minutes, the shame. It is not the call that traps you now, but the mind's grip on it. The work you did for twenty-two years is still there. The mind can let go of the call and hold onto the work instead.
