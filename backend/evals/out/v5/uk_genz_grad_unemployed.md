## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 18.48
LINE: Don't abandon work that suits you just because it has flaws. Every undertaking has some, like fire has smoke.
TRANSLATION: One should not give up the work suited to one's nature, Kaunteya, even if it has faults; for all undertakings are clouded by faults, as fire is by smoke.
MEANING: Krishna speaks to Arjuna on the battlefield, telling him to stay with the work that fits his nature rather than abandoning it for something that seems perfect. He compares every task to fire, which always has smoke. The smoke does not mean the fire is useless, it just means the work will never be flawless. The point is to do your own work as an offering, accepting its imperfections as part of the path.
FOR YOU: You are making flat whites while your friends are at top firms, and that gap feels like a verdict on you. This verse says the work itself is not the problem. The smoke is not a reason to put out the fire. Your job fits you, flaws and all.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Ramcharitmanas · Uttar Kand
LINE: Envy is an itch. Burning at another's happiness is a wasting disease of the mind.
TRANSLATION: Possessiveness is ringworm and envy is the itch; the swings of elation and dejection are throat ailments. The burning you feel at another's happiness is consumption, and malice and a crooked mind are leprosy.
MEANING: Kakbhushundi, the talking parrot, is describing the illnesses of the mind to Garuda, the king of birds. He says envy is an itch and the burning you feel at another person's happiness is a wasting disease. The point is to know these conditions by name so you can see them clearly instead of being controlled by them.
FOR YOU: Yesterday's verse named the grip of the job itself. Today you see the pattern underneath it. The freeze is not about the flat whites. It is the itch of envy, the burning at a friend's success, that makes you hide from LinkedIn. Naming it is the first step away from its power.
