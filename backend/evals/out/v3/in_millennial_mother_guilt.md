## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 2.7
LINE: I'm confused about what's right. Tell me plainly what's best; I'm ready to learn.
TRANSLATION: My nature is stricken by weakness and my mind is confused about what is right. I ask you: tell me for certain what is best for me. I am your student; I have come to you, teach me.
MEANING: Arjuna has laid down his weapons on the battlefield. He is weeping and trembling, unable to tell right from wrong. He tells Krishna that his nature is overcome by weakness and his mind is spinning. He asks Krishna to tell him plainly what is best, because he is ready to learn. He says he is Krishna's student and has come to him to be taught.
FOR YOU: In the car, after your daughter asked why you were not there, you felt the same confusion. You are not failing at your job or at being her mother. You are simply admitting that you do not know how to hold both, and that is the first honest step toward standing whole in both.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Mahabharata · Shanti Parva · Shanti: Consolation of Yudhishthira
LINE: Mental pain comes from two places: something you didn't want happened, or your mind is spinning. No third.
TRANSLATION: Sorrow of the mind has only two sources: a confused, wandering mind, or something unwanted happening to you. There is no third.
MEANING: The sage Ashma tells King Janaka that the mind's pain comes from only two sources: something unwanted has happened, or the mind itself is wandering in confusion. There is no third cause. The verse says that when a day is gone, we grieve what we lost, but then we steady ourselves with the people still standing before us.
FOR YOU: Your daughter asking why you were not at her school event is something unwanted happening. That grief is real. But the second source, the spinning mind, is where the guilt lives. Let the grief pass through you, then turn your attention back to the people still here.

## Day 3 [steady] Ashtavakra Gita 18.81
LINE: Not begging for gain, not grieving loss: a steady mind stays cool and full.
TRANSLATION: He does not pray for gain, nor grieve over loss. The steady one's cool mind is filled as if with nectar.
MEANING: In the closing chapter on peace, the sage Ashtavakra describes the mind of the steady one. He does not beg for gain, nor does he grieve over loss. The cool mind of the wise is filled as if with nectar. Calm is not found by chasing it or changing places. It is how you hold ordinary life.
FOR YOU: You can be loyal to both your work and your children without being split. Your mind can hold both. You do not have to beg for presence or grieve over absence. You are allowed to hold both duties and remain full inside.

## Day 4 [steady] Bhagavad Gita 4.22
LINE: Content with what comes, beyond envy, steady in success and failure, you stay free even while working.
TRANSLATION: Content with whatever comes unasked, beyond the pairs of opposites, free from envy, the same in success and failure, even when acting he is not bound.
MEANING: Krishna is telling Arjuna how the wise move through the world. He says they are content with what arrives unasked, free from envy, and steady whether they win or lose. Because they are not attached to gain or afraid of loss, they are not bound by their work, even when they are doing it with full force.
FOR YOU: You are holding the guilt of choosing. The texts say you can be fully present in your role, whether that is the office or the kitchen, without being split. Let the other part rest for now.

## Day 6 [act] Bhagavad Gita 2.47
LINE: Your work is yours to do; the results are not yours to hold. Don't let that make you stop.
