## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 3.35
LINE: Better your own path done imperfectly than someone else's done perfectly. Borrowed lives are dangerous.
TRANSLATION: Better is one's own duty, even done imperfectly, than the duty of another well done. Better is death in one's own duty; the duty of another is full of fear.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna on the battlefield, telling him that living by someone else's rules is full of fear, while dying in your own truth is better. The verse says your own path, walked imperfectly, is safer than another's done perfectly. It is not about staying in a family role. It is about finding the way that belongs to you and walking it, even if you stumble.
FOR YOU: The silence your parents keep is a borrowed life they are trying to force on you, and it is dangerous. You do not have to pretend to be a guest in your own home. Stay in your own truth, even if the path feels shaky right now.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Ramcharitmanas · Bal Kand
LINE: Most of us swell only at our own rise. Rare is the one who surges at another's.
TRANSLATION: Brother, most people in the world are like ponds and rivers: they swell only when their own waters rise. Rare is the good soul who, like the ocean, surges with joy on seeing another's moon grow full.
MEANING: Tulsidas is writing his poem, and he knows it is plain. He worries that readers will not like it. He tells them that most people in the world are like ponds and rivers. They swell only when their own waters rise. It is rare for someone to surge with joy at another person's moon growing full. He is asking you to notice if you can be glad at someone else's good work, not only your own.
FOR YOU: Your parents are like a river that only swells when their own waters rise. Their silence is their smallness, not your failing. You are not a guest at your own table. You are the son who is still standing in the house you built with them.

## Day 4 [steady] Mahabharata · Udyoga Parva · Vidula's counsel
LINE: Don't think less of yourself or settle for little. Set your mind on something good, and stop fearing.
TRANSLATION: Do not hold yourself in contempt, and do not keep it going on too little. Set your mind on what is truly good; don't be afraid. Pull yourself together.
MEANING: Queen Vidula finds her son Sanjaya lying defeated after losing his kingdom. She tells him not to hold himself in contempt or settle for little. FOR YOU: Your parents are treating you as a guest now, but that is their picture of you, not your reality. The home has not changed. You are still their child. Let their silence be theirs to keep. Hold on to your own truth about who you are, and let that be your ground.

TRANSLATION: Always doing the work that comes, seeing friend and foe alike, free of wanting and not-wanting, such a one neither grieves nor craves. Saying what is agreeable to all, speaking gently when asked, knowing the intent of others' hearts, such a one does not sink in the world.
MEANING: Ribhu tells Nidagha how a free person moves through the world. He says to do the work in front of you, speak gently, and read people's hearts, and you will not sink in the world. ## Day 6 [act] Bhagavad Gita 17.15
LINE: Speak words that don't wound, that are true, kind and helpful. That is discipline of speech.
TRANSLATION: Speech that causes no distress, that is truthful, pleasant and beneficial, and the regular study of sacred texts: this is called austerity of speech.
MEANING: Krishna is on the nature of discipline to Arjuna. He says that true austerity of speech means words that do not wound, that are true, kind and helpful. It is not about keeping quiet or saying what people want to hear. It is about speaking from a clear mind, where the truth is spoken without cruelty, and where the speaker has nothing to prove or gain. The speech comes from a place of giving, not taking.
FOR YOU: Speak the true thing gently and firmly, hold your ground on what is yours, and do your own duty without shrinking to fit the picture they hold.
