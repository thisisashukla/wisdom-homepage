## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 6.34
LINE: The mind is restless, stormy, stubborn and strong. Holding it feels like holding the wind.
TRANSLATION: For the mind is restless, Krishna, turbulent, powerful and stubborn. I think it is as hard to hold as the wind.
MEANING: Arjuna, standing on the battlefield, tells Krishna that the mind is restless and stubborn. He feels it is as hard to hold as the wind. Krishna does not tell him to stop the wind. He says that practice and letting go train the mind. The mind is hard to hold, but it can be trained.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Mahabharata · Vana Parva · Vana: Draupadi and Yudhishthira
LINE: Never look down on yourself. Nothing good grows from self-contempt.
TRANSLATION: A person should never look down on himself. The one who despises himself never finds real prosperity.
MEANING: Draupadi is speaking to Yudhishthira while they are in exile, and she is angry that he is waiting for fate to fix things. She tells him that no good comes from despising yourself. She says that if you look down on your own worth, you will never find real prosperity. The point is to do what is right for its own sake, and then to work with all your effort as if everything depends on you.
FOR YOU: You are holding a promotion that was given to you, yet you are telling yourself you do not deserve it. Let the work itself be the answer. Do the next right thing for its own sake, and put your full effort into it without waiting for the fear to disappear.

## Day 1 · Listen (script)
[scene] Arjuna stands before Krishna on the battlefield. He has been listening to Krishna's teachings on discipline and focus. But now he confesses his own inability to control his own mind. He tells Krishna that the mind is restless, turbulent, and powerful. Arjuna said:
[sanskrit] चञ्चलं हि मनः कृष्ण प्रमाथि बलवद्दृढम्, तस्याहं निग्रहं मन्ये वायोरिव सुदुष्करम्.
[line] In plain words: The mind is restless, stormy, stubborn and strong. Holding it feels like holding the wind.
[unpack] Krishna does not tell Arjuna to fight harder or to force his mind into submission. He simply acknowledges that the mind is naturally restless and stubborn. The way to work with it is through practice and letting go. You cannot brute force your thoughts into silence. You train the mind by returning to your work, by letting the anxious story run its course, and by not giving it the final word. This is easier said than done, because every time you prepare for a meeting, the story of being a fraud is already there, waiting.
[for_you] Today, as you prepare for your next presentation, notice the moment that story about being a fraud starts. Do not try to push it away or prove it wrong. Just let it sit there while you do your work. The preparation is real, but the story is not. Your part is to keep showing up.
[close] The wind moves through you, but you are not the wind.
