## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 2.67
LINE: When the mind chases a wandering sense, it is swept off like a boat in the wind.
TRANSLATION: When the mind runs after the roving senses, it carries away one's understanding, as the wind carries a boat away on the water.
MEANING: Krishna is answering Arjuna's question. He asks what a person of steady mind looks like. Krishna says that when the mind chases a wandering sense, it is swept off like a boat in the wind. He means that a steady mind is built by guarding your attention. When you let your mind drift toward things that disturb you, it carries your understanding away. That drifting is where anger and regret begin.
FOR YOU: You are trying to be two people at once. Your mind drifts toward the school event your daughter asked about. Let it drift. Notice it without pulling yourself out of the present hour. Your attention is the rudder right now.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: Let them praise or blame, let money come or go: the steady don't step off the right path.
TRANSLATION: Let the worldly-wise blame or praise; let fortune come or go as she pleases; let death come today or ages hence: the steadfast do not move a step from the path of what is right.
MEANING: Bhartrihari writes that a steady person does not move from the path of what is right. He says let others praise or blame. Let money come or go. Let death come today or ages hence. The steadfast person does not step off the path. Setbacks rarely last for those who keep going on the right path. He compares this to the gods who kept churning the ocean. They did not stop when they found jewels or poison. They kept going until they found nectar. A struck ball bounces back. A cut tree grows again.
FOR YOU: You are at the office, and your mind is at home. Your daughter asked why you were not at her school event. You feel you are failing at both. But your duty right now is to do what is in front of you, fully.

## Day 4 [steady] Bhagavad Gita 5.20
LINE: Don't get carried away by good news or crushed by bad. Keep your understanding steady.
TRANSLATION: One should not rejoice on getting what is pleasant nor be troubled on getting what is unpleasant. Steady in understanding and undeluded, the knower of Brahman abides in Brahman.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna on the battlefield, trying to steady his mind. He tells him that a person who truly knows what is real does not leap for joy when good things happen, nor does they sink when bad things arrive. Their understanding stays level and clear. The joy that comes from outside always ends, but the joy that is already inside does not. The verse asks you to rest in that inner steadiness, to let the rising and falling of praise and blame pass through you without carrying you away.
FOR YOU: When your daughter's words land hard, or a work task goes well, let your heart feel the moment without letting it tip you over. You can hold your own steady ground while the day shifts around you.

LINE: Your work is yours to do; the results are not yours to hold. Don't let that make you stop.
TRANSLATION: Your right is to the work alone, never to its fruits. Do not let the fruit be your motive, and do not cling to doing nothing.
MEANING: Krishna speaks again to Arjuna, this time about how to move through the world without being chained.
