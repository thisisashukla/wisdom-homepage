## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 18.47
LINE: Better your own path, imperfectly walked, than someone else's done perfectly.
TRANSLATION: Better is one's own duty, though imperfect, than the duty of another well performed. Doing the work born of one's own nature, one incurs no wrong.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna on the battlefield. Arjuna is frozen by doubt about what to do. Krishna tells him to focus on the work that matches his own nature. The work might have flaws. But doing your own duty, even if you do it poorly, is better than doing someone else's duty perfectly. The point is that you do not incur fault by doing what is truly yours.
FOR YOU: Your mother tells aunties you are exploring options. The shame of that feels heavy. But your own path, even in a café, is yours alone. Do not measure its worth against their desks at Goldman or Deloitte.

## Day 6 [act] Mahabharata · Shanti Parva · Bhishma, Rajadharma
LINE: Make peace with other people's success, even when you lack it. The wise can enjoy good fortune that isn't theirs.
TRANSLATION: Bear the good fortune of others, even though it is not with you. Wise people know how to enjoy good fortune even when it belongs to someone else; good fortune drains away from the one who resents.
MEANING: Kalakavrikshiya speaks to the deposed prince Kshemadarshi. The prince has lost his kingdom and wealth and is drowning in grief and envy.
