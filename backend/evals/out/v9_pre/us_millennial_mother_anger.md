LINE: Shaking limbs, a dry mouth, a racing body: the Gita opens with a panic attack, not a hero.
TRANSLATION: My limbs give way, my mouth is parched, my body trembles and my hair stands on end.
MEANING: Arjuna stands in his chariot between two armies. He looks at the faces before him and sees his own kin. His hands shake. His mouth goes dry. His body trembles and his hair stands on end. ## Day 2 [diagnose] Bhagavad Gita 2.62
LINE: Dwell on something and you get attached; attachment becomes wanting, and blocked wanting turns into anger.
TRANSLATION: When a person dwells on objects, attachment to them is born. From attachment comes desire, and from desire, anger.
MEANING: Krishna is answering Arjuna's question about what a steady person looks like. He explains that when you fix your thoughts on something, you start to feel attached. That attachment grows into a deep wanting. When that wanting is blocked, it turns into anger. The verse says anger does not come from nowhere. It starts with where your mind has been dwelling.
FOR YOU: The anger that arrives at your child's face and the shame that follows are two movements of the same moment. The fear that you are becoming her is the verdict you pass on yourself after the fact. See the pattern: the anger takes over, and then your mind judges you for it.

LINE: Don't answer anger with anger. The one who stays calm protects both people, and heals both.
TRANSLATION: One who does not get angry back at an angry person saves both himself and the other from great danger. He becomes the healer of both.
MEANING: In the forest, Draupadi is furious at the injustice done to her family and tells Yudhishthira to answer force with force. ## Day 6 [act] Mahabharata · Shanti Parva · Bhishma: Mokshadharma
LINE: Think it over and decide slowly, and you'll spend far less time in regret.
TRANSLATION: So, in every matter, a person should think it over and decide slowly; then he does not suffer regret for long.
MEANING: Bhishma tells Yudhishthira this while they are in the forest. He speaks of a sage named Gautama who orders his son Chirakari to kill his mother in a jealous rage, then storms off. Chirakari, known for his slowness, thinks so carefully about a father's command and a mother's life that he delays the act. Gautama returns in regret, and they are all saved. Bhishma says that when anger rises, or when someone you love seems to have wronged you, you could think it over and decide slowly.
FOR YOU: You are the witness of the anger, not the anger; the fire rises in the mind, but the one who sees it is untouched.
