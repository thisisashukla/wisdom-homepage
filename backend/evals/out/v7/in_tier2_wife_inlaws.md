## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 18.47
LINE: Better your own path, imperfectly walked, than someone else's done perfectly.
TRANSLATION: Better is one's own duty, though imperfect, than the duty of another well performed. Doing the work born of one's own nature, one incurs no wrong.
MEANING: Arjuna stands on the battlefield, paralyzed between what he is and what he is told he must be. Krishna tells him that it is better to follow the path that is truly his own, even if it is walked imperfectly, than to take on another's duty and perform it flawlessly. The instruction is to do the work that fits your own nature as an offering, because every other path carries its own flaws that you cannot escape.
FOR YOU: Your CA practice was built from nothing by your own effort. It is your own path, walked imperfectly but truly. Let that work hold its own worth today, even when others try to redraw it for you.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Ramcharitmanas · Sundar Kand
LINE: When advisers, doctors and teachers only say what pleases you, the ruin comes quickly.
TRANSLATION: When a minister, a doctor and a teacher speak only to please, out of fear or hope of reward, then the kingdom, the body and righteousness are soon destroyed.
MEANING: As the poet Tulsidas watches Ravana's court fill with sycophants, he warns that when a circle of advisers, doctors and teachers speaks only to please, the ruin comes quickly. The ministers flatter Ravana because they fear his anger or hope for reward, and in doing so they destroy the kingdom. The warning is that a voice that only echoes what you want to hear is not a guide. It is a trap that leads to ruin.
FOR YOU: When others see only a role, their picture is their smallness and not your measure; the harm deepens only when their picture begins to replace your own.

## Day 4 [steady] Katharudra Upanishad 8
LINE: Don't wear out your body chasing pleasure or fleeing pain. Praised, don't gloat; blamed, don't curse others.
TRANSLATION: Let him not torment the body too much for the sake of pleasure or pain. Praised, let him not gloat; blamed, let him not curse others.
MEANING: A teacher is laying down the ground rules for a person who has chosen to leave the world behind. The teacher tells them to stop punishing the body in the chase for joy or the flight from hurt. When someone offers praise, do not swell with pride. When someone offers blame, do not throw it back at them. The idea is to keep the inner self steady, not to ride the high of approval or the low of insult. This steadiness is what protects the life you are building from the noise around it.
FOR YOU: What you are cannot be cut smaller by what they see; your worth was never theirs to grant, and standing in your own truth is itself the duty.

## Day 5 [act] Mahabharata · Udyoga Parva · Vidula's counsel
LINE: Don't think less of yourself or settle for little. Set your mind on something good, and stop fearing.
TRANSLATION: Do not hold yourself in contempt, and do not keep it going on too little. Set your mind on what is truly good; don't be afraid. Pull yourself together.
MEANING: Queen Vidula finds her son Sanjaya lying on the ground, defeated after losing his kingdom. She tells him not to hold himself in contempt or settle for a small life. She urges him to set his mind on what is truly good and to stop fearing. The past failure does not have to be the final word. The future is uncertain, but those who never rise again simply never win.
FOR YOU: The CA practice you built is your kingdom. The silence beside you does not erase what you made. Name one thing you will not give up today, and let that be the first move back.

## Day 6 [act] Maha Upanishad 6.64-65
LINE: Do the work in front of you, speak gently, read people's hearts, and you won't sink in the world.
TRANSLATION: Always doing the work that comes, seeing friend and foe alike, free of wanting and not-wanting, such a one neither grieves nor craves. Saying what is agreeable to all, speaking gently when asked, knowing the intent of others' hearts, such a one does not sink in the world.
MEANING: Ribhu tells Nidagha that a free person moves through the world by doing the work in front of them and speaking gently. They see everyone with the same eye, free from craving and aversion. They know what is in another person's heart before the words come out. This is how someone stays afloat without sinking into the world's troubles.
FOR YOU: Speak the true thing gently and firmly, hold your ground on what is yours, and do your own duty without shrinking to fit the picture they hold.
