## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 3.35
LINE: Better your own path done imperfectly than someone else's done perfectly. Borrowed lives are dangerous.
TRANSLATION: Better is one's own duty, even done imperfectly, than the duty of another well done. Better is death in one's own duty; the duty of another is full of fear.
MEANING: Krishna tells Arjuna that it is better to follow your own path, even if you stumble, than to wear another person's life like a borrowed coat. He says that acting against your own nature brings fear, while acting in your own way, even imperfectly, keeps you whole. The choice is not between being perfect and being wrong, but between being true to yourself and living someone else's script.
FOR YOU: The silence from your partner and the pressure from your in-laws are trying to hand you a smaller life than the one you built. This verse says that walking your own path, even with stumbles, is safer than stepping into a role that was never yours.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Bhagavad Gita 18.16
LINE: If you think you alone made it happen, or alone made it fail, you're not seeing the whole picture.
TRANSLATION: That being so, whoever, through an untrained understanding, sees himself alone as the doer does not truly see.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna on the battlefield. After explaining that every action has five causes, Krishna points to the danger of thinking you are the only one responsible. If you believe you alone made something happen or alone caused it to fail, you are not seeing the whole picture.
FOR YOU: When others see only a role, their picture is their smallness and not your measure; the harm deepens only when their picture begins to replace your own.

## Day 5 [act] Bhagavad Gita 17.15
LINE: Speak words that don't wound, that are true, kind and helpful. That is discipline of speech.
TRANSLATION: Speech that causes no distress, that is truthful, pleasant and beneficial, and the regular study of sacred texts: this is called austerity of speech.
MEANING: Krishna speaks to Arjuna about the discipline of speech, telling them to use words that are true, kind, and helpful. This means speaking without causing distress, offering the truth gently without cruelty. It is about giving freely without expecting anything in return and letting go of performing for others. The idea is that speech should heal and clarify, not wound or flatter, and that true giving is done quietly without strings attached.
FOR YOU: Speak the true thing gently and firmly, hold your ground on what is yours, and do your own duty without shrinking to fit the picture they hold.

## Day 6 [act] Valmiki Ramayana · Kishkindha Kanda
LINE: Not too long, not vague, not rushed, not strained, in an even voice: that is how to speak.
TRANSLATION: His words were not long-winded, not ambiguous, not rushed, not strained; they came from the chest and throat in an even, middle tone.
MEANING: Ram has just watched Hanuman speak to the fearful monkey king Sugriva. Hanuman did not overwhelm him with long speeches, nor did he leave him guessing. He spoke with a steady, even voice from the chest. That is how words should move between people. When fear is loud, calm and clear speech builds trust faster than any proof. It is a discipline, not a trick, to let your voice stay level and your meaning land exactly where it should.
FOR YOU: Stand in your own truth and do your own duty from it; let their picture stay theirs, and let your conduct speak.
