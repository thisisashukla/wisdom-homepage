## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 2.67
LINE: When the mind chases a wandering sense, it is swept off like a boat in the wind.
TRANSLATION: When the mind runs after the roving senses, it carries away one's understanding, as the wind carries a boat away on the water.
MEANING: Arjuna asks Krishna how a person of steady mind acts, and Krishna tells him. He describes someone who is calm, content, and in control. Then Krishna explains how a person loses that steadiness. When the mind chases after what the senses are drawn to, it is swept away like a boat caught in a strong wind on the water. The understanding is carried off. Steadiness is built by guarding where the mind goes. Anger and confusion begin with what you dwell on.
FOR YOU: You are caught between the PM who meets targets and the mother your daughter wants. That pull is not a failure of you. It is the mind chasing after approval, and it is sweeping your understanding away. See the split for what it is, and let the need for their approval rest for a moment.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Bhartrihari · Vairagya Shatakam
LINE: Why exhaust yourself daily winning others' approval? Settle inside, and your own clarity grants every wish.
TRANSLATION: O heart, why do you enter into weariness, day after day trying to please the minds of others in so many ways, to win their favour? When you are at peace within, your own clear resolve rises by itself like a cluster of wish-granting gems: what wish of yours would it not fulfil?
MEANING: Bhartrihari asks his own heart why it exhausts itself trying to please the minds of others. When you are at peace within, your own clear resolve rises by itself, and there is no wish it would not fulfill.
FOR YOU: You are trying to win your daughter’s approval by being present in a way that is impossible for you. This need is what turns her question into a verdict on your worth. You do not have to earn her love by being the perfect mother.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: Let them praise or blame, let money come or go: the steady don't step off the right path.
TRANSLATION: Let the worldly-wise blame or praise; let fortune come or go as she pleases; let death come today or ages hence: the steadfast do not move a step from the path of what is right.
MEANING: Bhartrihari speaks to you from the section on steadfastness. He says that the steady do not step off the right path. Let the worldly-wise blame or praise. Let fortune come or go as she pleases. Let death come today or ages hence. He says setbacks rarely last for those who keep going on the right path. He is asking you to set your own worth inside, so that nothing outside can shake your ground.
FOR YOU: Your daughter's question is just one sound in a loud room. Do not let it knock you off the path you are already walking. You are not half a mother. You are the mother she has, and that is enough.

## Day 5 [act] Maha Upanishad 6.68
LINE: Be busy on the outside and unhurried inside. Act fully, but don't let it agitate you.
TRANSLATION: Outwardly busy, inwardly free of agitation; a doer on the outside, a non-doer within: move through the world with a clear mind.
MEANING: Ribhu tells Nidagha that a person can be fully active in the world while remaining calm within. The teaching is to act with the body and mind engaged, but to let the inner self remain free from agitation. The work happens on the outside, but the stillness stays inside. This is how a free person moves through life, doing what is needed without letting the results disturb their core.
FOR YOU: When you are with your daughter, let your work be at work and your presence be with her. The guilt comes from trying to be both at once. Let the PM rest at the office and let the mother be fully there when she is home.
