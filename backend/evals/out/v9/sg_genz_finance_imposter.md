## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 18.48
LINE: Don't abandon work that suits you just because it has flaws. Every undertaking has some, like fire has smoke.
TRANSLATION: One should not give up the work suited to one's nature, Kaunteya, even if it has faults; for all undertakings are clouded by faults, as fire is by smoke.
MEANING: Krishna speaks to Arjuna about finding peace in the work that is already yours. He tells him not to drop the duties that fit his nature just because they have imperfections. Every action has its flaws, Krishna says, just as every fire carries smoke. The point is to do what is yours, with its cracks and all, as an offering rather than a flawless performance.
FOR YOU: You got the promotion and you keep preparing until you are sick. That is the smoke around the fire of your own work. The work is yours. The fear is not proof that you do not belong there. Let the preparation be your offering and let the smoke clear on its own.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Mahabharata · Vana Parva · Vana: Draupadi and Yudhishthira
LINE: Never look down on yourself. Nothing good grows from self-contempt.
TRANSLATION: A person should never look down on himself. The one who despises himself never finds real prosperity.
MEANING: Draupadi is telling Yudhishthira that waiting on fate is no plan. She says people must act. She tells him never to look down on himself. She says the one who despises himself never finds real prosperity. She means that you could do right for its own sake. Then you could work as if it all depends on you. The truth underneath is that your own judgment is what makes you sick.
FOR YOU: You are the one who decides if you are worthy. The verdict you keep rehearsing is what makes you sick before the room. You can step out from under that verdict today.

## Day 3 [steady] Mahabharata · Udyoga Parva · Vidula's counsel
LINE: Don't look down on yourself because of past failures. Fortunes come and go, and can come again.
TRANSLATION: Son, don't look down on yourself because of past failures. Fortunes that never were come to be, and fortunes that were are lost. But don't start things out of mere resentment, like a fool.
MEANING: Queen Vidula finds her son Sanjaya lying defeated after he has lost his kingdom. She tells him not to look down on himself because of past failures, because fortunes that never were can come to be, and fortunes that were can be lost. But she warns him not to start out of mere resentment, like a fool. The verse proposes that past failure does not define you. Results are uncertain, but those who never try never succeed.
FOR YOU: You hold that a promotion is not a loan, and that you are more than the story you tell yourself about where you came from. The comparison you keep with others is a habit, not a verdict. Let it be there without letting it write your next move.

## Day 4 [steady] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: A ball knocked down bounces back up. For good people, setbacks rarely last.
TRANSLATION: A ball struck down by the hand flies right back up. As a rule, the misfortunes of those who live rightly do not last.
MEANING: Bhartrihari writes that a ball struck down by the hand flies right back up. He says the misfortunes of those who live rightly do not last. He is not offering a magic cure. He is simply saying that the downward swing is not the whole story. The ball has a nature to rise again. The steady person does not stay on the ground.
FOR YOU: You are holding a promotion that feels like a borrowed thing. Let the ball fall. It will rise again, not because you are perfect, but because you are still moving on the path you are on.

## Day 5 [act] Mahabharata · Vana Parva · Vyadha Gita
LINE: Turn to doing good after a wrong, and you come out like the moon from behind a cloud.
TRANSLATION: If a person who has done wrong turns to doing good, he is freed from all his wrongs, like the moon coming out from behind a great cloud.
MEANING: Dharmavyadha, a meat-seller in Mithila, tells the ascetic Kaushika that if a person turns to doing good after a wrong, they are freed like the moon from behind a cloud. He is not talking about grand penance. He is talking about the ordinary work of caring for your parents and keeping envy away. The moon does not fight the cloud. It simply keeps its place until the cloud moves.
FOR YOU: This week, your work is to do one ordinary thing well. Care for the people in your life, and let your work speak for itself. The cloud will move.

## Day 6 [act] Bhagavad Gita 3.35
LINE: Better your own path done imperfectly than someone else's done perfectly. Borrowed lives are dangerous.
TRANSLATION: Better is one's own duty, even done imperfectly, than the duty of another well done. Better is death in one's own duty; the duty of another is full of fear.
MEANING: Krishna speaks to Arjuna about action and the danger of borrowed lives. He says that doing your own duty, even if it is done imperfectly, is far better than perfectly performing someone else's duty. The path that belongs to you might have stumbles and rough edges, but it is yours. Walking a life that is not truly yours brings constant fear because the weight of it was never meant for you to carry.
FOR YOU: The overpreparing and the sick feeling before you present are signs of a borrowed life. You are trying to prove you belong in a role that was not truly yours to begin with, and that is what exhausts you.
