## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 6.34
LINE: The mind is restless, stormy, stubborn and strong. Holding it feels like holding the wind.
TRANSLATION: For the mind is restless, Krishna, turbulent, powerful and stubborn. I think it is as hard to hold as the wind.
MEANING: Arjuna tells Krishna that after all the teaching, his mind still feels like the wind. He says it is restless, turbulent, stubborn and strong, and that holding it is as hard as catching the air. Krishna does not argue. He says this is the nature of the mind, but that through practice and letting go, it can be trained. The verse offers the truth that a restless mind is not a personal failure, but a condition that can be worked with.
FOR YOU: Your body still wakes to the office. That is the mind caught in the wind, still pulling at the old shape. Let it pull for a while. You do not have to hold it still. You only have to notice the pull, and let it pass.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Bhagavad Gita 2.62
LINE: Dwell on something and you get attached; attachment becomes wanting, and blocked wanting turns into anger.
TRANSLATION: When a person dwells on objects, attachment to them is born. From attachment comes desire, and from desire, anger.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna on the battlefield, answering what a steady person looks like. He explains that when someone dwells on objects, attachment grows. From that attachment comes wanting, and when that wanting is blocked, it turns into anger. Krishna is tracing the exact path that leads to ruin. The mind does not simply feel angry. It becomes angry because it has been dwelling on what it has lost and what it wants back.
FOR YOU: Your mind is dwelling on the ten-minute call, and it is turning that shock into anger at your own worth. That is not proof. That is just the mind following its old habit of clinging to what it thinks it needs.

## Day 3 [steady] Ashtavakra Gita 15.10
LINE: Whether the body lasts a hundred years or ends today, what you are neither gains nor loses.
TRANSLATION: Let the body last till the end of the age, or let it go today: what gain or loss is that to you, who are pure awareness?
MEANING: Ashtavakra speaks to Janaka, pressing home that the core of a person is not the body or the mind but pure awareness. He tells Janaka that whether the body lasts a hundred years or ends today, what he truly is gains nothing and loses nothing. The body and the mind are waves on the ocean, but the ocean itself remains untouched by their rising and falling.
FOR YOU: The call was a wave, and your body still wakes as if the tide is coming in. But what you are is the ocean. Let the wave of that smallness rise and fall without touching the depth you have built over twenty-two years.

## Day 4 [steady] Mahabharata · Shanti Parva · Shanti: Consolation of Yudhishthira
LINE: Days don't come back, nor months, nor years. Be here for the ones in front of you.
TRANSLATION: Days do not come back, nor months, nor years. And beings, once parted, do not meet again in the same way.
MEANING: The sage Ashma speaks to King Janaka, who is grieving the loss of his family and wealth. Ashma tells him that time does not return.
