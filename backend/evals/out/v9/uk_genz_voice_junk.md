## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 6.34
LINE: The mind is restless, stormy, stubborn and strong. Holding it feels like holding the wind.
TRANSLATION: For the mind is restless, Krishna, turbulent, powerful and stubborn. I think it is as hard to hold as the wind.
MEANING: Arjuna is on the battlefield. They are looking at their own life and saying that the mind is restless, stormy, stubborn, and strong. Holding it feels like trying to catch the wind. Krishna hears this and says that practice and letting go can train it. The verse proposes that you do not have to fight the mind with force. ## Day 3 [steady] Bhagavad Gita 6.19
LINE: Like a lamp in a windless place that doesn't flicker: that's a settled mind.
TRANSLATION: As a lamp in a windless place does not flicker: that is the image used for the yogi of controlled mind practising union with the self.
MEANING: Krishna is teaching Arjuna how to meditate. He says that a mind in control is like a lamp in a place where there is no wind. It does not flicker. This is the image used for a yogi who is practicing union with the self. The idea is that a steady mind does not waver, even when the world around it moves. To get there, the practice is not to force the mind into stillness all at once. It is to bring it back, gently and again and again.
FOR YOU: Your mind moves from one thing to the next just as Krishna describes. The phone is one of those things. Today, try to notice the flickers. When your attention drops to the screen, just bring it back to one thing. Do not fight the restlessness. Just return to the one thing you are doing.

## Day 4 [steady] Katha Upanishad 1.3.5-6
LINE: Without understanding, the senses bolt like wild horses; with a steady mind, they run like trained ones.
TRANSLATION: For one who lacks understanding, whose mind is never held steady, the senses run out of control like a driver's wild horses. For one who has understanding, whose mind is always steady, the senses obey like good horses.
MEANING: Yama speaks to Nachiketa about the chariot of the self. The self is the rider, the body the chariot, the intellect the driver, the mind the reins and the senses the horses. Without understanding, the senses bolt like wild horses. With a steady mind, they run like trained ones. You can hold the reins, or the horses will drive you.
FOR YOU: Your mind is not broken, it is just untrained. When you feel scattered, the senses are the wild horses and the phone is the bit they are chewing. You do not need to stop them. You only need to hold the reins, even for a few minutes, and let them feel who is driving.

## Day 5 [act] Bhagavad Gita 2.58
LINE: Like a tortoise pulling in its limbs, draw your senses back from what pulls at them.
TRANSLATION: When, like a tortoise drawing in its limbs on every side, a person withdraws the senses from their objects, his wisdom is firmly set.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna on the battlefield, answering how a steady person acts. He says that a person of steady wisdom is like a tortoise pulling its limbs in from every side when danger comes. The senses are the limbs, and the objects of the world are the danger. When the person withdraws the senses from what pulls at them, their wisdom is firmly set. The point is that steadiness is not forced. It is built by guarding attention, moment by moment.

## Day 6 [act] Yoga Vasishtha
LINE: Daily practice and clear thinking make the mind clear, slowly, the way a mango ripens.
TRANSLATION: Through daily practice and discernment the mind soon becomes clear, just as a mango slowly ripens into its sweetness.
MEANING: Vasishtha tells Rama that Prahlada did not reach awakening by accident. The boy Prahlada earned his freedom through his own steady effort. Yet this effort was not alone. It was met by grace.
