## Day 2 [diagnose] Yoga Vasishtha
LINE: 'Done this, doing this, must do that.' Caught in endless lists, the mind never gets a moment's rest.
TRANSLATION: Carried along on endless great waves of tasks, the mind never gets a moment's rest; its depths dance with worry. 'This I've done, this I'm doing, this I'll do': so the bird of the mind, tangled in a net of reckonings, faints.
MEANING: Bhasa and Vilasa meet as old men in a forest. Vilasa asks how Bhasa is, and Bhasa says he is not well. He says his mind is caught in a net of reckonings. It thinks about what he has done, what he is doing, and what he must do next. These endless lists keep him from any rest.
FOR YOU: Your mind is caught in the same net. It is not the work that drains you. It is the fear of dropping one duty before starting the next. Notice the lists your mind makes, and see how they keep you from a single breath of rest.

## Day 3 [steady] Maha Upanishad 6.68
LINE: Be busy on the outside and unhurried inside. Act fully, but don't let it agitate you.
TRANSLATION: Outwardly busy, inwardly free of agitation; a doer on the outside, a non-doer within: move through the world with a clear mind.
MEANING: Ribhu is talking to Nidagha. Ribhu is describing what it looks like to be truly free. A free person does what comes up. They let go of what is lost. They do the work at hand. They speak gently. They are busy on the outside. Inside, they are calm. They move through the world with a clear mind. They do not get pulled into the noise around them.
FOR YOU: The phone calls, the money sent, the wedding plans. All of it is the work at hand. You can do it. You can let the worry about the result sit beside it instead of in front of it.

## Day 4 [steady] Bhagavad Gita 2.47
LINE: Your work is yours to do; the results are not yours to hold. Don't let that make you stop.
TRANSLATION: Your right is to the work alone, never to its fruits. Do not let the fruit be your motive, and do not cling to doing nothing.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna on the battlefield. Arjuna is frozen, unable to act because he is terrified of the outcome of the war. Krishna tells him that his duty is to fight, to do the work fully, but he must give up his attachment to the fruits, the results, of that action. The teaching is to act wholeheartedly while letting go of owning the outcome. You do your part with all your energy, and you release the need to control how it turns out.
FOR YOU: The result of your work is not yours to hold. The surgery, the wedding, the money. You can do your part. Then you can let the outcome sit without letting it shake you.

## Day 5 [act] Bhagavad Gita 6.26
LINE: Wherever the restless mind wanders, gently bring it back. Again and again.
TRANSLATION: Wherever the restless, unsteady mind wanders, from there one should draw it back and bring it under the control of the self.
MEANING: In this teaching, Krishna is describing the nature of a settled mind. He says that wherever the restless mind wanders, it should be gently drawn back and brought under control. The mind will wander, and that is natural. The practice is not to force it to stop, but to return it with moderation, again and again. It is a gentle, patient return to what is steady.
FOR YOU: The mind will pull toward the hospital bills, the brother's wedding, the call from home. Let it go there. Then bring it back to the single task in front of you. Do this for one hour today, and let the rest of the duties wait.
