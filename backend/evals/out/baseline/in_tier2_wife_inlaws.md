## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 18.47
LINE: Better your own path, imperfectly walked, than someone else's done perfectly.
TRANSLATION: Better is one's own duty, though imperfect, than the duty of another well performed. Doing the work born of one's own nature, one incurs no wrong.
MEANING: Krishna speaks to Arjuna on the field of battle, reminding him that a duty born of one's own nature is the only true path. He says that walking your own road, even with its flaws and stumbles, is better than playing a role that fits someone else perfectly. The work that flows from your own being, done with honesty, is the only work that leaves no stain on the soul. It is an invitation to stop measuring yourself against the family script and to trust the practice you built.
FOR YOU: You built this practice from nothing. It is your nature. Let them say you will leave it. You are walking your own road, and it is better than any script they have for you.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Bhagavad Gita 18.16
LINE: If you think you alone made it happen, or alone made it fail, you're not seeing the whole picture.
TRANSLATION: That being so, whoever, through an untrained understanding, sees himself alone as the doer does not truly see.
MEANING: Krishna explains to Arjuna that no action happens by one person alone. Every deed is born of five causes: the setting, the doer, the instruments, the effort, and providence. He warns that the person who thinks they alone are the maker of success or the sole author of failure is looking through a cracked lens. The truth is that you are one cause among five, and the rest belong to the world around you.
FOR YOU: Your mother-in-law speaks of leaving. Your husband stays silent. These are not your failures to prevent. They are the other four causes at work, and you are only one of the five.
