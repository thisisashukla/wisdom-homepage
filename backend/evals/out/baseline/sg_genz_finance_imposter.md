## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 1.29–30
LINE: Shaking limbs, a dry mouth, a racing body: the Gita opens with a panic attack, not a hero.
TRANSLATION: My limbs give way, my mouth is parched, my body trembles and my hair stands on end.
MEANING: Arjuna stands in his chariot between two armies and sees his own family. He does not raise his bow. His limbs shake, his mouth goes dry, his body trembles. The Gita opens with a panic attack, not a hero. Even the strongest people freeze. The teaching begins there.
FOR YOU: That sick feeling before you present is not a sign you are failing. It is a body that cares. Let it be there while you speak, and let the shaking be part of what carries you through the room.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Mahabharata · Vana Parva · Vana: Draupadi and Yudhishthira
LINE: Never look down on yourself. Nothing good grows from self-contempt.
TRANSLATION: A person should never look down on himself. The one who despises himself never finds real prosperity.
MEANING: Draupadi speaks to Yudhishthira in the forest. She tells him never to look down on himself. The one who despises himself never finds real prosperity. Do right for its own sake, then work as if it all depends on you. Effort still matters. Fate rewards no one who waits.
FOR YOU: Your overpreparing is a form of waiting. It asks the future to prove you are good enough. Today, do one thing for its own sake, not to prove anything. Let the work stand on its own and see what it gives back.

## Day 3 [steady] Mahabharata · Udyoga Parva · Vidula's counsel
LINE: Don't think less of yourself or settle for little. Set your mind on something good, and stop fearing.
TRANSLATION: Do not hold yourself in contempt, and do not keep it going on too little. Set your mind on what is truly good; don't be afraid. Pull yourself together.
MEANING: Queen Vidula finds her son Sanjaya lying defeated after he has lost his kingdom. She tells him not to hold himself in contempt, and not to settle for too little. Set your mind on what is truly good, and stop fearing. Pull yourself together.
FOR YOU: You are not your father's taxi. You are not a mistake waiting to be found out. Set your mind on what is truly good in the room, and let that be enough to pull you through the day.

## Day 4 [steady] Bhagavad Gita 6.7
LINE: Self-mastered and calm, you stay centred in heat and cold, pleasure and pain, praise and insult.
TRANSLATION: For one who has conquered himself and is at peace, the higher self stays steady in cold and heat, pleasure and pain, honour and dishonour.
MEANING: Krishna is with Arjuna on the battlefield, telling him that the self-mastered person remains steady when praise and insult come. The one who has conquered themselves stays centred in heat and cold, pleasure and pain, honour and dishonour. Their higher self does not shake, because they are no longer at war with themselves. This steadiness is not a mask. It is the result of choosing to be your own friend rather than your own worst enemy.
FOR YOU: When you stand to present, feel the room and the fear. You do not need to prove yourself to them, or to your father's taxi. You only need to be your own friend right now.

## Day 5 [act] Bhagavad Gita 2.47
LINE: Your work is yours to do; the results are not yours to hold. Don't let that make you stop.
TRANSLATION: Your right is to the work alone, never to its fruits. Do not let the fruit be your motive, and do not cling to doing nothing.
MEANING: Krishna tells Arjuna that his right is only to the work itself, never to its fruits. Do not let the outcome be the reason you act, and do not let the fear of the outcome stop you from acting. The work is yours to do fully. The results are not yours to hold, so you can act without being chained to what comes next.
FOR YOU: You are spending four hours a day overthinking your next presentation. Do your part with full attention, and then step away from the outcome. Your worth is not decided by how the room receives your words.

## Day 6 [act] Maha Upanishad 6.64-65
LINE: Do the work in front of you, speak gently, read people's hearts, and you won't sink in the world.
TRANSLATION: Always doing the work that comes, seeing friend and foe alike, free of wanting and not-wanting, such a one neither grieves nor craves. Saying what is agreeable to all, speaking gently when asked, knowing the intent of others' hearts, such a one does not sink in the world.
MEANING: Ribhu tells Nidagha that a free person does the work in front of them, speaks gently, and reads the hearts of others. They do not grieve over what is lost, nor do they crave what they do not have. They move through the world unhurried and calm inside, treating everyone as family. This is how one stays afloat in the world without sinking.
FOR YOU: In your next meeting, do the work that is in front of you. Speak gently, even when you feel the old doubt rising. Read the room for what it is, not for what you fear it might become. Let your inside stay calm while your outside does the job.
