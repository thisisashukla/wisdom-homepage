## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 6.33
LINE: This calm you describe, Krishna, I can't see it lasting. My mind is too restless.
TRANSLATION: Arjuna said: This yoga of evenness that you have taught, Madhusudana, I do not see how it can last, because of restlessness.
MEANING: Arjuna stands before Krishna, admitting he cannot see the calm he has been taught lasting. He says his mind is too restless. Krishna hears this and does not dismiss it. He agrees that the mind is hard to hold, like the wind. But he says that through steady practice, and through the gentle act of letting go, the mind can be trained. The struggle is real, and it is not a sign of failure.
FOR YOU: When you sit down to study, your mind runs to Instagram. That is not a broken mind. It is a mind that needs to be trained, gently and again, with no anger at yourself for the wandering.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhagavad Gita 6.19
LINE: Like a lamp in a windless place that doesn't flicker: that's a settled mind.
TRANSLATION: As a lamp in a windless place does not flicker: that is the image used for the yogi of controlled mind practising union with the self.
MEANING: Krishna describes a mind that is settled. He uses the image of a lamp in a windless place that does not flicker. He says this is the mark of a mind in control. But he also teaches that this comes from moderation and from bringing the wandering mind back, again and again. It is not about forcing the wind to stop. It is about steady practice.
FOR YOU: You do not need to stop your mind from wandering. You only need to bring it back, gently, each time you notice it is gone. That is the practice.

## Day 4 [steady] Ashtavakra Gita 18.58
LINE: A restless mind is agitated even doing nothing; a skilled one stays calm doing everything.
TRANSLATION: The fool is restless even when doing nothing; the skilful one stays calm even when doing everything that needs doing.
MEANING: In the closing verses of his teaching, the sage Ashtavakra speaks to Janaka about the true nature of peace. He points out that a foolish mind is agitated even in idle stillness, while a skilled mind remains steady through every task. The calm you seek is not a place you reach by stopping your life. It is the quality with which you hold the ordinary, the way you carry the weight of doing things without letting it disturb your core. This steadiness is not a reward for a finished exam.
FOR YOU: When you open Instagram, you are trying to escape the noise, but the noise simply moves with you. The restlessness is not the apps. It is the grip on the result. Let your hands type the words for your exam, and let your mind stay with the words, not the fear of the mark.
