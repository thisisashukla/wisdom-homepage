## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 18.47
LINE: Better your own path, imperfectly walked, than someone else's done perfectly.
TRANSLATION: Better is one's own duty, though imperfect, than the duty of another well performed. Doing the work born of one's own nature, one incurs no wrong.
MEANING: Krishna speaks to Arjuna on the battlefield, where Arjuna is paralyzed by grief and duty. He tells him to do his own work, however flawed, rather than another's. The path fits the person. This is not about your caste or family script, but about an honest fit with your own nature. Krishna says doing the work born of your own nature incurs no wrong.
FOR YOU: You are trying to be two people at once. Your daughter sees the split. Do the work that fits you, flaws and all, as an offering today. Your part is not to be a perfect mother and a perfect PM. It is to be you, doing what is yours.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Yoga Vasishtha
LINE: No one hurts us like we hurt ourselves. The mind becomes the mind's own enemy.
TRANSLATION: Our own senses are the enemies; truth itself has turned untrue; the self strikes the self, and the mind is the mind's own enemy.
MEANING: In the Yoga Vasishtha, Rama speaks to the sages after seeing the world's futility. He says our own senses are the enemies. The mind strikes the self, and the mind becomes its own enemy. This is a lament, a question, not an answer. The mind can lift itself, but first it must name what feels empty.
FOR YOU: The guilt is a voice in your head. It is not the truth. Name the feeling today. You are not failing. You are stretched thin. The mind is the mind's own enemy, and it is lying to you.

## Day 3 [steady] Ramcharitmanas · Uttar Kand
LINE: One parent, many children, each different in gifts and nature, and the love for all is the same.
TRANSLATION: One father has many sons, each different in gifts, nature and conduct: one a scholar, one an ascetic, one wise, one rich, one brave, one generous; one all-knowing, one devoted to duty. The father's love is the same for all; but if one is devoted to the father in mind, word and deed, knowing no other duty even in dreams...
MEANING: Ram speaks to Kakbhushundi, explaining how he loves all beings. He uses a father with many sons, each different in gifts and nature. The father's love is the same for all. Pride is lanced from those he loves, as a mother lances a child's boil. What remains is what matters.
FOR YOU: Your love for your children is whole, even when you feel split. The pride you feel in your career is lanced for your good. Hold the love. It is enough.

## Day 4 [steady] Ashtavakra Gita 15.11
LINE: Let the waves of the world rise or fall. You, the ocean, neither gain nor lose.
TRANSLATION: In you, the boundless ocean, let the waves of the world rise or fall by their own nature. You neither grow nor shrink.
MEANING: Ashtavakra speaks to Janaka, who is caught in the weight of his duties and his guilt. He tells him that he is the boundless ocean, and the world is only the waves. The waves may rise or fall, but the ocean itself neither grows nor shrinks. This means the roles you play, the guilt you carry, the pressure of being a mother and a manager, none of that changes who you truly are underneath.
FOR YOU: In the car today, when your daughter asked why you were not there, you felt the waves rise and crash over you. You are not the wave of her disappointment, nor the wave of your own guilt. You are the ocean that holds them both.

## Day 5 [act] Aitareya Upanishad 2.1.3
LINE: Caring for your child, you care for yourself. Through them you are born a second time.
TRANSLATION: When he cares for the child from birth onward, he is caring for himself, for the continuation of these worlds; that is how they continue. That is his second birth.
MEANING: A child is born a second time not only from the body, but from the care given to them. The one who feeds, protects, and raises the child is also feeding, protecting, and raising themselves, for the world continues only through this care. To tend to the child is to tend to your own continuation. The one who carries and raises the child is also carried and raised by the child.
FOR YOU: You are not two separate halves. When you hold your daughter, you are also holding yourself. Let her presence remind you that you are not failing, but continuing.

## Day 6 [act] Bhagavad Gita 2.38
LINE: Hold pleasure and pain, gain and loss, win and defeat as equal. Then step in and act.
TRANSLATION: Treating pleasure and pain, gain and loss, victory and defeat alike, then engage in the fight. Done this way, you will incur no wrong.
MEANING: Before you step into any task, see the result as equal to the effort. See pleasure and pain, success and failure, as the same. Then act. When you act from this steadiness, you do not fall into wrong.
FOR YOU: Your daughter's question brings pain, and work brings gain. See them as equal. Then do one small thing for her, and one small thing for your work. Act from steadiness, not from guilt.
