## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 1.29–30
LINE: Shaking limbs, a dry mouth, a racing body: the Gita opens with a panic attack, not a hero.
TRANSLATION: My limbs give way, my mouth is parched, my body trembles and my hair stands on end.
MEANING: Krishna finds Arjuna on the battlefield, and the prince is shaking, his mouth dry, his body trembling. He is not standing tall. He is collapsing. In that moment, Krishna sees the truth: even the strongest people freeze. The teaching does not begin with a hero. It begins with a breakdown, and the wisdom starts right there in the shaking.
FOR YOU: Right now, your body is still carrying the shake of that morning. The milk is gone, but the tremor remains, and it is asking for rest rather than a verdict.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Bhagavad Gita 2.63
LINE: Anger clouds judgement, clouded judgement loses memory, lost memory wrecks reason, and then you fall.
TRANSLATION: From anger comes delusion; from delusion, confusion of memory; from confusion of memory, the ruin of understanding; and with understanding ruined, a person is lost.
MEANING: Krishna explains how a person falls. It starts with anger, which clouds judgment. That clouded judgment loses memory, which ruins understanding, and then the person is lost. The teaching is about how attention works. What you dwell on becomes the seed of ruin. Steadiness is built by guarding your attention before anger takes hold.
FOR YOU: The milk is not the anger. The anger is the result of something you are dwelling on, a memory of being judged that is now clouding your judgment in the present.

## Day 3 [steady] Katharudra Upanishad 8
LINE: Don't wear out your body chasing pleasure or fleeing pain. Praised, don't gloat; blamed, don't curse others.
TRANSLATION: Let him not torment the body too much for the sake of pleasure or pain. Praised, let him not gloat; blamed, let him not curse others.
MEANING: A teacher gives a rule to a renunciant. Let him not torment his body by chasing pleasure or fleeing pain. If he is praised, let him not gloat. If he is blamed, let him not curse others. The body is not to be punished for a feeling, and the mind is not to be given away to the opinion of others.
FOR YOU: Your body is not a place to be punished for a feeling. When the blame comes, even from yourself, let it land without cursing the one who spilled the milk.

## Day 4 [steady] Mahabharata · Udyoga Parva · Vidula's counsel
LINE: Don't look down on yourself because of past failures. Fortunes come and go, and can come again.
TRANSLATION: Son, don't look down on yourself because of past failures. Fortunes that never were come to be, and fortunes that were are lost. But don't start things out of mere resentment, like a fool.
MEANING: Queen Vidula finds her son Sanjaya defeated after losing his kingdom. She tells him not to despise himself for what he has lost, because fortunes rise and fall like tides. What is gone can return, but a man who acts only from bitter anger destroys himself. The lesson is that a past defeat is not the final word on who you are, and the only way back is to rise again without letting shame or fury lead the way.
FOR YOU: At work, you steady twelve people without a tremor. But at home, the spilled milk becomes a verdict on your whole life, and you hear your mother's voice in your own. This verse says that losing your temper does not make you her daughter.

MEANING: Krishna tells Arjuna that you are your own best friend or your own worst enemy. You can lift yourself up, or you can push yourself down. The self that stays steady in honour and insult is the self that refuses to abandon itself, and that is the only loyalty that truly matters.
