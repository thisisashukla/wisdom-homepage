## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 6.34
LINE: The mind is restless, stormy, stubborn and strong. Holding it feels like holding the wind.
TRANSLATION: For the mind is restless, Krishna, turbulent, powerful and stubborn. I think it is as hard to hold as the wind.
MEANING: Arjuna tells Krishna that his mind is restless and stubborn, as hard to hold as the wind. Krishna does not argue or promise a quick fix. He simply agrees that the mind is powerful and hard to control, and says that practice and letting go are what train it. The idea is not to force the mind still, but to steady it through a steady hand. The wind does not stop because you shout at it.
FOR YOU: You are holding a mind that has been answering calls for eleven years. It is exhausted, and it will not settle because it has not been asked what it needs. Let the phone wait for one evening and give the mind a single task it chooses, so it remembers what it is to be held.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Yoga Vasishtha
LINE: The mind pounces on a task like a bird on food, then drops it like a bored child's toy.
TRANSLATION: The mind swoops onto tasks like a bird onto meat, and a moment later loses interest, like a child with a toy.
MEANING: Rama, sitting among the sages, tells them about his own restless mind. He says it seizes a task like a bird pouncing on meat, then abandons it a moment later like a bored child dropping a toy. The verse proposes that naming this emptiness honestly is the first step. It is a question, not an answer, asking what is the point of this constant grabbing and dropping.
FOR YOU: You are on the phone for hours, answering calls, and your mind is doing the same thing. It seizes each task, then drops it. You are not failing. You are simply naming what is empty inside you, and that is enough for now.

## Day 4 [steady] Bhagavad Gita 2.14
LINE: Heat and cold, pleasure and pain come and go. They don't last; learn to bear them.
TRANSLATION: Contact of the senses with their objects brings cold and heat, pleasure and pain. They come and go and do not last. Bear them patiently, Arjuna.
MEANING: Krishna speaks to Arjuna after the battle, when grief is raw and the world feels like it has ended. He reminds him that the true Self within never dies and never changes. He says that contact between the senses and the world brings cold and heat, pleasure and pain, but these things only come and go. They do not last. The instruction is to bear these passing states patiently, because what you truly are outlasts every loss and every passing pain.
FOR YOU: The calls from home, the worry about your parents, the weight of the money you send, these come and go. They do not last. Let them pass through you without settling in your mind. Bear them, and return to the part of you that remains steady.

LINE: Yoga is unhooking from sorrow. Practise it with resolve and a heart that doesn't lose hope.
TRANSLATION: Know that to be yoga: the breaking of contact with sorrow. This yoga should be practised with determination and with an undespairing mind.
MEANING: Krishna is teaching Arjuna the nature of a settled mind. He says that yoga is the breaking of contact with sorrow.
