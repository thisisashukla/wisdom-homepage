## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 18.26
LINE: Free of clinging and boasting, full of resolve and enthusiasm, unchanged by success or failure: the best kind of doer.
TRANSLATION: The doer who is free from attachment, does not speak of himself, is full of steadiness and enthusiasm, and unmoved by success or failure, is called sattvic.
MEANING: Krishna is sorting the different ways a person acts and feels, and he comes to the best kind of doer. This is someone who does not cling to the outcome or boast about their own cleverness. They stay full of steady enthusiasm and do not let success or failure change them. They simply keep going, steady in their own resolve, and the joy they find is hard at first but becomes sweet later.
FOR YOU: You have crossed a line, and now the work is yours to keep. See if you can stay steady when the phone rings and the next task appears. Let your enthusiasm carry you, but do not let the first customer become the thing you cling to.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Yoga Vasishtha
LINE: Effort brings results, sooner or later, depending on time and place. That timing is what we call fate.
TRANSLATION: Results come from effort, sooner or later, depending on place and time. That arrival of the fruit, early or late, is what people call fate.
MEANING: Vasishtha is teaching Rama that the world is not a game of chance. He tells him that what people call fate is nothing more than the harvest of effort planted long ago. The timing of that harvest changes with the season and the soil, but the fruit always belongs to the one who tended the tree. Rama is being asked to stop grieving what he could not control and to trust that his own hands can still shape what comes next.
FOR YOU: You have signed the first customer after fourteen months of tending your tree. The next year will test your patience as the fruit takes its own time to ripen. Do not mistake the quiet between now and the next milestone for a sign that the work is failing. Stay with your effort.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: Let them praise or blame, let money come or go: the steady don't step off the right path.
TRANSLATION: Let the worldly-wise blame or praise; let fortune come or go as she pleases; let death come today or ages hence: the steadfast do not move a step from the path of what is right.
MEANING: Bhartrihari writes to the reader from a place of quiet certainty. He says let the world praise or blame, let money come or go, let death come today or ages hence, the steadfast do not move a step from the path of what is right. The gods kept churning the ocean past jewels and poison until they found nectar. The steady do not leave the path whatever people say.
FOR YOU: The next year will bring praise and blame in equal measure. Let the money come and let it go. Your task is to keep your hand on the wheel and your eyes on the road. The steady do not step off the right path.

## Day 4 [steady] Mahabharata · Vana Parva · Vana: Draupadi and Yudhishthira
LINE: Don't despair because effort hasn't paid off yet. Every attempt can go either way.
TRANSLATION: 'I keep working and still don't succeed' is no reason to despair. Every effort can end two ways, in success or not; only by not acting is the outcome certain.
MEANING: Draupadi is speaking to Yudhishthira while they are in exile. She sees him waiting on fate and she tells him that effort is not wasted just because it has not paid off yet. Every attempt can end two ways. The only thing that is certain is that if you do nothing, you will get nothing. She is telling him to do what is right for its own sake, and then to work as if the whole outcome rests entirely in his hands.
FOR YOU: You have just crossed a milestone. Now you can feel the year ahead stretching out. Do your part each day as if the whole outcome rests in your hands. Do not wait for the next big sign. Let the work itself be the proof that the effort matters.

## Day 5 [act] Mahabharata · Udyoga Parva · Vidura Niti
LINE: Decide, then begin. Don't stall halfway, don't waste time, and keep yourself steady.
TRANSLATION: One who begins only after deciding, does not stop halfway through the work, does not waste time and keeps himself in hand, is called wise.
MEANING: Vidura is speaking to King Dhritarashtra in the middle of the night. The king cannot sleep and asks for counsel. Vidura tells him that a wise person decides calmly, begins the work, does not stop halfway, does not waste time, and keeps himself steady. He is telling the king to move forward with his eyes open and his hands busy.
FOR YOU: The milestone is behind you. Today, pick one small thing that moves the work forward and begin it. Do not wait for the perfect mood. Keep your hands busy and your mind steady.

## Day 6 [act] Bhagavad Gita 2.47
LINE: Your work is yours to do; the results are not yours to hold. Don't let that make you stop.
TRANSLATION: Your right is to the work alone, never to its fruits. Do not let the fruit be your motive, and do not cling to doing nothing.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna on the battlefield. He is telling Arjuna that he has a right to the work alone, never to its fruits. He is telling Arjuna not to let the fear of the outcome stop him from acting. The work is his to do, but the results belong to the larger order. He is asking Arjuna to act fully and then let go.
FOR YOU: You are building something of your own. Do the work fully and let go of owning the result. Care, plan and aim well, but do not let the outcome own you. The work is yours to do.
