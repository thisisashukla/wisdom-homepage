## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 3.35
LINE: Better your own path done imperfectly than someone else's done perfectly. Borrowed lives are dangerous.
TRANSLATION: Better is one's own duty, even done imperfectly, than the duty of another well done. Better is death in one's own duty; the duty of another is full of fear.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna on the battlefield. He sees Arjuna paralyzed by doubt, trying to live a role that does not fit. Krishna says that it is better to follow your own path, even with its flaws, than to copy someone else perfectly. A borrowed life creates fear. The truth is that your own duty, walked imperfectly, holds more life than another's duty done well.
FOR YOU: You are standing in a business that you built, yet you are playing a role your father still owns in his mind. Choose to walk your own path today, even if your father does not understand. Your own imperfect truth is better than his perfect control.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: Affection fades with distance, friendship without warmth, wealth with carelessness, a child with spoiling.
TRANSLATION: A king is ruined by bad counsel, an ascetic by attachment, a son by spoiling, a brahmin by neglecting study, a family by a wayward son, character by serving the wicked, modesty by drink, farming by neglect, affection by living far away, friendship by lack of warmth, prosperity by bad conduct, and wealth by careless spending.
MEANING: Bhartrihari speaks to the reader from his collection on wealth. He lists how good things fade: affection with distance, friendship without warmth, and wealth with carelessness. He shows that what we value is fragile. The point is that what we hold dear requires our active attention, or it will slip away.
FOR YOU: (library) Money changes how others see you, not who you are. Give it, enjoy it, or lose it.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: Learning is wealth no thief can see. It grows when you give it away and never perishes.
TRANSLATION: It never comes within a thief's sight; it always nourishes some deep good; given to those who ask, it grows greater; even at the end of an age it does not perish. Kings, drop your pride before those who own this inner wealth called learning. Who can compete with them?
MEANING: Bhartrihari speaks to proud kings who think they own everything. He says that learning is a wealth no thief can steal. It grows when you share it and it never perishes. He tells the rulers to drop their pride before those who possess this inner wealth. It is the one thing that cannot be taken by force or time.
FOR YOU: Your business is your learning, your skill, your voice. Your father cannot take that from you. Hold onto it. Let your work and your decisions speak with the quiet authority of what you know is true.

## Day 4 [steady] Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 1.4.14
LINE: Nothing is higher than what is right. With it the weak can face the strong. What is right is truth.
TRANSLATION: Still he did not flourish. So he created a higher form, dharma. Dharma is the power over power; nothing is higher than dharma. Through dharma even a weak man can stand up to a strong one, as with the help of a king. That dharma is truth. So people say of one who speaks truth, 'He speaks dharma,' and of one who speaks dharma, 'He speaks truth.' Both are the same.
MEANING: In the beginning of things, the first being felt afraid and alone, and then he longed for a companion. After that came dharma, the highest power of all. It is the truth that lets the weak stand against the strong. When you speak the truth, you are speaking dharma, and both names mean the same thing. This truth gives a footing to anyone who feels small and alone.
FOR YOU: The business is yours in fact, even if it is his in memory. The silence you keep is not respect. It is the cost of his picture being bigger than your own. Let dharma be the truth that lets you stand in your own space, even when he is there.

LINE: Not too long, not vague, not rushed, not strained, in an even voice: that is how to speak.
TRANSLATION: His words were not long-winded, not ambiguous, not rushed, not strained; they came from the chest and throat in an even, middle tone.

## Day 6 [act] Mahabharata · Shanti Parva · Bhishma, Rajadharma
LINE: People walk over the soft and fear the harsh. Be firm when it's needed, gentle when it's needed.
TRANSLATION: People look down even on the gentle, and they shrink from the harsh. Do not be only harsh, do not be only soft: be firm when firmness is needed and gentle when gentleness is.
MEANING: Brihaspati tells Indra that people will walk over the soft and fear the harsh. So do not be only harsh, and do not be only soft. Be firm when firmness is needed, and be gentle when gentleness is needed. The right response depends on the moment, not on a fixed rule.
