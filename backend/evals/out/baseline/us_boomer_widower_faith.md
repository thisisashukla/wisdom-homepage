## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 2.8
LINE: Even winning everything wouldn't touch this grief that's drying me up inside.
TRANSLATION: I see nothing that could drive away this sorrow that is drying up my senses, not even an unrivalled, prosperous kingdom on earth or rule over the gods.
MEANING: Arjuna has stopped on the field. He has set down his bow and his body is heavy with grief. He looks at the faces around him and tells Krishna that even if he were to win every battle, even if he were made king of the whole earth, none of it would reach the sorrow that is drying him up inside. He says he sees no power that can dry up these tears. This is where he stops. He admits he does not know what to do, and he asks for help.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Mahabharata · Shanti Parva · Shanti: Consolation of Yudhishthira
LINE: In grief, ask: where did I come from, where am I going, who am I? Let that steady you.
TRANSLATION: 'Where was I before? Where will I go? Who am I, really, and why am I here? Why should I grieve, and for what?' Ask these, and steady the mind.
MEANING: The sage Ashma speaks to King Janaka, who is also grieving the loss of family and wealth. Ashma tells him to steady his mind by asking a few deep questions. Where was I before this life? Where will I go after it ends? Who am I, really, and why am I here? Why should I grieve, and for what? These questions are not meant to dismiss the pain. They are meant to hold it while the mind steadies itself.
FOR YOU: You want to know where your wife is, beyond the words that try to make you feel better. You can ask yourself where you came from and where you are going. You can ask who you are in this new quiet.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhagavad Gita 2.22
LINE: As we drop worn-out clothes and put on new ones, the self leaves a worn body for a new one.
TRANSLATION: Just as a person casts off worn-out clothes and puts on new ones, so the embodied self casts off worn-out bodies and enters others that are new.
MEANING: Krishna tells Arjuna that what is truly in us is never born and never dies. He says that just as a person puts off worn-out clothes and puts on new ones, the self leaves an old body and enters a new one. This is the nature of the embodied self. It changes bodies the way we change clothes, but it remains the same.
FOR YOU: You are living in a new body of days without her. You can hold onto the idea that what you truly are outlasts every loss and every passing pain. This does not mean she is gone. It means she is part of what is eternal, just as you are.

## Day 4 [steady] Ashtavakra Gita 15.9
LINE: The body comes and goes; what you truly are does not. Why grieve so?
TRANSLATION: The body, made of nature's qualities, comes, stays and goes. The Self neither comes nor goes. Why grieve for it?
MEANING: Ashtavakra tells Janaka that the body is made of natural qualities and simply comes, stays, and goes away. The Self, however, neither comes nor goes. He asks why anyone would grieve for the Self. He is drawing a line between the temporary vessel and the enduring awareness that witnesses it. He does not deny the pain of the moment, but he offers a different way to stand inside it. You are the ocean, and the waves of life rise and fall without touching the depth of who you are.
FOR YOU: When the temple quiet settles in the evening, let the thought of her body come and go like the day. You are not the space she left behind. Stay with that, even when the silence feels wide.

MEANING: The seer of the Isha Upanishad tells us to do our work here and wish to live a full hundred years. He says there is no other way. LINE: Keep your mind attached to me, lean on me, keep practising. You will come to know me fully.
TRANSLATION: Hear, Partha, how with your mind attached to me, practising yoga and taking refuge in me, you will know me fully, without doubt.
MEANING: Krishna tells Arjuna to hear how, with his mind attached to him, practising yoga and taking refuge, he will come to know him fully. He is not offering a quick fix or a comforting story about where the dead go. He is offering a path of steady attention and practice.
