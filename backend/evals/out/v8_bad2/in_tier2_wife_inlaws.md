## Day 1 [hook] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: Same person, same mind, same voice. Lose money, and people suddenly treat you as someone else.
TRANSLATION: The same faculties, unimpaired; the same name; the same unclouded mind; the same speech. Yet a man deprived of the warmth of wealth becomes, in a moment, someone else. How strange!
MEANING: Bhartrihari is looking at someone who has lost money. He says they have the same body, the same name, the same mind. Yet in a moment, people treat them as a stranger. The world changes its warmth toward them. He is not saying this is fair. He is naming a truth about how the world acts. It sees the money, not the person. And he is saying this is strange.
FOR YOU: What you are cannot be cut smaller by what they see; your worth was never theirs to grant, and standing in your own truth is itself the duty.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Bhagavad Gita 3.35
LINE: Better your own path done imperfectly than someone else's done perfectly. Borrowed lives are dangerous.
TRANSLATION: Better is one's own duty, even done imperfectly, than the duty of another well done. Better is death in one's own duty; the duty of another is full of fear.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna on the battlefield. He is telling Arjuna to act according to his own nature rather than trying to be someone else. The point is that your own path, even if it is not perfect, is safer and truer than copying another person. It is better to follow what is natural to you, even with flaws, than to live a borrowed life that is not yours. This is about finding your own way of being in the world.
FOR YOU: Your CA practice is your path. It was built from nothing. The smallness your mother-in-law sees is not yours. It is theirs. Let your own work be the truth you hold, even when it is hard.

## Day 5 [act] Maha Upanishad 6.64-65
LINE: Do the work in front of you, speak gently, read people's hearts, and you won't sink in the world.
TRANSLATION: Always doing the work that comes, seeing friend and foe alike, free of wanting and not-wanting, such a one neither grieves nor craves. Saying what is agreeable to all, speaking gently when asked, knowing the intent of others' hearts, such a one does not sink in the world.
MEANING: Ribhu tells his student Nidagha that a free person moves through the world by doing the work that comes. They speak gently, see friend and foe alike, and read people's hearts. They let go of what is lost and take up what comes next. Such a person neither grieves nor craves, and does not sink in the world.
FOR YOU: you can do one small thing. When your mother-in-law speaks, let your voice stay gentle but clear, and read the intent behind her words without letting them sink into your center. You do not have to match her volume to be seen.

## Day 6 [act] Bhagavad Gita 17.15
LINE: Speak words that don't wound, that are true, kind and helpful. That is discipline of speech.
TRANSLATION: Speech that causes no distress, that is truthful, pleasant and beneficial, and the regular study of sacred texts: this is called austerity of speech.
MEANING: Krishna is teaching Arjuna about the different kinds of effort, speech and giving. He says that speech which is true, kind and helpful, and which does not wound the person listening, is the highest discipline of speech. It is not about being soft. It is about being clear and caring at the same time.
FOR YOU: Speak the true thing gently and firmly, hold your ground on what is yours, and do your own duty without shrinking to fit the picture they hold.

## Day 7 [story] Gargi questions Yajnavalkya at Janaka's court
LINE: In a hall full of the most learned men in the land, one woman stood up twice.
FOR YOU: Gargi stood in a hall full of scholars and asked the hardest questions, then gave the wisest man credit when she knew he had won. You are being asked to shrink, but you do not have to. Ask your hard questions about your future and your worth, and do not let silence be the answer.
STORY: King Janaka offers a thousand cows to the wisest scholar. Yajnavalkya claims them and is challenged by one learned man after another. Gargi, the only woman, rises twice like a warrior with two arrows, then graciously tells the hall none of them can beat him. Yajnavalkya closes by asking what root a person grows from when cut down by death.
LESSON: Ask your hard questions bravely, and give credit where it is due.
