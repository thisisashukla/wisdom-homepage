## Day 1 [hook] Bhartrihari · Vairagya Shatakam
LINE: I neither learned, nor earned, nor truly cared for my parents. Time slipped away chasing scraps.
TRANSLATION: I did not gain spotless learning, nor earn wealth; I did not even serve my parents with an attentive mind; I did not hold wide-eyed beloveds even in dreams. Time is driven on, like a crow greedy for others' scraps.
MEANING: Bhartrihari speaks from the middle of his life, looking back with a heavy heart. He says he did not gain true learning, he did not earn wealth, and he did not serve his parents with an attentive mind. He did not even hold his beloveds in his dreams. Time is driven on, like a crow greedy for scraps. He is telling us that time is slipping away while we are busy chasing things that do not matter. He is asking us to notice time before it is gone.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Mahabharata · Shanti Parva · Bhishma, Mokshadharma
LINE: Dwelling on what is lost adds a second sorrow to the first.
TRANSLATION: Whoever keeps grieving over the past, over what has died or been lost, gets sorrow on top of sorrow: two misfortunes instead of one.
MEANING: Narada finds the young sage Shuka in grief. Shuka is mourning the loss of his father, Narada tells him that dwelling on what is gone does not bring it back. It only adds a second sorrow to the first loss. He says that brooding does not heal pain. It grows it. The mind needs the same care as the body, through understanding and medicine. If something can be fixed, act on it. If not, let the mind rest in contentment.
FOR YOU: You are grieving the split between your work and your children, and that grief is real. But when you replay your daughter's question in the car, you are adding a second sorrow to the first. You are already paying the price of love by being both a mother and a professional.

LINE: Days don't come back, nor months, nor years. Be here for the ones in front of you.
TRANSLATION: Days do not come back, nor months, nor years. And beings, once parted, do not meet again in the same way.
MEANING: The sage Ashma speaks to King Janaka, who asks how to bear the loss of family and wealth. Ashma says that pain comes from what we lose, but meetings end and days do not return.
