## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 18.47
LINE: Better your own path, imperfectly walked, than someone else's done perfectly.
TRANSLATION: Better is one's own duty, though imperfect, than the duty of another well performed. Doing the work born of one's own nature, one incurs no wrong.
MEANING: Krishna is telling Arjuna that the path is not about perfection. He says that doing the work that comes naturally to you, even if it is flawed, is better than doing someone else's work perfectly. The key is to offer your own work as a true expression of who you are. This means your value is not in the result, but in the honest effort you put in today.
FOR YOU: The stack of rejections feels heavy, but the coffee machine in your hand is your own path right now. Do that work with your full attention, and let the shame of the applications go for a little while.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Ramcharitmanas · Uttar Kand
LINE: Envy is an itch. Burning at another's happiness is a wasting disease of the mind.
TRANSLATION: Possessiveness is ringworm and envy is the itch; the swings of elation and dejection are throat ailments. The burning you feel at another's happiness is consumption, and malice and a crooked mind are leprosy.
MEANING: Kakbhushundi tells Garuda that envy is a disease of the mind. He lists it as a wasting sickness that burns you from the inside when you see someone else succeed. The teaching is to name this feeling for what it is so you are not ruled by it. You do not have to act on it or let it consume your energy.
FOR YOU: Yesterday you saw your own path. Today you name the itch. When you look at your friend's job, you see a disease, not a life. You do not have to catch it.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhagavad Gita 4.22
LINE: Content with what comes, beyond envy, steady in success and failure, you stay free even while working.
TRANSLATION: Content with whatever comes unasked, beyond the pairs of opposites, free from envy, the same in success and failure, even when acting he is not bound.
MEANING: Krishna tells Arjuna that a wise person is free from envy and steady in both success and failure. He is content with whatever comes without asking and remains unbound by his actions. The freedom comes from letting go of the need to compare your outcomes with anyone else's.
FOR YOU: Your friends are climbing, but their climb does not lower the ground you stand on. You are free to take your next step without measuring your height against theirs.

## Day 4 [steady] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: A pot draws the same water from a well or the ocean. Keep your dignity before the rich.
TRANSLATION: Whatever wealth, small or great, the Creator has written on your forehead, you will get it even in a desert, and no more even on Mount Meru. So be steady; do not play the beggar before the rich for nothing. Look: a pot draws the same water from a well as from the ocean.
MEANING: Bhartrihari speaks to you from the world of money, where people change their tune when the wallet opens or closes. He tells you that whatever share of the world was meant for you, you will find it, whether you are in a desert or on a mountain. So do not stand before the rich like a beggar, begging for a scrap of respect. A pot draws the same water from a small well as it does from the ocean. Your worth is not in the size of the water, but in the pot itself.
FOR YOU: You serve flat whites for people who talk about their own futures. Let their talk pass through you like water through a pot. You hold your dignity in your hands, not in their hands.

## Day 6 [act] Mahabharata · Shanti Parva · Bhishma, Rajadharma
LINE: Make peace with other people's success, even when you lack it. The wise can enjoy good fortune that isn't theirs.
TRANSLATION: Bear the good fortune of others, even though it is not with you. Wise people know how to enjoy good fortune even when it belongs to someone else; good fortune drains away from the one who resents.
MEANING: The sage Kalakavrikshiya speaks to the deposed prince Kshemadarshi, who is grieving his lost kingdom and resenting the success of others.
