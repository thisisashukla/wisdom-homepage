## Day 3 [steady] Mahabharata · Shanti Parva · Shanti: Consolation of Yudhishthira
LINE: Gain and loss, joy and sorrow come to everyone in turn. Don't be crushed, and don't get carried away.
TRANSLATION: Happiness and sorrow, rise and fall, gain and loss, death and life: everyone receives all of these in turn. So do not be thrown off by them, and do not get carried away either.
MEANING: Vyasa tells the grieving king Yudhishthira a story about a man named Senajit. Senajit has lost his wealth and his family. He sits in the dust, refusing to eat or drink. Senajit believes his life is over. But his minister reminds him that the wheel of the world turns. Gains become losses, and losses become gains. The minister asks him to look at his own body. It is made of the same elements that once made up his dead children and wealth. Nothing truly ends. What is gone is only rearranged.
FOR YOU: You moved for your son's future, and that choice was real. But the present you are driving through at night is also real. The loss of your project is part of this world's turning. You do not have to give up the father you are to protect the son you love.

## Day 6 [act] Bhagavad Gita 2.47
LINE: Your work is yours to do; the results are not yours to hold. Don't let that make you stop.
TRANSLATION: Your right is to the work alone, never to its fruits. Do not let the fruit be your motive, and do not cling to doing nothing.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna on the battlefield, who is frozen by grief and confusion over fighting his own kin.
