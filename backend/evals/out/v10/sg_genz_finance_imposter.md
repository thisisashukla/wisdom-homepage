## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 18.48
LINE: Don't abandon work that suits you just because it has flaws. Every undertaking has some, like fire has smoke.
TRANSLATION: One should not give up the work suited to one's nature, Kaunteya, even if it has faults; for all undertakings are clouded by faults, as fire is by smoke.
MEANING: Krishna tells Arjuna that no work is flawless. He says you could not abandon the work that suits your nature just because it has faults. Every undertaking is clouded by flaws, just as fire is always surrounded by smoke. The point is to do the work that fits you, with all its imperfections, as an offering.
FOR YOU: You are rehearsing your life before you live it. Krishna is telling you to let the smoke of your over-preparing be there, and still show up for the work that is yours, without demanding it be perfect first.

## Day 4 [steady] Mahabharata · Udyoga Parva · Vidula's counsel
LINE: Don't think less of yourself or settle for little. Set your mind on something good, and stop fearing.
TRANSLATION: Do not hold yourself in contempt, and do not keep it going on too little. Set your mind on what is truly good; don't be afraid. Pull yourself together.
MEANING: Vidula finds her son Sanjaya lying on the ground after losing his kingdom, defeated and ready to surrender. She tells him not to hold himself in contempt or settle for a small life. She asks him to set his mind on what is truly good and to pull himself together. She is saying that a loss is an event, not a verdict on who he is. It is a call to stand up and act, not to shrink from what he has become.
FOR YOU: You are in the room where the promotion sits, real and done, and you are rehearsing a speech to prove you belong there. Try setting your mind on the work itself instead of the verdict you keep passing on your HDB roots. Let the facts of the day hold you for a moment.

## Day 5 [act] Mahabharata · Vana Parva · Vyadha Gita
LINE: Turn to doing good after a wrong, and you come out like the moon from behind a cloud.
TRANSLATION: If a person who has done wrong turns to doing good, he is freed from all his wrongs, like the moon coming out from behind a great cloud.
MEANING: In Mithila, a meat-seller named Dharmavyadha teaches a proud ascetic named Kaushika about true virtue. The ascetic has come looking for great wisdom, but the butcher offers something simpler. He says that if a person turns to doing good after a wrong, they are freed from it, just as the moon comes out from behind a cloud. The butcher's point is that wisdom is not in grand gestures but in ordinary work done honestly, like caring for your parents.
FOR YOU: You have turned your hard work into a promotion, and now you are doing good in your job. Let your over-preparation be your honest work, not a mask to hide behind. Trust that your background does not cancel the good you are doing now.

## Day 6 [act] Bhagavad Gita 3.35
LINE: Better your own path done imperfectly than someone else's done perfectly. Borrowed lives are dangerous.
TRANSLATION: Better is one's own duty, even done imperfectly, than the duty of another well done. Better is death in one's own duty; the duty of another is full of fear.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna on the battlefield. Arjuna is caught between two ways of living: following his own nature, which feels risky and imperfect, or taking on a role that looks better from the outside but does not fit him. Krishna tells him that walking his own path, even if he stumbles, is safer and truer than trying to live someone else's life perfectly. The borrowed role is full of fear because it asks you to perform a part that is not yours.
FOR YOU: You are rehearsing a part that was never written for you. The promotion was real, but you are still trying to play someone else's script. Let your own work, however imperfect, be enough. You do not need to over-prepare to prove you belong in the room.
