## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 18.48
LINE: Don't abandon work that suits you just because it has flaws. Every undertaking has some, like fire has smoke.
TRANSLATION: One should not give up the work suited to one's nature, Kaunteya, even if it has faults; for all undertakings are clouded by faults, as fire is by smoke.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna on the battlefield. Arjuna is hesitating, wondering if he should abandon his duty as a warrior because it is bloody and painful. Krishna tells him that no work is perfect. Every path has flaws, just as fire always has smoke. The work that fits your own nature is still your work, even with its smoke. You do not throw it away because of the smoke. You do it as an offering, flaws and all.
FOR YOU: The fire you feel is your own, not borrowed from a salary or a title. The work you chose to build has its own smoke. Keep doing it as an offering. Let the flaws be part of the work, not a reason to walk away.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Yoga Vasishtha
LINE: Effort brings results, sooner or later, depending on time and place. That timing is what we call fate.
TRANSLATION: Results come from effort, sooner or later, depending on place and time. That arrival of the fruit, early or late, is what people call fate.
MEANING: Vasishtha is teaching the young Rama that what people call fate is only the fruit of effort, delayed by time and place. He tells Rama that his past actions shaped his present, and his present actions will shape his future. The timing of results is not magic. It is simply the natural arrival of what you have already planted through your own work.
FOR YOU: You quit your job and the fire is real. But the mind will start watching for the prize instead of the work. Effort brings results, sooner or later, depending on time and place. That timing is what we call fate. Your past effort shaped this morning. Today's effort shapes tomorrow.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: Let them praise or blame, let money come or go: the steady don't step off the right path.
TRANSLATION: Let the worldly-wise blame or praise; let fortune come or go as she pleases; let death come today or ages hence: the steadfast do not move a step from the path of what is right.
MEANING: Bhartrihari tells the reader to let the world praise or blame, let money come or go, and let death come today or ages hence. The steadfast do not step off the right path. He says setbacks rarely last for those who keep going on the right path, because a struck ball bounces back and a cut tree grows again.
FOR YOU: You are building your brand right now, and that path is already right. Let the praise and the doubt come and go. The hard weeks are just noise around a path that is already yours.
