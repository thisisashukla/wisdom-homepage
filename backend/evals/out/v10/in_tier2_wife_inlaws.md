## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 2.7
LINE: I'm confused about what's right. Tell me plainly what's best; I'm ready to learn.
TRANSLATION: My nature is stricken by weakness and my mind is confused about what is right. I ask you: tell me for certain what is best for me. I am your student; I have come to you, teach me.
MEANING: Arjuna has collapsed on the battlefield and refuses to fight. Krishna asks him why he is giving in to this weakness when he has always been a warrior. Arjuna admits his mind is confused about what is right, that he does not know which choice is better, and he asks Krishna to teach him plainly what is best for him.
FOR YOU: You have always been the one who knows the numbers and the way forward, yet now you are being told your practice will end. You do not need to know the exact words to say to your in-laws today, only to admit that you are confused and to ask for the clarity you need.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Bhagavad Gita 3.35
LINE: Better your own path done imperfectly than someone else's done perfectly. Borrowed lives are dangerous.
TRANSLATION: Better is one's own duty, even done imperfectly, than the duty of another well done. Better is death in one's own duty; the duty of another is full of fear.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna on the battlefield just before the war begins. Arjuna is paralyzed by doubt about whether fighting his own kin is right. Krishna tells him that the problem is not the action itself, but that Arjuna is trying to live a role that is not his own. The verse says that your own duty, even if done imperfectly, is better than the duty of another done perfectly.
FOR YOU: You built your CA practice from nothing. That is your path. The idea that you could step away for the baby is not your own duty. It is a borrowed one. You do not have to accept a smaller role just because it is offered.

## Day 6 [act] Bhagavad Gita 17.15
LINE: Speak words that don't wound, that are true, kind and helpful. That is discipline of speech.
TRANSLATION: Speech that causes no distress, that is truthful, pleasant and beneficial, and the regular study of sacred texts: this is called austerity of speech.
MEANING: Krishna tells Arjuna that true discipline of speech means saying words that are true, kind and helpful, without wounding anyone. It is not about hiding your thoughts or keeping quiet to stay safe. It is about choosing your words with care, so they build up rather than tear down. In the midst of a hard conversation, this is the path. It asks you to speak the truth you could hear, but to wrap it in a way that does not break the person in front of you.
FOR YOU: When you speak to your partner tonight, let the words be true and kind, but do not let them carry the weight of your silence. Speak what is real, without cruelty, and let the truth stand on its own.
