## Day 2 [diagnose] Bhagavad Gita 2.62
LINE: Dwell on something and you get attached; attachment becomes wanting, and blocked wanting turns into anger.
TRANSLATION: When a person dwells on objects, attachment to them is born. From attachment comes desire, and from desire, anger.
MEANING: Arjuna asks how a steady person moves through the world. Krishna answers that this steadiness is not a gift but a craft, built by guarding where you place your mind. The mind follows what you feed it. If you keep returning to what is missing or what you want to control, that focus becomes a knot. When the wanting is blocked, the knot tightens into anger. The anger does not come from the outside event. It comes from the mind already full of wanting, and the attention that has been dwelling there.
FOR YOU: You manage twelve people at your practice without a raised voice, because your mind is on the work. At home, the mind is already full. The spilled milk is not the cause. The wanting that could not be met is.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhagavad Gita 6.7
LINE: Self-mastered and calm, you stay centred in heat and cold, pleasure and pain, praise and insult.
TRANSLATION: For one who has conquered himself and is at peace, the higher self stays steady in cold and heat, pleasure and pain, honour and dishonour.
MEANING: Krishna tells Arjuna that a person can be their own best friend or their own worst enemy. The self-mastered person stays steady in heat and cold, pleasure and pain, honour and insult. The teaching is that you can lift yourself by yourself. You are the one who watches the storm, and the child is watching that self.
FOR YOU: The child is watching the part of you that can pause. That is the inheritance you want to leave. When the heat rises again, let the part that watches the storm speak first. The child sees that steadiness, not the snap.

## Day 4 [steady] Ramcharitmanas · Ayodhya Kand
LINE: Gain and loss, meeting and parting come like day and night. The steady hold both alike.
TRANSLATION: Birth and death, joy and sorrow, loss and gain, meeting and parting with those we love: these come under the sway of time and action, as surely as night and day. The foolish rejoice in good times and wail in bad; the steady hold both alike in the mind. Hold on to your courage, think with discernment, and let go of worry, you who care for everyone.
MEANING: Sumantra, the charioteer, comes back to the grieving Dasharath and speaks to him about the nature of the world. He tells him that birth and death, joy and sorrow, loss and gain, and meeting and parting come like day and night, entirely under the sway of time and action. The foolish rejoice in good times and wail in bad. The steady hold both alike in the mind. He asks the king to hold on to his courage, think with discernment, and let go of worry.
FOR YOU: You hold that the self that watches the storm is the one that remains, and the child is watching that self. The snap hurt, but the steadiness you showed the twelve people at work is the same steadiness that can sit with your child now.

## Day 5 [act] Valmiki Ramayana · Sundara Kanda
LINE: Blessed are those who use good sense to put out rising anger, the way water puts out fire.
TRANSLATION: Blessed indeed are the great-hearted who, with good sense, check anger the moment it rises, as water puts out a blazing fire.
MEANING: After setting Lanka ablaze in fury, Hanuman fears he has harmed Sita and turns on his own anger. He reflects that those who use good sense to put out rising anger are blessed, the way water puts out fire. He recognizes that anger can win a small fight and ruin the real goal.
FOR YOU: You try the pause: one breath, one step away, before the word comes out. The fire you felt was real, but the water you carry inside is the one that can put it out. Let that be the small thing you do today.

## Day 6 [act] Katharudra Upanishad 8
LINE: Don't wear out your body chasing pleasure or fleeing pain. Praised, don't gloat; blamed, don't curse others.
TRANSLATION: Let him not torment the body too much for the sake of pleasure or pain. Praised, let him not gloat; blamed, let him not curse others.
MEANING: A teacher tells a renunciant how to live. He says do not push the body too hard to get pleasure or run from pain. When praised, do not swell with pride. When blamed, do not throw your words at others. The teacher is saying that your body and mind are not meant to chase after every feeling or push away every hard moment. The real task is to keep your reactions inside your own chest instead of letting them fly out as blame.
FOR YOU: When your child spills the milk, let your body feel the sting of the moment without letting it turn into blame. You can hold the sting and still keep your words inside.

## Day 7 [story] Chirakari, the one who acts slowly
LINE: Ordered by his furious father to kill his mother, Chirakari did what he always did: he thought about it.
FOR YOU: Chirakari did not move fast when his father ordered him to kill his mother. He let the words sit and cool. You can let your own rage sit for one breath before it reaches your child. The pause is how you keep your mother's face from appearing in your child's eyes.
STORY: In a jealous rage, the sage Gautama orders his son Chirakari to kill his mother and storms off. Chirakari, famous for slowness, weighs a father's word against a mother's worth for so long that Gautama returns, regretful, and finds them both alive.
LESSON: In anger, and when loved ones seem to have wronged you, slow down.
