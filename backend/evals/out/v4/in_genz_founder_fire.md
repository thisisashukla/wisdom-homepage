## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 18.48
LINE: Don't abandon work that suits you just because it has flaws. Every undertaking has some, like fire has smoke.
TRANSLATION: One should not give up the work suited to one's nature, Kaunteya, even if it has faults; for all undertakings are clouded by faults, as fire is by smoke.
MEANING: Krishna speaks to Arjuna on the battlefield, knowing they are afraid to act. He tells them that the work which fits their nature will always have its rough edges. He compares these flaws to smoke around a fire, saying it is not a reason to walk away. He asks them to stay with the work that belongs to them, offering it fully even with its imperfections.
FOR YOU: You are building your own brand, and it is yours. The days will not always feel electric. When the hard days come and the work feels heavy, do not abandon it. Stay with the work that fits you, flaws and all, and offer it to the life you are making.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Yoga Vasishtha
LINE: Effort brings results, sooner or later, depending on time and place. That timing is what we call fate.
TRANSLATION: Results come from effort, sooner or later, depending on place and time. That arrival of the fruit, early or late, is what people call fate.
MEANING: Vasishtha speaks to the young Rama, who is grieving his fate. He tells him that what people call fate is only the result of past effort, arriving on its own schedule. He says Rama should not torment himself over what was never in his hands, but trust that his present effort is already shaping tomorrow.
FOR YOU: Yesterday's verse named the grip. Today, trust the work. The money and the results are coming, but not on your clock. Let go of the anxious need to control the outcome, and trust that your effort is already building the foundation.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: Let them praise or blame, let money come or go: the steady don't step off the right path.
TRANSLATION: Let the worldly-wise blame or praise; let fortune come or go as she pleases; let death come today or ages hence: the steadfast do not move a step from the path of what is right.
MEANING: Bhartrihari speaks to the reader from a place of deep steadiness, telling them to let the world praise or blame, let fortune come or go. He says the steadfast do not step off the right path, no matter what happens. He compares them to the gods who kept churning the ocean through poison to find nectar.
FOR YOU: Let the money come or go, let people praise or blame. You are building something new, and the path will have its rough patches. Keep going on the path that is yours. The steady work will carry you through.

## Day 4 [steady] Mahabharata · Vana Parva · Vana: Draupadi and Yudhishthira
LINE: Don't despair because effort hasn't paid off yet. Every attempt can go either way.
TRANSLATION: 'I keep working and still don't succeed' is no reason to despair. Every effort can end two ways, in success or not; only by not acting is the outcome certain.
MEANING: Draupadi speaks to Yudhishthira in the forest. They have lost their kingdom and sit in waiting. She tells him that waiting for fate is no plan. She says that saying 'I keep working and still don't succeed' is no reason to despair. Every effort can end two ways, in success or not. Only by not acting is the outcome certain. She asks him to do right for its own sake, then work as if it all depends on him.
FOR YOU: Your fire is real and it belongs to you. The days will come when the work feels heavy and the money does not show up. When that happens, do not step off the path because the result is missing. Do the work because it is yours, and let the outcome take care of itself.

## Day 5 [act] Bhagavad Gita 2.47
LINE: Your work is yours to do; the results are not yours to hold. Don't let that make you stop.
TRANSLATION: Your right is to the work alone, never to its fruits. Do not let the fruit be your motive, and do not cling to doing nothing.
MEANING: Krishna speaks to Arjuna on the battlefield, where Arjuna is frozen by grief and doubt about the war ahead. Krishna tells him to drop the obsession with victory or defeat and simply do the work before him. The verse proposes that you have a right only to the action itself, not to the outcome. The reward belongs to the doing, not to the winning.
FOR YOU: You are building something new. Do the one small thing that moves your brand forward today. Let go of checking if it is working yet.

## Day 6 [act] Mahabharata · Udyoga Parva · Vidura Niti
LINE: Decide, then begin. Don't stall halfway, don't waste time, and keep yourself steady.
TRANSLATION: One who begins only after deciding, does not stop halfway through the work, does not waste time and keeps himself in hand, is called wise.
MEANING: Vidura, the king's wise brother, speaks to the sleepless King Dhritarashtra on the eve of a peace talk. Vidura tells him that a wise person decides calmly and then acts without stopping halfway or wasting time. The verse proposes that real wisdom is steady action after a clear choice, without stalling.
FOR YOU: Yesterday's verse named the grip on results. Today, decide what you will build tomorrow and begin. Do not let the day pass without one steady step.

## Day 7 [story] Hanuman's leap

FOR YOU: Yesterday's verse named the grip. Today, look at the fire inside you. Hanuman did not stop to rest on the golden mountain because he knew his leap was his own promise. When the work feels hard, let your steadiness be the mountain you build on, not the comfort you refuse.
