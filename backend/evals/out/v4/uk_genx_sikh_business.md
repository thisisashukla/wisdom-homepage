## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 18.47
LINE: Better your own path, imperfectly walked, than someone else's done perfectly.
TRANSLATION: Better is one's own duty, though imperfect, than the duty of another well performed. Doing the work born of one's own nature, one incurs no wrong.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna on the field. Arjuna is frozen, unable to choose. Krishna points to the work that lives in a person's own nature. The work you are born to do, the work that is truly yours, is better for you than the work of another, even if yours is imperfect. You will not incur any wrong by doing it. This is not about a fixed role you were born into. It is about the honest fit between your own nature and your actions.
FOR YOU: The business you run is your own. When your father steps in, you feel the ground shift. This week, you can begin to see the business as yours again. Do your own work, imperfectly walked, and let it be your own offering.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Ramcharitmanas · Sundar Kand
LINE: When advisers, doctors and teachers only say what pleases you, the ruin comes quickly.
TRANSLATION: When a minister, a doctor and a teacher speak only to please, out of fear or hope of reward, then the kingdom, the body and righteousness are soon destroyed.
MEANING: Tulsidas is writing in the Sundar Kand of the Ramcharitmanas. Ravana is in his court, surrounded by ministers who tell him only what he wants to hear. Tulsidas warns that when advisers speak only to please, out of fear or hope of reward, ruin comes quickly. This is a warning about the circle you keep. The silence you keep is often because you are surrounded by voices that flatter the person in charge rather than the truth of the room. The ruin comes when no one tells you what you actually need to hear.
FOR YOU: Yesterday's verse named the grip of the business as your father's. Today, look at the circle around you. Are the voices in your life telling you the truth, or are they pleasing someone else? The ruin comes when the truth is silenced in the room.

LINE: Don't wear out your body chasing pleasure or fleeing pain. Praised, don't gloat; blamed, don't curse others.
TRANSLATION: Let him not torment the body too much for the sake of pleasure or pain. Praised, let him not gloat; blamed, let him not curse others.
MEANING: A teacher instructs a renunciant on the rules of conduct for a life of withdrawal. Do not torment the body by chasing pleasure or fleeing pain. When praised, do not gloat. When blamed, do not curse others. The verse proposes that a person should stop exhausting themselves in the push and pull of feeling good and feeling bad. It asks for a steadiness that does not depend on praise or blame. It offers a way to hold on to something solid while the hurt is still there.
