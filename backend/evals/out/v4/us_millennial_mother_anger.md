## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 2.62
LINE: Dwell on something and you get attached; attachment becomes wanting, and blocked wanting turns into anger.
TRANSLATION: When a person dwells on objects, attachment to them is born. From attachment comes desire, and from desire, anger.
MEANING: Arjuna asks what a steady person looks like. Krishna begins by tracing the mind backward from anger to its root. A person dwells on an object. That dwelling breeds attachment. Attachment becomes wanting. When that wanting is blocked, it turns to anger. The storm at home is not the child. It is the mind dwelling on what it does not have, until the door closes and the wanting has nowhere left to go.
FOR YOU: Anger is a passing fire in the mind; you can watch it rise and fall without letting it speak for you.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Bhagavad Gita 3.37
LINE: It's desire, which turns into anger when blocked. Never satisfied, it is the real enemy here.
TRANSLATION: The Blessed Lord said: It is desire, it is anger, born of the quality of passion; all-devouring and greatly harmful. Know this to be the enemy here.
MEANING: Krishna tells Arjuna that desire is the root. When it is blocked, it turns into anger. This anger is the real enemy, all-devouring and harmful. It clouds the mind and makes a person say things they later regret. It is the force that drives a person to act against their own will.
FOR YOU: Today, see that the temper is blocked wanting, not the child. The mind clouds itself the moment the day offers nothing back. You can name the wanting and let it pass. You do not have to let it become anger. The wanting is the enemy, not the person in front of you.
