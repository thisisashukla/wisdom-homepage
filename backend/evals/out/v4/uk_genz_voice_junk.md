## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 6.34
LINE: The mind is restless, stormy, stubborn and strong. Holding it feels like holding the wind.
TRANSLATION: For the mind is restless, Krishna, turbulent, powerful and stubborn. I think it is as hard to hold as the wind.
MEANING: Arjuna is at the edge of his strength. They have laid down their bow and told Krishna that the mind is too restless, too powerful to hold. It feels like trying to catch the wind. Krishna does not argue. They agree that the mind is stubborn and hard to control. But Krishna says that through practice and by letting go, the mind can be trained. It is not a promise that the storm will stop, but that you can learn to stand in it without being swept away.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Yoga Vasishtha
LINE: The mind pounces on a task like a bird on food, then drops it like a bored child's toy.
TRANSLATION: The mind swoops onto tasks like a bird onto meat, and a moment later loses interest, like a child with a toy.
MEANING: Rama speaks to the sages Vasishtha and Vishvamitra. He has been trying to rule and has seen how hollow it all feels. He tells them his mind behaves like a bird. It swoops onto a task for a quick bite, then flies off without a thought. A moment later it is gone, leaving the task behind like a bored child tossing aside a toy. This is the truth of a mind left to its own devices. It does not stay long enough to finish anything, and it never fills.
FOR YOU: Your phone does the same thing. It offers a quick bite of a video and then drops it. Notice that empty feeling when you close the app. That emptiness is the bird, not the exam. Name it as emptiness before you open the next screen.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhagavad Gita 2.58
LINE: Like a tortoise pulling in its limbs, draw your senses back from what pulls at them.
TRANSLATION: When, like a tortoise drawing in its limbs on every side, a person withdraws the senses from their objects, his wisdom is firmly set.
MEANING: Krishna answers Arjuna. He asks what a steady person looks like. Krishna says a steady person is like a tortoise. When the world gets loud, the tortoise pulls its limbs inside. It keeps its body safe. In the same way, when you draw your senses back from what pulls at them, your wisdom holds. Steadiness is not about doing more. It is about letting go of the pull. That quiet pull-back is where your mind begins to rest.
FOR YOU: Today, when the phone calls for your attention, try pulling one sense back. Close one app and leave the room. That one quiet minute is your tortoise shell. It is enough to begin.

LINE: Without understanding, the senses bolt like wild horses; with a steady mind, they run like trained ones.
TRANSLATION: For one who lacks understanding, whose mind is never held steady, the senses run out of control like a driver's wild horses. For one who has understanding, whose mind is always steady, the senses obey like good horses.
MEANING: Yama is still speaking to Nachiketa about the chariot that carries the self. He says the senses are the horses. When a person has no understanding of what is real, the senses run wild like horses with no reins. They bolt in every direction, driven by desire.
