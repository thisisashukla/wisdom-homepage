## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 6.34
LINE: The mind is restless, stormy, stubborn and strong. Holding it feels like holding the wind.
TRANSLATION: For the mind is restless, Krishna, turbulent, powerful and stubborn. I think it is as hard to hold as the wind.
MEANING: Arjuna is on the chariot with Krishna. He has laid down his bow, and he is telling Krishna that his mind is running away from him. He says it is turbulent, stubborn and powerful. He cannot hold it. Krishna answers that it is difficult, but through practice and letting go, the mind can be trained. Your mind is doing exactly what Arjuna described. It is running ahead of you, carrying the weight of your daughter’s question and the noise of the office. It feels like you are trying to cup water in your hands.
FOR YOU: You are not failing. You are simply trying to hold a mind that is already running ahead of you. When you sit with your daughter, let the guilt be there, but do not let it steer you. You are allowed to be only half present for a moment. That is the practice.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: Same person, same mind, same voice. Lose money, and people suddenly treat you as someone else.
TRANSLATION: The same faculties, unimpaired; the same name; the same unclouded mind; the same speech. Yet a man deprived of the warmth of wealth becomes, in a moment, someone else. How strange!
MEANING: Bhartrihari, a poet who saw the world turn on money, speaks to the reader. He says that when a man loses his wealth, the same faculties remain, the same mind stays, the same voice speaks. Yet people suddenly treat him as a stranger. The world changes its face, not the man himself. This is the strange power of money: it changes how others see you, not who you are.
FOR YOU: When your worth depends on others' praise, every word of blame shakes you; craving approval keeps the mind unsteady.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhagavad Gita 12.19
LINE: Praise and blame weigh the same. Quiet, content with what comes, rooted in steadiness, not status.
TRANSLATION: to whom blame and praise are equal, who is silent, content with whatever comes, without a fixed home, steady in mind and full of devotion, that person is dear to me.
MEANING: Krishna speaks to Arjuna about the person dear to God. He says that to whom blame and praise are equal, who is silent, content with whatever comes, without a fixed home, steady in mind, that person is dear. This is the mark of true steadiness. It is not about never asking for credit. It is about not letting the world's approval define you.
FOR YOU: You hold that praise and blame are not your truth, and your love is not made smaller by the hours you cannot give. The praise of a perfect mother is a cloud. The blame of a missed event is a storm. You are steady in the middle.

## Day 4 [steady] Katharudra Upanishad 8
LINE: Don't wear out your body chasing pleasure or fleeing pain. Praised, don't gloat; blamed, don't curse others.
TRANSLATION: Let him not torment the body too much for the sake of pleasure or pain. Praised, let him not gloat; blamed, let him not curse others.
MEANING: A teacher speaks to a renunciant about the rules of his life. He tells him not to torment his body chasing pleasure or fleeing pain. If he is praised, he should not gloat. If he is blamed, he should not curse others. This is a life of steady balance, where praise and blame are met with the same quiet mind, rooted in what is real and not in what others think.
FOR YOU: You carry the praise of your team and the blame of your own mind like weights on your chest. Let the praise not make you proud, and let the blame not make you small. Your worth is not made of their words.

## Day 5 [act] Bhagavad Gita 3.35
LINE: Better your own path done imperfectly than someone else's done perfectly. Borrowed lives are dangerous.
TRANSLATION: Better is one's own duty, even done imperfectly, than the duty of another well done. Better is death in one's own duty; the duty of another is full of fear.
MEANING: Krishna speaks to Arjuna on the battlefield, urging him to act free of ego and imitation. He says it is better to walk your own path imperfectly than to walk another's perfectly. A borrowed life is full of fear, because it is not rooted in your own truth. Your own duty, however flawed, is the only one that can set you free.
FOR YOU: You are trying to be the mother your friend is, or the project manager your boss wants. Stop borrowing that life. Do your duty as a mother and as a manager with your own hands, and let the rest go. Give yourself the grace you give your daughter when she spills her milk.

## Day 6 [act] Mahabharata · Anushasana Parva · Anushasana
LINE: Start the day by greeting your mother and father. Small daily respect adds up to a good life.
TRANSLATION: Don't sleep past sunrise. On rising, first greet your mother and father, and your teacher or another elder; that is how a long life is won.
MEANING: Bhishma speaks to Yudhishthira on his deathbed, describing the small acts that make a life long and good. He says to greet your mother and father on rising, to be slow to anger, and to speak kindly. A good life is not built on grand deeds, but on the daily conduct of a gentle heart.
FOR YOU: You are the mother who greets her daughter each morning, even when you are tired. You are the manager who speaks kindly to her team, even when the pressure is high. These small acts are the roots of your life. They are enough, and they are real.

## Day 7 [story] Kausalya and the child who ran from dinner
LINE: Kausalya was shown the whole universe in her newborn, and all she wanted was for him to cry like a baby.
FOR YOU: You are trying to be both the whole universe and the mother who chases a dusty child. Let your daughter see the ordinary, present version of you. She does not need the grand version to know you are hers.
STORY: At his birth Ram shows Kausalya his cosmic form, and she asks him instead to be simply a baby.
