## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 2.40
LINE: No sincere effort here is ever wasted. Even a little of it shields you from great fear.
TRANSLATION: On this path no effort is lost and no harm results. Even a little of this practice protects one from great fear.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna as the two stand on the battlefield. Arjuna is paralyzed by doubt and grief, unable to lift his bow. Krishna is telling him to act, to fight, without clinging to what will happen next. The verse says that no sincere effort ever goes to waste. Even a small amount of this practice, this giving yourself fully to your part while letting go of the result, protects you from great fear.
FOR YOU: You quit your job and put everything into this brand. That is the work. The fear you feel is the old life fading. Keep going. Your effort is already shielding you.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Yoga Vasishtha
LINE: Effort brings results, sooner or later, depending on time and place. That timing is what we call fate.
TRANSLATION: Results come from effort, sooner or later, depending on place and time. That arrival of the fruit, early or late, is what people call fate.
MEANING: Vasishtha is teaching the young Rama that the world is not ruled by some hidden force. He says that what people call fate is only the result of past effort. The fruit of your work comes sooner or later, depending on the time and the place. Your present effort shapes tomorrow, so do not grieve over what was never in your hands.
FOR YOU: You have left the safety of a job to build something yours. Today, let the quiet moments remind you that your effort is already shaping tomorrow, even if the results are not here yet.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: Let them praise or blame, let money come or go: the steady don't step off the right path.
TRANSLATION: Let the worldly-wise blame or praise; let fortune come or go as she pleases; let death come today or ages hence: the steadfast do not move a step from the path of what is right.
MEANING: Bhartrihari speaks to the reader from a place of steady resolve. He tells them that praise and blame from others, the coming and going of money, and the timing of death are all passing things. What matters is the path of what is right. The steady do not move a step from it no matter what happens. He says setbacks rarely last for those who keep going on the right path. This is the truth underneath your own early days.
FOR YOU: Your work right now is to keep going on the path you chose when you quit your job. The money has not come yet, and people will talk. Let them praise or blame. Stay on the path. The steady do not step off it.

## Day 4 [steady] Mahabharata · Vana Parva · Vana: Draupadi and Yudhishthira
LINE: Don't despair because effort hasn't paid off yet. Every attempt can go either way.
TRANSLATION: 'I keep working and still don't succeed' is no reason to despair. Every effort can end two ways, in success or not; only by not acting is the outcome certain.
MEANING: Draupadi, who has lost her kingdom, stands in the forest and questions Yudhishthira, the eldest, about why the righteous suffer. She asks how he can keep his faith when his effort has led only to exile and loss. Yudhishthira answers that he acts because it is right, not for reward, and that effort still matters. The verse tells us that every attempt can end in success or not, and only by not acting is the outcome certain.
FOR YOU: Some mornings the work will feel like a gamble with no prize. That is the test. Do what you do because it is yours, not because the next sale is already in the bag.
