## Day 1 [hook] Valmiki Ramayana, Sundara 38.32
LINE: I have someone who is mine, and still I feel like a person with no one at all.
TRANSLATION: Show me your great-hearted kindness, best of men. I have you, my protector, and yet I look like someone who has no one.
MEANING: Sita, held in Lanka, sends this message to Ram through Hanuman. She says she has him, her protector, and yet she looks like someone with no one at all. She is not denying he exists or that she loves him. She is naming a real ache: being with someone in spirit and feeling utterly alone. The verse proposes that love can be real and present, and still leave a person feeling abandoned. It holds the truth of your waiting without calling it wrong.
FOR YOU: You have lived with this waiting for nine years. The verse shows that love can exist and still leave you feeling alone. Your heart knows the truth of this. The waiting is real, and it is not a failing.

LINE: In grief, ask: where did I come from, where am I going, who am I? Let that steady you.
TRANSLATION: 'Where was I before? Where will I go? Who am I, really, and why am I here? Why should I grieve, and for what?' Ask these, and steady the mind.
MEANING: The sage Ashma speaks to King Janaka, who has lost family and wealth. Ashma says to ask where you came from, where you are going, and who you are in the midst of this loss. He is not asking you to forget the grief. He is asking you to steady the mind with questions about what remains true. The idea is that loss is part of the path, but it does not define the whole of you.
FOR YOU: Your mind is keeping the bond alive by waiting. The verse suggests asking who you are beyond this waiting. The bond is real, but you are also the one who remains. Let the waiting rest in the larger truth of who you are.
