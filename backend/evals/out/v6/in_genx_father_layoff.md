## Day 1 [hook] Valmiki Ramayana · Ayodhya Kanda
LINE: What hurt most was not the news, but that he didn't tell me himself.
TRANSLATION: Only one sorrow burns my heart: that the king did not tell me himself about Bharata's coronation.
MEANING: Ram has just accepted fourteen years of exile. On the morning he was to be crowned king, Kaikeyi demanded he leave for the forest. He agreed without anger, but one wound still burns. He says he is not hurt by the news itself, but by the fact that his father did not tell him himself. The verse says you cannot control the news that comes to you, but you can control the person who walks out of the room.
FOR YOU: The ten-minute call ended your role. But the wake-up at six is your body still trusting the person who held it. The hurt is not the loss. It is that they did not tell you themselves.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: Same person, same mind, same voice. Lose money, and people suddenly treat you as someone else.
TRANSLATION: The same faculties, unimpaired; the same name; the same unclouded mind; the same speech. Yet a man deprived of the warmth of wealth becomes, in a moment, someone else. How strange!
MEANING: Bhartrihari looks at the reader and points out a strange human habit. He says a man can lose all his wealth, yet his mind, his voice, his name remain exactly the same. The world, however, suddenly treats him as someone else entirely. The verse says money changes how others see you, not who you are. It asks you to give it, enjoy it, or lose it, and to see the person underneath the loss.
FOR YOU: Twenty-two years in that company made you someone they knew. Now that the money is gone, they see a stranger. The person inside has not changed. The wake-up at six is your body still believing the old name.

## Day 4 [steady] Ashtavakra Gita 18.81
LINE: Not begging for gain, not grieving loss: a steady mind stays cool and full.
TRANSLATION: He does not pray for gain, nor grieve over loss. The steady one's cool mind is filled as if with nectar.
