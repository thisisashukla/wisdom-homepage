## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 6.26
LINE: Wherever the restless mind wanders, gently bring it back. Again and again.
TRANSLATION: Wherever the restless, unsteady mind wanders, from there one should draw it back and bring it under the control of the self.
MEANING: Krishna is showing Arjuna how to settle the mind when it refuses to stay still. The mind is restless by nature, and it will run off toward noise and worry. The instruction is not to fight it or punish it. It is to notice where it went and gently bring it back, again and again. This is the work of settling. It is not about forcing silence, but about returning, as if guiding a wandering animal home.
FOR YOU: You sit down to read and your mind is already on Instagram. The guilt follows, heavy and loud. You do not need to force the thoughts away. Just notice where they went, and bring your attention back to the page. Do this again and again, without scolding yourself.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Maha Upanishad 3.18
LINE: The mind runs here and there for nothing, wandering far like a stray village dog.
TRANSLATION: The mind runs here and there, restless and pointless; it wanders far away, like a dog in a village.
MEANING: Nidagha is telling Ribhu about his wandering mind after coming back from a pilgrimage. He says the mind runs here and there for no reason, like a stray village dog. He is not angry at the dog. He is just naming what it does. The mind runs because it is looking for something to hold onto, even if that something is just a distraction. The verse proposes that the first step toward peace is to see the mind clearly, without fighting it or covering it up.
FOR YOU: You open Instagram when the guilt gets too loud, and then the guilt gets louder. The phone is not the enemy. It is where your mind goes to hide from the fear of not being good enough. Today, when you open the app again, just notice the dog running. Do not chase it.

## Day 3 [steady] Ashtavakra Gita 18.58
LINE: A restless mind is agitated even doing nothing; a skilled one stays calm doing everything.
TRANSLATION: The fool is restless even when doing nothing; the skilful one stays calm even when doing everything that needs doing.
MEANING: Ashtavakra spoke to Janaka about the calm of the wise. The wise person stays calm even when doing everything, while the fool is restless even when doing nothing. The truth is that calm is not a place you reach by running away from your life. It is the way you hold ordinary life, whether you are busy or still.
FOR YOU: Your mind feels restless right now, but that is just a habit, not a verdict on your worth. You are not broken. You are just a person who has never been taught how to rest, so your thoughts keep running long after you have asked them to stop.

TRANSLATION: Why do you wander about in vain, mind? Rest somewhere. Whatever is to happen happens just as it will, and not otherwise. Not recalling the past, not planning the future, I enjoy the pleasures that come to me unlooked-for.
