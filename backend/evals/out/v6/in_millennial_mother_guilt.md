## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 18.47
LINE: Better your own path, imperfectly walked, than someone else's done perfectly.
TRANSLATION: Better is one's own duty, though imperfect, than the duty of another well performed. Doing the work born of one's own nature, one incurs no wrong.
MEANING: Krishna speaks to Arjuna on the battlefield of as Arjuna lays down his bow, saying he cannot kill his own kin. Krishna tells him that the path that is his own, born of his nature and duty, is the only one that will not betray him. He says that doing the work that fits you, with all its flaws, is better than taking up another's role and doing it perfectly. The work is the offering, and the offering is the path.
FOR YOU: You are trying to be a perfect mother and a perfect PM. The Gita says your own path, even when it is messy and split, is the only one that will not break you. Let your work at the office and your time with your children be your own offerings, not a borrowed script.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Mahabharata · Shanti Parva · Shanti: Consolation of Yudhishthira
LINE: Mental pain comes from two places: something you didn't want happened, or your mind is spinning. No third.
TRANSLATION: Sorrow of the mind has only two sources: a confused, wandering mind, or something unwanted happening to you. There is no third.
MEANING: The sage Ashma speaks to King Janaka, who is asking how to remain steady when everything around him shifts. Ashma tells him that sorrow of the mind comes from only two places. It is either something unwanted that has happened to him, or his own mind is spinning in confusion. There is no third source. The mind wanders when it does not know what belongs to it. Ashma says that days do not come back. Grieve what has passed, then steady yourself with the people still standing in front of you.
FOR YOU: Your guilt is not a third thing. It is either the pain of missing your daughter's school, or a mind spinning between two worlds. The sage says days do not return, so steady yourself with the people still in front of you.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: Let them praise or blame, let money come or go: the steady don't step off the right path.
TRANSLATION: Let the worldly-wise blame or praise; let fortune come or go as she pleases; let death come today or ages hence: the steadfast do not move a step from the path of what is right.
MEANING: Bhartrihari speaks to the reader about what it means to stay steady. He says that the wise do not let praise or blame, wealth or loss, or even death itself make them move from the right path. The steady person holds their ground. Setbacks rarely last for those who keep going. This means that your confusion about being half a mother and half a PM is a passing storm, not a permanent state. You are still on the right path.
FOR YOU: You can be both the mother at the school gate and the leader in the meeting. Neither half of your life is a failure, and the guilt is only a passing cloud, not the sky itself.

## Day 4 [steady] Bhagavad Gita 6.7
LINE: Self-mastered and calm, you stay centred in heat and cold, pleasure and pain, praise and insult.
TRANSLATION: For one who has conquered himself and is at peace, the higher self stays steady in cold and heat, pleasure and pain, honour and dishonour.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna on the battlefield, just before the great war begins. Arjuna is overwhelmed and ready to drop his weapons. Krishna is telling him that the one who has conquered themselves and is at peace can remain steady no matter what happens. The self-mastered person does not get thrown off by cold, heat, pain, or pleasure, and they do not lose their centre when people praise them or insult them.
FOR YOU: You do not have to choose between being a mother and being a professional. You can be your own best friend today and simply stay steady in the car, in the meeting, and at the dinner table without letting either half break you.

## Day 6 [act] Ashtavakra Gita 18.20
LINE: Don't cling to doing or to not doing. Do what's in front of you and stay at ease.
TRANSLATION: The steady person has no stubborn attachment to doing or to not doing. Doing whatever comes to be done, he stays at ease.
MEANING: Ashtavakra is speaking to Janaka about the calm of the wise. He says the steady person has no stubborn attachment to doing or not doing. They simply do whatever comes to be done and stay at ease. The point is that calm is not found by chasing it or changing places. It is how you hold ordinary life.
FOR YOU: Tomorrow morning, when you are getting your daughter ready, just do that. Do not try to also solve the work email in your head. Do what is in front of you and let the rest wait. You do not have to fix everything at once.

## Day 7 [story] Kausalya and the child who ran from dinner
LINE: Kausalya was shown the whole universe in her newborn, and all she wanted was for him to cry like a baby.
FOR YOU: Kausalya asked to be spared the grand form and to keep her child in the ordinary hours. Your daughter did not ask for a cosmic explanation of your absence, she asked for your presence in the small hours.
STORY: At his birth Ram shows Kausalya his cosmic form, and she asks him instead to be simply a baby. Later scenes are pure childhood: the boy runs off mid-meal, curd on his face, dusty from play, while his father laughs and his mother chases him. When she once glimpses the whole universe in him again, she asks only to be spared that and keep her child.
LESSON: The grandest thing about a child may be the ordinary hours of childhood itself.
