## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 9.22
LINE: Turn to me with your whole heart, and I will carry what you lack and guard what you have.
TRANSLATION: To those who think of me alone and worship me, ever devoted, I bring what they lack and preserve what they have.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna about two kinds of worship. One group worships with a list of demands. They want heaven, wealth, or safety, and they keep track of what they are owed. When the rewards run out, they have to come back. The other group worships with no list. They think of Krishna alone, and they give everything they have. Krishna says to this second group: I will carry what you lack and guard what you have. The idea is that trust takes the weight off the mind.
FOR YOU: You are home with your children right now, but you are not truly there. You are holding the shame of the snap and the fear of becoming someone you do not want to be.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Bhagavad Gita 2.62
LINE: Dwell on something and you get attached; attachment becomes wanting, and blocked wanting turns into anger.
TRANSLATION: When a person dwells on objects, attachment to them is born. From attachment comes desire, and from desire, anger.
MEANING: Krishna is telling Arjuna what holds a steady mind together. He says that when you dwell on something, you become attached to it. That attachment turns into wanting, and when wanting is blocked, it turns into anger. The work of steadiness is in where you let your mind rest.

Today, notice what you are dwelling on when the house is still. Is it the work that follows you home, or the feeling that you are not quite enough?

[sanskrit] अनन्याश्चिन्तयन्तो मां ये जनाः पर्युपासते, तेषां नित्याभियुक्तानां योगक्षेमं वहाम्यहम्.

It does not erase the work. It just removes the weight of having to secure everything yourself.
