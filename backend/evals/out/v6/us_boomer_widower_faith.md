## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 2.22
LINE: As we drop worn-out clothes and put on new ones, the self leaves a worn body for a new one.
TRANSLATION: Just as a person casts off worn-out clothes and puts on new ones, so the embodied self casts off worn-out bodies and enters others that are new.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna on the battlefield, who is frozen by grief. He tells Arjuna that the true self is not born and does not die. It simply sheds an old body the way a person discards worn-out garments and takes on a new one. The essence that moves through these changes remains untouched. This is the truth that outlasts every passing pain.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Yoga Vasishtha
LINE: Those days, those people, that wealth, those deeds are memories now. We too will pass soon.
TRANSLATION: Those days, those great people, that wealth, those deeds: all have passed into memory. We too will go in a moment.
MEANING: Rama speaks to the sages Vishvamitra and Vasishtha from his place of deep questioning. He lists everything that slips away. The days, the people we admired, the wealth, the deeds we thought mattered. All have passed into memory. We too will go in a moment. He is not offering a solution. He is naming the emptiness honestly. He is asking what remains when the story is over. The truth underneath is that what is real in us does not wear out like a cloth. It stays while the story changes.
FOR YOU: The evenings when the temple seva ends and the house is quiet are where this emptiness lives. Use that stillness to ask what remains in you after the years, the roles, the routines have all gone. The answer is not in where she is now. It is in what in you does not pass.

## Day 3 [steady] Katha Upanishad 1.2.22
LINE: Knowing what stays steady inside all that changes, the wise do not grieve.
TRANSLATION: Knowing the self as bodiless within bodies, steady among the unsteady, great and all-pervading, the wise one does not grieve.
MEANING: Yama, the god of death, speaks to Nachiketa, a young seeker who has come to understand what lies beyond the end. He says the true self is bodiless, steady among the unsteady, and all-pervading. It is not born, it does not die, and it is not killed when the body is. Knowing what stays steady inside all that changes, the wise do not grieve.
FOR YOU: The evenings you spend in seva are a quiet, honest life. In that steadiness, hold onto the knowledge that what you most are cannot be lost.
