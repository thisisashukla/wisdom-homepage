## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 18.47
LINE: Better your own path, imperfectly walked, than someone else's done perfectly.
TRANSLATION: Better is one's own duty, though imperfect, than the duty of another well performed. Doing the work born of one's own nature, one incurs no wrong.
MEANING: Arjuna is still on the battlefield, unsure of what to do. Krishna tells him that it is better to follow your own path, even if you stumble, than to walk someone else's perfectly. The work that comes from your own nature, done honestly, carries no fault. It is an offering of who you are, flaws and all.
FOR YOU: You love your father, and that love makes every argument feel like a test. But your worth does not have to wait for his approval to be real. Your own path, even in the middle of the fighting, is enough.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: Have money and suddenly you are wise, well-born and charming. All virtues seem to lodge in gold.
TRANSLATION: Whoever has wealth is well-born, learned, well-read and a judge of merit; he is the eloquent one, the handsome one. All virtues take shelter in gold.
MEANING: Bhartrihari writes that gold makes people look wise, well-born, and charming. All virtues seem to live inside money. It is a satire on how the world changes its tune when wealth appears, not a lesson on how to be rich.
FOR YOU: Your father sees the MBA as a stamp of success. But the world flatters money, not the person. The fight is about what he thinks is valuable, not what you actually are. You do not have to earn your worth in his eyes.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: Let them praise or blame, let money come or go: the steady don't step off the right path.
TRANSLATION: Let the worldly-wise blame or praise; let fortune come or go as she pleases; let death come today or ages hence: the steadfast do not move a step from the path of what is right.
MEANING: Bhartrihari is writing to you about what happens when praise or blame, or money, comes and goes. He says the steady person does not step off the right path. The world will praise you or blame you, and fortune will come and go, but you keep going. This is what steadfastness is. It is not about never feeling bad. It is about the path staying under your feet even when people are shouting at you. The idea is that setbacks rarely last for those who keep going on the right path.
FOR YOU: The kitchen cries and the living room fights are loud. But your path is your own. Let them praise or blame. You do not step off it. Your worth is not in their verdict.

## Day 4 [steady] Mahabharata · Udyoga Parva · Vidura Niti
LINE: Don't swell with praise or burn at a slight; stay deep and still like a river pool.
TRANSLATION: One who is not elated when honoured, nor pained when slighted, and who stays unruffled like a deep pool of the Ganga, is called wise.
MEANING: Vidura is speaking to King Dhritarashtra, who cannot sleep because he is worried about the coming war. Vidura tells him not to swell with praise or burn at a slight. He says a wise person stays unruffled like a deep pool of the Ganga. They do not get carried away by good words or bad words. They stay deep and still, and from that stillness they know what real happiness is made of.
FOR YOU: Your father dismisses your films and your mother cries in the kitchen. Let their words come and go. You do not have to burn at their slight or swell at their praise. You stay deep and still like a river pool, and you keep going.
