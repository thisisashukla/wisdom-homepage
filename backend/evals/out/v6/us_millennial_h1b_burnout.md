## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 6.34
LINE: The mind is restless, stormy, stubborn and strong. Holding it feels like holding the wind.
TRANSLATION: For the mind is restless, Krishna, turbulent, powerful and stubborn. I think it is as hard to hold as the wind.
MEANING: Arjuna is on the battlefield, and they have just admitted that their mind is running wild at two in the morning, checking work messages again and again. They tell Krishna that trying to control the mind feels like trying to catch the wind. Krishna does not deny how strong the mind is. He says that practice and letting go are what train it. The mind does not need to be crushed. It needs to be held steady, again and again, until it learns to rest.
FOR YOU: The phone at two in the morning is the wind. You cannot stop the thoughts, but you can stop answering them right now. Let the check wait until morning. That is how the mind begins to learn it is safe.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Vishnu Gita 1.98
LINE: Honour fears disgrace, virtue fears the wicked, power fears critics, learning fears debate. Every gain brings a fear.
TRANSLATION: In honour lies the fear of being brought low; in virtue, the fear of the wicked; in power, the fear of critics; in learning, the fear of rival debaters.
MEANING: The gods ask Vishnu how to be free from fear. Vishnu looks at what they value. He says that honour carries the fear of disgrace, power carries the fear of critics, and learning carries the fear of debate. Every gain they have built carries its own shadow of fear. The fear of losing the visa is not separate from the career they have built. It is the price of that gain. The only way out is to hold what they have lightly, without letting it own them.
FOR YOU: The terror of the rating is the price of the career you built. You do not have to pretend the rating does not matter. But you can notice that your fear is tied to the value of the thing you stand to lose. Holding that value lightly does not make you careless.

## Day 4 [steady] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: A ball knocked down bounces back up. For good people, setbacks rarely last.
TRANSLATION: A ball struck down by the hand flies right back up. As a rule, the misfortunes of those who live rightly do not last.
MEANING: Bhartrihari is writing to someone who reads these verses, and he is talking about what it means to stay steady. He says a ball that is knocked down by a hand will fly right back up. He means that when good people face a setback, it does not last. The hit does not end the game. This is not a promise that pain is always short, or that anyone who suffers long did something wrong. It is simply the truth about a life that keeps moving.
FOR YOU: The bad rating is a hit, but it does not end your game. Your part is to keep going on the path you are on. The rating is a number, and it cannot take the life you built. Hold on to that truth while the hurt is here.

## Day 5 [act] Bhagavad Gita 2.47
LINE: Your work is yours to do; the results are not yours to hold. Don't let that make you stop.
TRANSLATION: Your right is to the work alone, never to its fruits. Do not let the fruit be your motive, and do not cling to doing nothing.
MEANING: Krishna is telling Arjuna that they are not fighting this war for a prize. The right they have is simply to do the work, to stand in the chariot and aim the bow. The outcome, the victory or the loss, is not theirs to hold. It belongs to the larger order. The point is to let go of clinging to what comes next, because that clinging is what chains them to misery.
FOR YOU: You cannot control the rating that decides your visa. But you can control the next hour. Take one hour today, maybe the first one, and do only what is in front of you. Close the phone and let the result belong to the day, not to your fear.

## Day 6 [act] Bhagavad Gita 12.6
LINE: Lay your work in me and keep your mind on me; I soon lift you out of the drowning sea.
TRANSLATION: But those who lay all their actions in me, intent on me, and worship me meditating with single-minded yoga — for them, whose minds are set on me, I soon become the one who lifts them out of the ocean of death and rebirth, Partha.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna, who is standing in a chariot on a battlefield, and he is offering him a way out of the drowning sea. He says those who lay all their actions in him, with their minds set on him, are soon lifted out of the ocean of death and rebirth. The promise is not that the sea disappears, but that they will not drown in it alone.
FOR YOU: The phone is your sea right now. Try one hour today where you lay your work in something steady and close the phone. Let that hour be your small lift out of the drowning, just for now.
