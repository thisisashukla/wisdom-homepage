## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 18.47
LINE: Better your own path, imperfectly walked, than someone else's done perfectly.
TRANSLATION: Better is one's own duty, though imperfect, than the duty of another well performed. Doing the work born of one's own nature, one incurs no wrong.
MEANING: Arjuna stands on a battlefield, confused about which path to take. Krishna tells him that the right path is the one that comes from your own nature. It is better to walk your own path, even if you stumble, than to walk someone else's path perfectly. You do not carry a sin for doing the work that fits you.
FOR YOU: You quit your job last week because the work felt like air. That is your own nature speaking, and it is worth more than a perfect salary. Let the brand be a sketch. Do your own work, even when it is rough, and trust that the fire you feel is the right kind of heat.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Bhagavad Gita 5.12
LINE: Let go of the payoff and you find lasting peace. Chase it and it chains you.
TRANSLATION: The disciplined person, giving up the fruit of action, attains lasting peace. The undisciplined, attached to the fruit and driven by desire, is bound.
MEANING: Krishna speaks to Arjuna about how to act in the world. He says that a disciplined person finds lasting peace by giving up the payoff of their actions. The undisciplined person, driven by desire for results, becomes bound. It is about acting without letting the outcome stain your mind.
FOR YOU: You want to keep the fire when it gets hard. That means you cannot hold the brand's success in your hands every morning. Do the work because it is yours, and let the payoff sit on the other side of the field.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: Let them praise or blame, let money come or go: the steady don't step off the right path.
TRANSLATION: Let the worldly-wise blame or praise; let fortune come or go as she pleases; let death come today or ages hence: the steadfast do not move a step from the path of what is right.
MEANING: Bhartrihari is speaking to the reader about what it means to stay true. He says let the worldly-wise blame or praise. Let fortune come or go as she pleases. Let death come today or ages hence: the steadfast do not move a step from the path of what is right. The idea is that your work has its own rhythm. Setbacks rarely last for those who keep going on the right path.
FOR YOU: You are building your own brand with no money, and the high is real. The path is yours now. The test will come when praise turns to blame, or when the money does not. Stay on the path.

## Day 4 [steady] Bhagavad Gita 2.40
LINE: No sincere effort here is ever wasted. Even a little of it shields you from great fear.
TRANSLATION: On this path no effort is lost and no harm results. Even a little of this practice protects one from great fear.
MEANING: Krishna is telling Arjuna about the yoga of action. He says no sincere effort here is ever wasted. Even a little of it shields you from great fear. The point is to do the work fully and let go of owning the result. Work wholeheartedly, give up clinging to outcomes, and keep a single steady purpose.
FOR YOU: You cannot control if the brand takes off. You can only control the work itself. Do it fully today, and let the result be what it will be.

## Day 5 [act] Mahabharata · Udyoga Parva · Vidura Niti
LINE: Decide, then begin. Don't stall halfway, don't waste time, and keep yourself steady.
TRANSLATION: One who begins only after deciding, does not stop halfway through the work, does not waste time and keeps himself in hand, is called wise.
MEANING: The night before the great peace talks, the sleepless King Dhritarashtra calls for his wise half-brother Vidura. Vidura tells him that a wise person decides calmly, then begins, does not stop halfway, does not waste time, and keeps themselves steady. That is the mark of wisdom. What comes next in the story is that the king ignores this counsel and the war begins anyway.
FOR YOU: The work you started last week is the decision made. Today, give your full attention to the next small task. Do not look ahead to the hard days or the money. Just steady your hand on what is in front of you now.

## Day 6 [act] Mahabharata · Udyoga Parva · Vidura Niti
LINE: Don't announce your plans; let people see your results.
TRANSLATION: Don't talk about what you are going to do; let people see only what you have done. That way your plans for duty, pleasure and gain are never exposed.
MEANING: Still in that same counsel, Vidura tells King Dhritarashtra that a wise person does not announce their plans. They let people see only what has been done. That way, their intentions for duty, pleasure, and gain remain hidden and safe. The story shows that announcing too much brings quarrels and exposes you to those who might use your words against you.
FOR YOU: Keep the details of your new brand to yourself for now. Let your first small results do the talking instead of your plans. Let the work show before you explain it.

## Day 7 [story] Hanuman's leap
LINE: Halfway across the ocean, a golden mountain rose out of the water and offered him a place to rest.
FOR YOU: Hanuman kept his promise to the mountain and his focus on the goal. They are building something from nothing. The fire they feel will test them when the work becomes ordinary. Let your steadiness, your clear sight, your good judgement and your skill carry you across the ocean.
STORY: Hanuman leaps across the ocean. A mountain rises to repay an old kindness by offering rest; he thanks it but keeps his promise not to stop. He outwits Surasa by shrinking small and defeats Simhika, and the watching beings praise his steadiness, sight, judgement and skill.
LESSON: Gratitude and focus can go together; know when to be big and when to be small.
