## Day 1 [hook] Valmiki Ramayana · Yuddha Kanda
LINE: After all these years together, if you still don't know me, that loss is forever.
TRANSLATION: We grew up together in love and lived together so long. If even so you have not understood me, then I am lost forever.
MEANING: After the war, Ram publicly voices doubt about Sita's purity before the entire court. Sita, who has already suffered enough, answers him with a quiet, devastating truth. She reminds him that they grew up together in love, that they lived together for so long. If even after all that time he still does not know her, then that loss is forever. She is not asking for proof. She is naming the deep injustice of being doubted by someone who is supposed to know her best.
FOR YOU: What you are cannot be cut smaller by what they see; your worth was never theirs to grant, and standing in your own truth is itself the duty.

MEANING: Tulsidas speaks this in the Ramcharitmanas as Ravana's ministers flatter their king while the enemy army waits at the gates. The poet notes that when advisers, doctors and teachers only say what pleases you, the kingdom, the body and righteousness are soon destroyed. The verse proposes that ruin comes quickly when a circle of yes-men replaces honest counsel. ## Day 4 [steady] Mahabharata · Udyoga Parva · Vidula's counsel
LINE: Don't think less of yourself or settle for little. Set your mind on something good, and stop fearing.
TRANSLATION: Do not hold yourself in contempt, and do not keep it going on too little. Set your mind on what is truly good; don't be afraid. Pull yourself together.
MEANING: Queen Vidula finds her son Sanjaya lying on the ground, defeated and refusing to rise after losing his kingdom. She tells him to stop holding himself in contempt. ## Day 5 [act] Maha Upanishad 6.64-65
LINE: Do the work in front of you, speak gently, read people's hearts, and you won't sink in the world.
TRANSLATION: Always doing the work that comes, seeing friend and foe alike, free of wanting and not-wanting, such a one neither grieves nor craves. Saying what is agreeable to all, speaking gently when asked, knowing the intent of others' hearts, such a one does not sink in the world.
MEANING: Ribhu is speaking to Nidagha about how a person who has found freedom moves through the world. He tells him to do the work in front of him, to speak gently, to read what is in people's hearts, and not to sink. This means you live fully in the world, doing what comes, while staying calm and unhurried on the inside. You let go of what is lost and take up what is there. You do not grieve or crave. You speak what is agreeable to all, and you know the intent of others.
FOR YOU: The work in front of you is your CA practice and the man you married. Do it. Speak gently to your mother-in-law, but read her heart. She wants to erase you, but you will not sink. You are building a life, and you will stay afloat.

## Day 6 [act] Bhagavad Gita 17.15
LINE: Speak words that don't wound, that are true, kind and helpful. That is discipline of speech.
TRANSLATION: Speech that causes no distress, that is truthful, pleasant and beneficial, and the regular study of sacred texts: this is called austerity of speech.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna about what real effort looks like, not the kind done for show. He says that speech is a form of discipline, and the right kind is words that do not wound, that are true, kind and helpful. This is not about being polite to avoid conflict. It is about speaking with a steady heart, saying what is needed without cruelty, and letting the truth stand on its own. What comes next in the story is that effort and giving also have three kinds, and the showy kind does not last.
FOR YOU: Speak the true thing gently and firmly, hold your ground on what is yours, and do your own duty without shrinking to fit the picture they hold.

## Day 7 [story] Queen Chudala teaches her husband what renunciation really is
LINE: The queen had found peace, and the king, who loved her, could not believe it.
FOR YOU: Running away doesn't free you; letting go inside does. Sometimes the teacher is the person beside you.
STORY: Queen Chudala finds inner peace, but her husband, King Shikhidhvaja, doesn't believe her. Restless, he leaves his kingdom for the forest. Chudala rules wisely in his absence and visits him disguised as a young sage, Kumbha, who shows him that giving up kingdom, home and body is not renunciation; letting go of the clinging mind is.
