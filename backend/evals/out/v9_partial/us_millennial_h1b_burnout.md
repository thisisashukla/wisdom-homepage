## Day 1 [hook] Ashtavakra Gita 18.61
LINE: A restless mind turns even rest into busyness. A still mind finds rest even in work.
TRANSLATION: Even a fool's retreat from action turns into activity; even a wise person's activity bears the fruit of stillness.
MEANING: At the end of the long chapter on peace, Ashtavakra sits with Janaka and says that a restless mind turns even rest into busyness. A still mind finds rest even in work. The restless chase peace and never find it. The wise find it inside ordinary life. This is what happens when the mind cannot stop multiplying what has not happened.
FOR YOU: The two in the morning messages are the mind turning rest into busyness. The rating is not the real thing keeping you awake. The clock is not the danger. The danger is that the mind has forgotten this.

## Day 4 [steady] Bhagavad Gita 5.20
LINE: Don't get carried away by good news or crushed by bad. Keep your understanding steady.
TRANSLATION: One should not rejoice on getting what is pleasant nor be troubled on getting what is unpleasant. Steady in understanding and undeluded, the knower of Brahman abides in Brahman.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna about a person who sees clearly. Such a person does not leap for joy at good news or sink when bad news arrives. Their understanding stays steady and they are not fooled by passing events. They find their happiness within, knowing that sense pleasures always end in sorrow. They can withstand desire and anger because they know what is real in them cannot be lost.
FOR YOU: You are not crushed by a bad rating and you are not carried away by a good one. You can let the sixty-day clock pass without letting it own your mind. You hold what is real in you, and no deadline can cut it.

## Day 5 [act] Bhagavad Gita 2.47
LINE: Your work is yours to do; the results are not yours to hold. Don't let that make you stop.
TRANSLATION: Your right is to the work alone, never to its fruits. Do not let the fruit be your motive, and do not cling to doing nothing.
MEANING: Krishna tells Arjuna that his right is only to the work itself, never to its fruits. He is in the middle of a great war, and Arjuna is paralyzed by what will happen if he fights. Krishna says do not let the fear of results make you stop. The work is yours to do. The result is not yours to hold. This does not mean you could stop caring or stop planning. It means you do the work fully, and you let go of owning the outcome.
FOR YOU: Do what you can today, take refuge in what does not change, and meet each moment with courage instead of rehearsing the worst.

## Day 6 [act] Bhagavad Gita 12.12
LINE: Practice is good, understanding better, meditation better still. But letting go of results brings peace at once.
TRANSLATION: Knowledge is better than mere practice; meditation is better than knowledge; giving up the fruit of action is better than meditation. From that renunciation, peace follows immediately.
MEANING: Arjuna wants to know who is the best devotee, and Krishna offers a ladder of practices. First comes devotion to a form. Then fixing the mind. Then practice. Then working for me. But Krishna says that letting go of results brings peace at once. If the higher steps feel too hard, take the step just below you and keep going. Peace does not wait for perfection.
FOR YOU: You have tried to meditate and you have tried to work without attachment, and both have felt impossible. Take the next rung: let your mind rest for one hour before sleep, without checking the rating. Let that be enough for today.
