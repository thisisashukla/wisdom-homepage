## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 2.22
LINE: As we drop worn-out clothes and put on new ones, the self leaves a worn body for a new one.
TRANSLATION: Just as a person casts off worn-out clothes and puts on new ones, so the embodied self casts off worn-out bodies and enters others that are new.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna, who has laid down his bow and is broken by the sight of his own family arrayed against him. He is asking what is real, and what is left when everything he loves is about to be destroyed. Krishna says that the self inside us is never born and never dies. It simply leaves this worn-out body and enters another, just as we take off an old shirt and put on a new one.
FOR YOU: You have laid down your routine and are looking for what is real beneath the loss of your partner. In the quiet of your evenings, you can let go of the worn-out shape of your days and rest in the truth that the self which was your partner has not been destroyed by March.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Valmiki Ramayana · Ayodhya Kanda
LINE: Grief wears away your steadiness and your judgement. There is no enemy like it.
TRANSLATION: Grief destroys steadiness, grief destroys learning, grief destroys everything. There is no enemy like grief.
MEANING: In the Ayodhya Kanda of the Valmiki Ramayana, Kausalya speaks to Dasharatha. She has just lashed out at him over Ram's exile, and now she apologises. She tells him that grief wears away your steadiness and your judgement. It destroys your learning, and it destroys everything. She says there is no enemy like it. The verse proposes that sorrow can make us wound those we love, and that apologising quickly heals more than being right.
FOR YOU: The evenings bring a heaviness that makes the past feel sharper and the future harder to see. When that happens, you might notice your mind turning over the same sharp words you wish you had said, or the same moments you want to undo. Grief wears away your steadiness.

## Day 3 [steady] Katha Upanishad 1.2.22
LINE: Knowing what stays steady inside all that changes, the wise do not grieve.
TRANSLATION: Knowing the self as bodiless within bodies, steady among the unsteady, great and all-pervading, the wise one does not grieve.
MEANING: Yama tells the boy Nachiketa that the true self is bodiless within bodies, steady among the unsteady, great and all-pervading. The wise one does not grieve. He says the self is never born and never dies, not killed when the body is. It is found not by cleverness or study but by a calm, honest life.
FOR YOU: In the quiet of the evening, the ache is real because your love was real. But the self that was your partner is not gone. Nothing that is real is ever lost. Hold onto this truth as you sit with your grief.

## Day 4 [steady] Ashtavakra Gita 15.9
LINE: The body comes and goes; what you truly are does not. Why grieve so?
TRANSLATION: The body, made of nature's qualities, comes, stays and goes. The Self neither comes nor goes. Why grieve for it?
MEANING: Ashtavakra speaks to Janaka. He tells him that the body is made of changing elements, so it is born and it dies. But the true Self is not born and does not die. It simply stays. Ashtavakra asks why Janaka would grieve for what only changes, when the core of who he is remains untouched by time.
FOR YOU: In the evenings, the quiet makes the loss feel final. But the body that held your partner is only a vessel. The truth is that the core of who they were, the awareness they shared with you, is not gone. It stays.

## Day 5 [act] Mahabharata · Shanti Parva · Shanti: Consolation of Yudhishthira
LINE: In grief, ask: where did I come from, where am I going, who am I? Let that steady you.
TRANSLATION: 'Where was I before? Where will I go? Who am I, really, and why am I here? Why should I grieve, and for what?' Ask these, and steady the mind.
MEANING: The sage Ashma answers King Janaka, who asks how to bear the loss of family and wealth. Ashma says that pain comes from what we did not want or what we lost, that meetings end and days do not return, and that we should ask where we came from, where we are going, who we are, and why we are here. Then steady the mind, and hold to the people still in front of you.
FOR YOU: Let yourself grieve, honour them by living well, and remember that the self they loved in you, and in them, does not perish.

## Day 6 [act] Mahabharata · Shanti Parva · Bhishma: Mokshadharma
LINE: Life doesn't pause, not even for the blink of an eye. Don't spend it brooding.
TRANSLATION: Not even for the blink of an eye does life stand still. In a body that is passing, why brood as if things last forever?
MEANING: The sage Narada visits the young Shuka and sees him lost in grief. He tells him that life never pauses, not even for an instant. In a body that is passing, there is no point in brooding as if things will last forever. Brooding does not heal pain, it only makes it grow. The mind is healed by understanding, just as the body is healed by medicine.
FOR YOU: The temple seva and the daytime keep the hours moving. In the evening, the stillness returns and the mind wants to circle the same loss. This verse asks you to guard the mind as you would the body. Do not let the evening hours become a place of brooding.
