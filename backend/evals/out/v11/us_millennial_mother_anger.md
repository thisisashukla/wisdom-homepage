## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 1.29–30
LINE: Shaking limbs, a dry mouth, a racing body: the Gita opens with a panic attack, not a hero.
TRANSLATION: My limbs give way, my mouth is parched, my body trembles and my hair stands on end.
MEANING: Arjuna stands in his chariot on the field. He sees his own family, his teachers, his grand-uncles, and his cousins in the opposing army. His limbs begin to shake and his mouth goes dry. His body trembles and his hair stands on end. He is a warrior about to fight, but he freezes. What he is feeling is not weakness. It is the body responding when the mind is overwhelmed by grief and fear.
FOR YOU: You are a calm leader at your practice, but at home you feel the same shaking limbs. The fear of becoming your mother is not a sign of failure. It is the body speaking before the mind can catch up.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Bhagavad Gita 2.63
LINE: Anger clouds judgement, clouded judgement loses memory, lost memory wrecks reason, and then you fall.
TRANSLATION: From anger comes delusion; from delusion, confusion of memory; from confusion of memory, the ruin of understanding; and with understanding ruined, a person is lost.
MEANING: Krishna is answering Arjuna's question about what a steady mind looks like. Arjuna is shaken and confused on the battlefield, and Krishna is showing him how a person loses their footing. He traces a chain: anger clouds judgement, clouded judgement loses memory, lost memory wrecks reason, and then you fall. The idea is that steadiness is built by guarding your attention. Anger does not come out of nowhere. It starts with what you dwell on, and without rest, the mind has no room to catch a spill before it floods.
FOR YOU: At your practice, you hold the room with calm. That same mind is full of noise when you are home. The verse asks you to see that the anger was already there before the milk, and that a mind without rest has no room for a spill.

MEANING: Ram has just been told he must leave for the forest for fourteen years. His brother Lakshman is burning with rage. He wants to seize the throne by force and punish those who did this. Ram speaks to him in the middle of this storm. He tells Lakshman to rein in the anger and grief. He asks him to lean on his steadiness alone. He tells him to set aside the insult and take up real cheerfulness. He says to quickly undo the coronation preparations and get on with what needs doing, without fuss.

## Day 4 [steady] Bhagavad Gita 2.15
LINE: The one who stays the same in pain and pleasure, undisturbed by either, is ready for real freedom.
TRANSLATION: The steady person whom these do not disturb, who stays the same in pain and pleasure, becomes fit for immortality.
MEANING: Krishna speaks to Arjuna, who is sitting in grief on the battlefield. He tells him that the true self never changes. It is never born and it never dies. Pleasure and pain, heat and cold, praise and blame, all pass over the surface of who you are. The one who remains undisturbed by these passing states, staying the same through them all, becomes ready for something far deeper than temporary comfort. The self underneath your anger is not touched by it. This steadiness is not numbness.
FOR YOU: At work you are steady with twelve people, but at home the anger takes over before you can choose. This steadiness is not hiding what you feel. It is the quiet knowing that the woman who snapped is not the whole of you, and she does not have to stay.

## Day 6 [act] Mahabharata · Shanti Parva · Bhishma, Mokshadharma
LINE: Guard your effort from anger, your success from envy, your learning from pride, and yourself from carelessness.
TRANSLATION: Always guard your discipline from anger and your good fortune from envy; guard your learning from both pride and insult, and guard yourself from carelessness.
MEANING: Narada tells the young Shuka that brooding does not heal pain, it grows it. The mind is healed by understanding, just as the body is healed by medicine. When you feel a wound, do not sit with it until it swells. If you can fix the cause, act. If you cannot, find a way to rest. True contentment is the only real wealth.
FOR YOU: Take one hour for yourself before the day begins, and when anger rises, let it pass instead of letting it become the sky. You are calm at work because you are fully there.

## Day 7 [story] Kausalya and the child who ran from dinner
LINE: Kausalya was shown the whole universe in her newborn, and all she wanted was for him to cry like a baby.
FOR YOU: Kausalya saw the whole universe in her son, but she only wanted him to be a child. When your twelve people calm down because you are steady, you give your children that same safety. Your presence is the ground they stand on. The anger that takes over is not who you are.
STORY: At his birth Ram shows Kausalya his cosmic form, and she asks him instead to be simply a baby. Later scenes are pure childhood: the boy runs off mid-meal, curd on his face, dusty from play, while his father laughs and his mother chases him. When she once glimpses the whole universe in him again, she asks only to be spared that and keep her child.
LESSON: The grandest thing about a child may be the ordinary hours of childhood itself.
