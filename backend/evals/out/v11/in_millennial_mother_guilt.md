## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 6.34
LINE: The mind is restless, stormy, stubborn and strong. Holding it feels like holding the wind.
TRANSLATION: For the mind is restless, Krishna, turbulent, powerful and stubborn. I think it is as hard to hold as the wind.
MEANING: Arjuna tells Krishna that the mind is restless and stormy, as hard to hold as the wind. Krishna agrees that this is the nature of the mind, but he does not leave it there. He says that practice and letting go can train it. The idea is that the mind will not sit still on its own, but it can be shaped by what you do with it, not by what you demand of it.
FOR YOU: You are in the car, the engine off, and your mind is running through everything you did not do. You are trying to hold it still, to force it into the shape of a perfect mother and a perfect worker, and it is only making the storm worse.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Bhagavad Gita 2.62
LINE: Dwell on something and you get attached; attachment becomes wanting, and blocked wanting turns into anger.
TRANSLATION: When a person dwells on objects, attachment to them is born. From attachment comes desire, and from desire, anger.
MEANING: Krishna is describing the mind of a steady person to Arjuna. He says that when a person dwells on objects, attachment to them is born. From attachment comes desire, and from desire, anger. A person who is steady does not dwell on things in that way. They guard their attention. The mind stays calm because it is not caught in a loop of wanting and then wanting what it cannot have.
FOR YOU: A mistake is something you did, not who you are; no one who tries to do good is ever lost.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhagavad Gita 18.47
LINE: Better your own path, imperfectly walked, than someone else's done perfectly.
TRANSLATION: Better is one's own duty, though imperfect, than the duty of another well performed. Doing the work born of one's own nature, one incurs no wrong.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna on the battlefield. He has been explaining that every action carries risk, and that no one is free from fault. In this moment, he tells Arjuna to stop measuring himself against the perfect performance of others. He says it is better to walk your own path, even if you stumble, than to walk someone else's path with perfect steps. The work that comes from your own nature is your offering, and doing it, even with its flaws, brings no real wrong.
FOR YOU: The car ride after school is where you feel the weight of a standard you have set for yourself that you would never place on your children. Your work is your own path, and your presence with your kids is your own path.

TRANSLATION: If a person who has done wrong turns to doing good, he is freed from all his wrongs, like the moon coming out from behind a great cloud.
MEANING: In Mithila, a meat-seller named Dharmavyadha speaks to a proud ascetic named Kaushika, who has come to judge him.
