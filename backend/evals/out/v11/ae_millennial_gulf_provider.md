## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 1.29–30
LINE: Shaking limbs, a dry mouth, a racing body: the Gita opens with a panic attack, not a hero.
TRANSLATION: My limbs give way, my mouth is parched, my body trembles and my hair stands on end.
MEANING: Arjuna stands in his chariot on the field. He sees his own family, his teachers, and his grand-uncles on the side he is meant to fight. The weight of killing them crashes down. His limbs shake, his mouth goes dry, his body trembles, and his hair stands on end. He drops his bow. He tells Krishna he would rather die than kill his own people. The Gita begins not with a warrior, but with a man who cannot stand.
FOR YOU: The work you do, the money you send, the home you hold together. It is all real. But right now your body is showing you what your mind has known for a while. It is pulled in every direction. That is not a failure. It is the truth of where you stand today.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Yoga Vasishtha
LINE: 'Done this, doing this, must do that.' Caught in endless lists, the mind never gets a moment's rest.
TRANSLATION: Carried along on endless great waves of tasks, the mind never gets a moment's rest; its depths dance with worry. 'This I've done, this I'm doing, this I'll do': so the bird of the mind, tangled in a net of reckonings, faints.
MEANING: Bhasa and Vilasa are two old friends meeting after a lifetime apart. They had both spent years in severe austerities, hoping to find peace, but neither found it. Bhasa admits that the mind never gets a moment's rest. It is carried along on endless waves of tasks, dancing with worry. The mind faints under the weight of its own endless lists, caught between what has been done, what is being done, and what must still be done. This is the trap of years of hard effort without inner understanding.
FOR YOU: Your own duty, done imperfectly, is better than another's; when the mind clears, the right step becomes plain.

## Day 3 [steady] Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 4.4.21
LINE: Know the one thing, and let wisdom grow from it. Don't brood over many words; that only tires you.
TRANSLATION: Knowing that one alone, let the wise seeker cultivate understanding. Let him not brood over many words, for that only wearies the voice.
MEANING: Yajnavalkya is speaking to King Janaka about what happens when a person dies. He says that a person becomes exactly what they desire, will, and do. If you want to be a brave hero, you become one. If you want to be a quiet sage, you become that. A person is simply the sum of their deepest wishes and actions. He tells Janaka to know this one thing clearly. Do not brood over many words or endless lists. That only tires the voice and the mind.
FOR YOU: Your mind has been running through endless lists for years. It is time to stop. Choose one thing that matters to you right now and let it carry you. Let the rest wait. You do not have to decide everything at once.

## Day 4 [steady] Ashtavakra Gita 15.19
LINE: Don't churn your mind with plans and second thoughts. Be still and rest in yourself.
TRANSLATION: Do not stir your mind with yes and no, with plans and doubts. Be still, and rest happily in your own joyful Self.
MEANING: Ashtavakra is speaking to Janaka, who sits with the weight of a kingdom on his shoulders. Ashtavakra knows that Janaka has spent years playing the role of a ruler, a husband, a father, and now he is trying to play the role of a seeker. Ashtavakra tells him that he is not these things. He is the vast, still ocean in which all the waves of duty rise and fall. The mind is the wave, always churning, always deciding, always doubting. Ashtavakra says, be still.
FOR YOU: The mind has been churning over the money sent to your parents, the work that demands your hours, and the wish to return to India. You do not have to solve all of it right now. Just let the thoughts rise and fall.

## Day 5 [act] Maha Upanishad 6.68
LINE: Be busy on the outside and unhurried inside. Act fully, but don't let it agitate you.
TRANSLATION: Outwardly busy, inwardly free of agitation; a doer on the outside, a non-doer within: move through the world with a clear mind.
MEANING: Ribhu tells Nidagha how a person who has found freedom lives in the world. They let go of what is gone, take up what comes, speak gently, and treat the whole world as family. The verse says a free person is outwardly busy but inwardly free of agitation. They do the work at hand, yet their mind does not churn. They move through the world with a clear mind, acting fully without being shaken by it.
FOR YOU: pick one duty that belongs to you and nothing else. It might be the work at hand, or a minute of quiet. Let your mind rest in that one thing, and do not let it churn with second thoughts.

## Day 6 [act] Ram Gita 46 (Adhyatma Ramayana, Uttara Kanda 5)
LINE: Sit somewhere quiet, let the senses rest, and give your full attention to one thing.
TRANSLATION: Sitting in a quiet place, senses at rest, self-mastered, pure within, one should contemplate the One with undivided attention, resting in the Self.
MEANING: After the war, Lakshmana finds Rama alone and asks how to cross the sea of confusion. Rama tells him to sit in a quiet place, let the senses rest, and give undivided attention to the One, resting in the Self. The idea is that quiet sitting and steady faith carry you until understanding becomes natural. He does not say to run away or to fight more. He says to rest the senses and focus on one thing until the mind becomes still.
FOR YOU: You have been moving money and answering calls for years. Today, give your mind one quiet minute. Turn off the phone. Sit still. Let the noise of the partner, the parents, and the work rest. Do this for just one minute. That is your duty today.

LINE: One spring morning, a king out for a walk overheard a song that he could not stop thinking about.
