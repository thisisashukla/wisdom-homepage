LINE: Past practice carries you forward, even when you don't feel like it.
TRANSLATION: By that former practice he is carried on, even against his will. Even one who merely wishes to know yoga goes beyond the ritual word.
MEANING: Arjuna asks Krishna a hard question. He has been trying to live well, but he keeps failing. He wonders if all this effort is just wasted. Krishna answers that the practice itself is the key. A person is carried forward by what they have already done. Even if they are pulled away from it, the old practice still holds them. It pushes them back toward the path. The effort is never truly lost.

LINE: Steadiness comes from doing. No one becomes steady without acting; act, and steadiness follows.

This is not about forcing yourself to feel better through sheer effort, but about recognizing that the doing itself creates the ground on which steadiness can grow. The next step in this teaching is that action is only the beginning.

## Day 6 [act] Mahabharata · Shanti Parva · Bhishma, Mokshadharma
LINE: Do tomorrow's work today, and the afternoon's in the morning. Time doesn't wait for you to be ready.
TRANSLATION: Do tomorrow's work today, and the afternoon's work in the morning. Death does not wait to see whether your work is done or not.
MEANING: Medhavin speaks to his father about time and readiness. The father lays out the long path of study and duty, but the son says death does not wait to see whether your work is done. He tells him to do tomorrow's work today, and the afternoon's in the morning. The point is that life slips away while we stay busy. The son is asking his father to stop waiting for the perfect moment and to act on what matters right now, before the chance is gone.
