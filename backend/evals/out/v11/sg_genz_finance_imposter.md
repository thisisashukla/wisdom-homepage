## Day 1 [hook] Bhagavad Gita 6.34
LINE: The mind is restless, stormy, stubborn and strong. Holding it feels like holding the wind.
TRANSLATION: For the mind is restless, Krishna, turbulent, powerful and stubborn. I think it is as hard to hold as the wind.
MEANING: Arjuna tells Krishna that the mind is restless, turbulent, and stubborn. He says it is as hard to hold as the wind. Krishna agrees that the mind is difficult to control, but says that practice and letting go can train it.

## Day 2 [diagnose] Ashtavakra Gita 1.11
LINE: Believe you are free and you are. Believe you are bound and you are. As you think, so you become.
TRANSLATION: One who thinks 'I am free' is free, and one who thinks 'I am bound' stays bound. The saying here is true: as you think, so you become.
MEANING: King Janaka asks the young sage Ashtavakra how to become free. Ashtavakra replies that you are not your body or your roles but the awareness watching them. He tells the king that the mind's belief shapes your reality. If you think you are bound by your fears and roles, you stay bound. If you think you are free, you are. The verse proposes that your internal belief is what creates your experience of being trapped.
FOR YOU: The belief that you are a fraud is what makes you feel trapped. Today, notice the thought that says you are not enough and see it as just a thought. You are the awareness watching that thought, not the thought itself.

## Day 3 [steady] Bhartrihari · Niti Shatakam
LINE: A ball knocked down bounces back up. For good people, setbacks rarely last.
TRANSLATION: A ball struck down by the hand flies right back up. As a rule, the misfortunes of those who live rightly do not last.
MEANING: Bhartrihari, the poet, is writing for anyone who has ever felt knocked down. He says a ball struck by a hand flies right back up. He means that for people who keep going on the right path, setbacks rarely last. The misfortunes of the steady do not stick. He is not saying suffering is always short, or that long pain means a person did wrong. He is simply saying that staying on the path is what makes the ball bounce back.
FOR YOU: The over-preparing and the fear before your presentations are the hand that keeps striking you down. But you are still on the path. Let the ball of this setback bounce back up today by trusting that your steady work is what matters, not the verdict you rehearse.

## Day 5 [act] Mahabharata · Vana Parva · Vyadha Gita
LINE: Turn to doing good after a wrong, and you come out like the moon from behind a cloud.
TRANSLATION: If a person who has done wrong turns to doing good, he is freed from all his wrongs, like the moon coming out from behind a great cloud.
MEANING: In Mithila, a meat-seller named Dharmavyadha speaks to a proud ascetic named Kaushika. Kaushika is full of pride and has done wrong. The butcher tells him that if he turns to doing good after a wrong, he is freed from it all, like the moon coming out from behind a great cloud. The truth underneath is not about the past mistake itself, but about the choice to turn toward honest action now. You do not have to carry the dark cloud of what you fear you are.
FOR YOU: Before your next presentation, let the facts of what you prepared stand on their own. You are not the verdict you rehearse. Let your work do the talking for a moment.

## Day 6 [act] Bhagavad Gita 3.35
LINE: Better your own path done imperfectly than someone else's done perfectly. Borrowed lives are dangerous.
TRANSLATION: Better is one's own duty, even done imperfectly, than the duty of another well done. Better is death in one's own duty; the duty of another is full of fear.
MEANING: Krishna speaks to Arjuna on the battlefield, telling them to act free of ego, like and dislike. He says that your own path, imperfectly walked, beats a borrowed one. The truth underneath is that your own nature and calling are the only safe ground.
FOR YOU: You are preparing for a presentation, but you are rehearsing someone else's idea of success. Today, do your own part. Write one sentence that is true to your own view, not the view you think they want to hear.
