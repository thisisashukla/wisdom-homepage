## Day 2 [diagnose] Bhagavad Gita 3.35
LINE: Better your own path done imperfectly than someone else's done perfectly. Borrowed lives are dangerous.
TRANSLATION: Better is one's own duty, even done imperfectly, than the duty of another well done. Better is death in one's own duty; the duty of another is full of fear.
MEANING: Krishna is speaking to Arjuna, who is standing on the battlefield, unsure of his duty and terrified of the cost. Arjuna has asked for clear direction. Krishna tells him that it is better to walk your own path, even if you stumble along the way, than to perfectly copy the path of someone else. Following a borrowed life brings only fear. The idea is that your own nature and calling, followed honestly, is stronger than any performance of another's life.
FOR YOU: When others see only a role, their picture is their smallness and not your measure; the harm deepens only when their picture begins to replace your own.

## Day 5 [act] Bhagavad Gita 17.15
LINE: Speak words that don't wound, that are true, kind and helpful. That is discipline of speech.
TRANSLATION: Speech that causes no distress, that is truthful, pleasant and beneficial, and the regular study of sacred texts: this is called austerity of speech.
MEANING: Krishna is explaining to Arjuna the nature of true discipline, and he places speech at the center. He says that true austerity of speech is to use words that cause no distress, that are true, pleasant and beneficial. It is not about hiding the truth behind a wall of niceness, but about saying what needs to be said without adding cruelty. The idea is to strip away the performance of communication, so that what remains is a clear, kind exchange between people.
FOR YOU: You are sitting at the table and feeling like a guest in your own home. Let your words to your parents today be true without being sharp. Speak the simple truth about your day without adding the weight of their silence, and let that be enough.

LINE: Not too long, not vague, not rushed, not strained, in an even voice: that is how to speak.
TRANSLATION: His words were not long-winded, not ambiguous, not rushed, not strained; they came from the chest and throat in an even, middle tone.
MEANING: Ram tells Lakshman that Hanuman spoke to them with perfect balance. His words were not long-winded, not ambiguous, not rushed, not strained. Hanuman did not match that fear. He spoke calmly and clearly so Ram would trust him. The verse proposes that fear jumps ahead of facts, but calm, clear words build trust fast.
