# corpus_bh_1 notes: Bhartrihari, Nitishataka (45 verses, bh.niti.3 to bh.niti.86)

Sources used throughout (all public domain or open, all read locally from downloaded full texts):
- GRETIL Nitishataka (Chaukhamba ed., Tripathi 1990), the numbering our ids mostly follow.
- D. D. Kosambi's 1948 critical edition (via ambuda.org), for textual status. Group I = nos. 1-200, found in all versions; Group II = 201-352, "reason to doubt"; Group III = 353+, stray verses from single versions or anthologies.
- Ramachandra Budhendra's Sanskrit tika (southern text, Nirnaya Sagar 1914, archive.org).
- Janardana Shastri Pandey's Sanskrit and Hindi commentary (1974, archive.org).
- M. R. Kale (1902), B. H. Wortham (1886) and K. T. Telang (1874) translations and notes (archive.org).

No classical bhashya exists for Bhartrihari, so the commentaries are these tikas and editors' notes. All 45 entries have 2 or more sourced commentaries.

## Data errors (fix in our records)

1. **bh.niti.85 is numbered from a different edition.** The ball verse (patito 'pi karāghātaiḥ...) is Kale/Wortham no. 85 and Kosambi 270. It is **not in the GRETIL/Chaukhamba text at all**: GRETIL 1.85 is a different verse (the rat and the snake in a basket, on fate). The rest of this group follows GRETIL numbering, so "Nitishatakam 85" points to the wrong verse for anyone using that text. Relabel it, e.g. "Nitishataka (Kale/Wortham 85)". It is also absent from Ramachandra Budhendra's and Pandey's texts, and Kosambi puts it in Group II (doubtful).
2. **Wrong Kosambi numbers in verified_with / ref_label:**
   - bh.niti.41: says 521, should be **51** (521 is a different chataka verse).
   - bh.niti.55: says 27, should be **37**.
   - bh.niti.70: says 26, should be **36**.
   - Missing Kosambi numbers, now supplied: 3 (8), 19 (76), 21 (237), 49 (56), 66 (335), 67 (33), 71 (63), 75 (221).
3. **Sanskrit typos in our text:**
   - bh.niti.21: 'विद्या न वन्द्या यदि' should be 'विद्याऽनवद्या यदि' (flawless learning). As printed it means "if learning is not honoured". The English is correct.
   - bh.niti.42: 'त्यागः प्रमादाद्धनम्' does not construe; read 'त्यागात् प्रमादाद् धनम्' or GRETIL 'त्याग-प्रमादाद् धनम्'.
   - bh.niti.82: 'कण्ठाधारी' should be 'कन्थाधारी' (kanthādhārī, wearing a rag-quilt). The English "rags" is correct.
   - bh.niti.85: 'साधुवृत्तनाम' should be 'साधुवृत्तानाम्'.
4. **Translation and line points:**
   - bh.niti.25: 'parijano avañcakaḥ' means honest servants or staff, not kinsfolk.
   - bh.niti.72: 'śruta' is learning or scripture that has been heard. Our line's "ears shine by listening" is loose; "by what they have learned" is closer.
   - bh.niti.78: our Sanskrit follows GRETIL 'prakhyāpaya praśrayam' (show humility). Kosambi, Kale and Wortham read 'pracchādaya svān guṇān' (hide your own virtues). Both are legitimate readings, but a writer should know which one we use.
   - bh.niti.66: the verse describes the great, but our line turns it into an order ("be soft... be firm"), as our_note says. Do not present the order as the poet's words.
   - bh.niti.62: Ramachandra Budhendra reads 'śaktir ātma-damane' as strength when oneself is humiliated (not passive meekness). Kale and Pandey read it as self-control. Our translation follows the usual reading, which is fine.
5. **Attribution cautions.** These are in the common text but weakly attested as Bhartrihari's own:
   - Group III: 25, 40, 51.
   - Group II: 14, 21, 27, 64, 66, 68, 75, 84, 85, 86.
   - Note that **bh.niti.84 (nindantu nītinipuṇāḥ), "the best known verse", is Kosambi Group II.**
   - bh.niti.71 is also, word for word, a stanza in Kalidasa's Shakuntala.
   - bh.niti.27 is also in the Mudrarakshasa.
   - bh.niti.63 is also in the Hitopadesha.
6. **Unverified Vivekananda citations.** verified_with for 75, 79 and 84 cites "Vivekananda, Complete Works (see notes)". I could not confirm these: the Wikisource pages would not load. No Vivekananda gist was added. Confirm before using.

## Weak or thin sources
- bh.niti.85: no Sanskrit commentary. It has only Kale's and Wortham's notes (the pun on sādhu-vṛtta, "well-rounded" and "well-conducted") plus Kosambi.
- No Ramachandra Budhendra (traditional) tika, because the verse is missing from his southern text: 14, 25, 40, 51, 85, 86. These rely on Pandey (modern) and the English editors.
- Kale's notes are OCR-damaged for most verses after 65. Kale is cited mainly for his translation choices.

## Cautions for editors (corpus readings and plan-building)
- **Honour-before-life rhetoric:** 55 ("if disgrace, what need of death?"), 28 ("even at the cost of life") and 84 ("let death come today"). Never use these alone with anyone facing public shame, failure or suicidal thoughts, or in grief plans.
- **Blessings framed as reward for merit or divine favour:** 25 and 68 (good son, wife and friend come to the meritorious) and 85 (good people's troubles don't last). These can read as blame for people who are childless, widowed, estranged, chronically ill or long unemployed. For 68, keep the card limited to the friend line, as our record already does.
- **Fatalism:** 49 (written on the forehead). This group also borders the fate section (GRETIL 85 onward). Avoid for readers facing injustice or poverty.
- **Effort and hardship verses:** 82 and 86 can feed hustle culture or shame in depression and burnout. Keep the rest misuse line for 86, and pair 82 with it.
- **Forbearance and conciliation verses:** 57, 70 and 78 must not be offered to someone in an abusive home or workplace as a reason to endure.
- **Harsh or contemptuous labels:** fools (3, 4, 14), "beast without learning" (20) and "demons in human form" (75). Frame them as self-examination, not verdicts on others.
- **Gendered or sectarian items:** 26, 42, 62 and 68. Render them inclusively (faithfulness to one's partner) when used for general readers. Ramachandra Budhendra on 42 cites a maxim favouring beating children; do not reproduce it.
- **Strong candidates for new plans:**
  - Friendship (60, 68 friend line, 73, 76).
  - Humility in success (63, 66, 71, 83).
  - Integrity under pressure (84, 64, 70).
  - Envy into appreciation (62, 79).
  - Company and environment (67, 23).
