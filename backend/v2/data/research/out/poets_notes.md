# poets: editor notes

22 of 22 verses filled, same order as input. Every entry has 2 to 4 sourced commentary notes. 19 entries came from the previous agent and were kept as they were. The 3 Sur/Kabir entries (sur.bgs.316, sur.bgs.10, kabir.kk.66) were empty and are now researched.

## Data errors (fix in our data)

1. **bh.niti.87: Sanskrit and translation do not match the cited text.** Our text reads 'vardhate loke ... na loke 'smin'. GRETIL reads 'santapyante na duḥkheṣu' (not consumed in their sorrows), and Kosambi reads 'na vidhureṣu' (not in misfortunes). Ours repeats 'loke' and loses the point that good people are not crushed *by their troubles*. A suggested translation is in the entry. The setting label 'section on steadfastness' also conflicts with Wortham, who puts it in the section on fate.
2. **bh.niti.106: ID numbering does not match.** Every other bh.niti id uses GRETIL numbering, but this one uses Wortham/Kale. In GRETIL this verse is 1.105, and GRETIL 1.106 is a different verse.
3. **bh.vairagya.82: translation fix.** 'For long' belongs to the poet's reflection ('though I have pondered it long...'), not to the mind turning outward. Suggested wording is in the entry.
4. **kabir.kk.66: text and reference.** The verse is sakhi 1 of 'Het Preeti Saneh ko Ang' (section 44) in Shyamsundar Das's Kabir Granthavali (NPS), cited by Hazari Prasad Dwivedi as K. Gr. p. 67. The Granthavali reads 'kamodani jalahari basai ... jo jahi ka bhavata, so tahi ke pas'. Ours ('जल में बसै', 'जो है जाको भावता') is a later popular form. 'Kavita-Kaumudi 66' could not be verified. Our translation adds 'always', which the original does not have.
5. **viv.7.want_a_bit_of_peace: setting.** The letter was written from Camp Taylor, northern California, not Los Angeles. Mrs Blodgett had been his hostess in LA earlier.
6. **viv.5.ridicule_opposition: letter number.** The letter is XLVII. The online CW page title misprints it as XLVI.
7. **viv.4.hold_on_brave_heart: excerpt.** The excerpt joins stanzas 1 and 4. Stanza 4 says 'brave soul', not 'brave heart'.
8. **sur.bgs.10: listener.** Krishna speaks to Uddhav, giving him a message to carry to Yashoda. 'Listener: Yashoda' is acceptable only if read as the recipient. 'Warm milk' undersells 'dhaiya': the commentary glosses it as milk straight from the cow's udder.
9. **Plan headings left over from replaced verses:** gratitude day 1 ('The taste in water', from bg.7.8) and losing-hope day 2 ('Stand up', from bg.2.3) no longer fit their verses.
10. **viv.3.dares_to_differ: setting.** 'Friends who turned critics' were Theosophists and public supporters, not personal friends.

## Attribution cautions
- **Bhartrihari: Kosambi's 'doubtful' block.** In Kosambi's critical edition, vairagya.82, vairagya.44, niti.87 and niti.106 fall among the doubtful verses (201-352). niti.53 and niti.58 are floating gnomic verses that also appear in the Hitopadesha and in Chanakya collections. For niti.53, say 'a verse in Bhartrihari's Nitishatakam'.
- **viv.6.one_thing_at_a_time** comes from class-talk notes. The recorder and date are unknown, so attribute it as 'from notes of a class talk'.
- **Kabir** is as reliable as Kabir sakhis get: it is in the NPS Granthavali. **The two Sur padas** are confirmed under the same numbers (10 and 316) in two commentary editions of Shukla's Bhramargeet-saar: Anita Prakashan, Delhi 1977, and N. Shastri, Agra 1957. Their wording follows the 1977 edition.

- **Kavita Kosh** is named in the input's `verified_with` for Kabir/Sur, but its robots.txt blocks ClaudeBot and anthropic-ai. It was not used as a source. One page was fetched before the block was noticed, and nothing from it was used.

## Cautions for plan editors
- **bh.niti.87 in someone-left (day 3):** the image of a cut tree growing back and the moon waxing again is about changing fortunes, not people. Wortham notes that the same image is used elsewhere to say the dead do not return. Do not imply that the person will come back.
- **sur.bgs.10 in far-from-parents (day 1):** Krishna promises to be back 'in four or five days', and in the story he never returns. A reader whose parent has died may feel this sharply.
- **kabir.kk.66 in far-from-parents (day 4):** the original is about guru/God and disciple, so using it for family stretches it. 'Those we love are never far' can sound like a platitude after a death or an estrangement.
- **sur.bgs.316 in someone-left (day1_alt):** pure lament with no consolation. That is right for a 'see' day, but don't pair it with a reading that promises the pain will end. The full pada has bodily imagery (tears soaking the bodice) and ends with Gokul 'drowning'; our excerpt avoids both.
- **viv.4.hold_on_brave_heart in work-feels-unsafe:** 'hold on' must not read as 'put up with harassment or danger'.
- **viv.1.misery_attachment in running-on-empty:** 'it's your attachment' can sound like blame to someone who is burned out. Acknowledge the workload first.
- **viv.5.bob_up_soon:** mentions an accidental death, and Vivekananda died the next year. Do not frame 'bob up' as a guarantee for a reader who is ill.
- **bh.vairagya.82 in gratitude:** the 'fruit of past austerity' framing can suggest that hardship is deserved.
- **bh.niti.53 in someone-hurting-you:** if the reader is being abused, add a pointer to real help.
